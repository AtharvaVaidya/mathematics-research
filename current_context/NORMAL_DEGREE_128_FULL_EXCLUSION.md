# Exclusion of the full normal-degree \((12,8)\) frontier

Date: 26 July 2026

## Outcome

Let
\[
 [F,G]_{x,y}=\lambda\in\mathbf C^\times,\qquad
 \deg_yF=12,\qquad \deg_yG=8,
\tag{1}
\]
and suppose this is the first unresolved arithmetic normal-degree
chart after the standard target reductions and high source shear.  Its
leading coefficients have the form
\[
 F_{12}=h^3,\qquad G_8=h^2,\qquad
 \gcd(4,\deg h)=4.
\tag{2}
\]
Then \(F,G\) cannot be a noninvertible Keller pair.

The proof has three inputs:

1. the local invariant-boundary theorem for degree \((12,8)\),
   \[
   \operatorname {pole}(A_7)\geq3\rho;
   \tag{3}
   \]
2. the exact classification of inverse-character differentials on
   \(H^4=h\);
3. injectivity on one source line.

The fourth-power chart, including every extra approximate-root
constant, is closed by the Laurent-time reduction and (3).  If the
fourth root is not rational, the terminal equation and the Kummer
classification force a finite pole of \(A_7\).  The same local
inequality then makes the restriction of an original coordinate to a
vertical line affine.  The remaining constant-fourth-root chart has
total degrees at most \(12\) and \(8\), so the independently proved
counterexample lower bound \(125\) excludes it.

This proves a normal-degree theorem, not the plane Jacobian
conjecture in arbitrary degree.

## 1. Normalization and the local pole theorem

On a fourth-root cover
\[
 H^4=h,\qquad z=Hy,
\tag{4}
\]
depress the lower coordinate and write
\[
 g=w^8+\sum_{j=2}^8c_jw^{8-j},\qquad
 s=g^{1/8}=w+O(w^{-1}).
\tag{5}
\]
At fixed \(s\), expand
\[
 f=\Phi(s)+\sum_{\ell\geq1}A_\ell s^{-\ell}.
\tag{6}
\]
The bracket identity gives
\[
 A_1'=\cdots=A_6'=0,\qquad
 8A_7'=\frac{\lambda}{H}.
\tag{7}
\]

At a place of the coefficient field put
\[
 \rho=\max_{2\leq j\leq8}
 \frac{\operatorname {pole}(c_j)}j.
\tag{8}
\]
The exact Davenport--Stothers boundary, collision-normal, and
extra-upper-constant calculations in
`NORMAL_DEGREE_128_COUNTEREXAMPLE_CUSP_AND_LIFT_OBSTRUCTION.md`
prove the local valuative implication
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname {pole}(A_7)\geq3\rho.}
\tag{9}
\]
Although it was discovered using rational one-pole curves, the proof
of (9) is local.  After a finite ramified extension it uses only the
leading Laurent coefficients at the chosen place.  It therefore
applies equally to a place on a connected or disconnected Kummer
normalization.

## 2. The fourth-power chart

Suppose first that \(h=r^4\) with \(r\in\mathbf C[x]\).  Then the
normalized bracket is
\[
 [f,g]_{x,w}=\frac{\lambda}{r},
\tag{10}
\]
and the terminal time
\[
 T=8A_7
\tag{11}
\]
satisfies \(T'=\lambda/r\).

If \(r\) is nonconstant, the rational-time classification gives
\[
 T=\alpha+\beta(x-a)^{-k},\qquad
 r=\gamma(x-a)^{k+1}.
\tag{12}
\]
Cyclic descent to the field generated by the normalized
coefficients gives a parameter \(v=u^m\), \(u=x-a\), with
\[
 [f,g]_{v,w}
 =\frac{\lambda}{m\gamma v^{n+1}},
 \qquad k=mn.
\tag{13}
\]
The local theorem (9) is exactly \(n\geq3\rho\) in this quotient
parameter.  The Taylor estimate of the general Laurent-line lemma
then makes \(G(a,y)\) affine.  If it is nonconstant, \(G\) is
injective on \(x=a\).  If it is constant, restricting (1) to that
line makes \(F(a,y)\) affine and nonconstant.  Gwoździewicz's
one-line theorem therefore makes the map an automorphism.

It remains to treat constant \(r\).  Now \(f,g\in\mathbf C[x,w]\)
form a Keller pair and \(T\) is affine.  At \(x=\infty\),
\[
 \operatorname {pole}_\infty(A_7)=1.
\tag{14}
\]
The coefficient curve is nonconstant, since otherwise \(A_7\) would
be constant.  Thus \(\rho>0\), and (9) gives
\[
 \rho\leq\frac13.
\tag{15}
\]

Every coefficient of \(w^{8-j}\) in \(g\) has \(x\)-degree at most
\(j/3\).  Every coefficient of \(w^{12-k}\) in \(f\) is weighted
homogeneous of deficit \(k\) in the \(c_j\), with the upper
approximate-root constants assigned their usual constant values.
It therefore has \(x\)-degree at most \(k/3\).  Consequently
\[
 \deg_{\rm tot}g\leq8,\qquad
 \deg_{\rm tot}f\leq12.
\tag{16}
\]
The established theorem that a noninvertible plane Keller map must
have maximum total degree at least \(125\) now makes this normalized
pair an automorphism.  Undoing the polynomial depression and the
constant target changes preserves invertibility.

## 3. The non-fourth-power Kummer chart

Assume now that \(H\notin\mathbf C(x)\).  The deck action sends
\[
 H\longmapsto\zeta H,\qquad w\longmapsto\zeta w
\tag{17}
\]
on a faithful component, or restricts this action to the nontrivial
subgroup when \(h\) is a square.  Since \(7\equiv-1\pmod4\), the
terminal coefficient is in the inverse character.  Hence
\[
 A_7=\frac{S}{H},\qquad S\in\mathbf C(x).
\tag{18}
\]
Equation (7) becomes
\[
 4S'-\frac{h'}hS=\frac{\lambda}{2}.
\tag{19}
\]

The exact Kummer differential classification says that \(S\) is a
nonconstant squarefree polynomial.  If
\[
 S=L\prod_i(x-a_i),
\tag{20}
\]
then
\[
 h=C\prod_i(x-a_i)^{m_i},\qquad
 m_i=4-\frac{\lambda}{2S'(a_i)}
 \in\mathbf Z_{\geq0},\qquad m_i\ne4.
\tag{21}
\]
If \(\deg S\geq2\), the integers \(4-m_i\) sum to zero.  Some
\(m_i\) is therefore strictly larger than four, and \(A_7\) has a
finite pole over \(x=a_i\).

The one-root alternative is
\[
 h=C(x-a)^M,\qquad M\ne4.
\tag{22}
\]
But (2) says \(4\mid\deg h=M\).  If \(M=0\), this is the constant
fourth-power chart already treated.  If \(M>0\), then (22) is itself
a fourth power; \(M=4\) is forbidden by (21).  Thus every genuinely
non-fourth-power chart has a finite terminal pole.

Choose a root of multiplicity \(M>4\), put
\[
 e=\frac4{\gcd(4,M)},\qquad
 h_0=\frac{M}{\gcd(4,M)},\qquad
 n=h_0-e,
\tag{23}
\]
and take a local parameter \(t\) with
\[
 x-a=t^e\cdot(\text{unit}),\qquad
 H=t^{h_0}\cdot(\text{unit}).
\tag{24}
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname {pole}_t(A_7)=n,\qquad
 [f,g]_{t,w}
 =(\text{unit})\,t^{-n-1}.
\tag{25}
\]
By (9),
\[
 n\geq3\rho.
\tag{26}
\]

Let \(\tau(t)\) be the depressing center.  Polynomiality of
\(G(x(t),0)\) gives
\[
 \operatorname {pole}_t(\tau)\leq\rho.
\tag{27}
\]
Writing \(G=\sum_iG_i(x)y^i\), Taylor expansion after
\(w=Hy+\tau\) gives
\[
 \operatorname {ord}_tG_i(x(t))
 \geq i h_0-(8-i)\rho.
\tag{28}
\]
For \(i\geq2\), (23) and (26) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 i h_0-(8-i)\rho
 &=i(n+e)-(8-i)\rho\\
 &\geq(4i-8)\rho+ie>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Thus
\[
 G_i(a)=0\qquad(i\geq2),
\tag{30}
\]
so \(G(a,y)\) is affine.  As in Section 2, either it is nonconstant,
or the Keller identity makes \(F(a,y)\) affine and nonconstant.
The map is injective on \(x=a\), and the one-line theorem makes it an
automorphism.

This eliminates the non-fourth-power Kummer chart and completes the
normal-degree \((12,8)\) exclusion.

## 4. Scope

The proof closes all leading-factor possibilities at this exact
normal degree:

* nonconstant fourth powers;
* constant fourth powers;
* faithful connected fourth-root covers;
* the intermediate square-but-not-fourth-power covers.

It does not prove the local pole inequality (9) for arbitrary lower
normal degree, and it does not eliminate the later arithmetic
frontiers such as \((15,10)\).  Those are the remaining obstacles to
turning this degree-frontier method into a proof of \(JC(2)\).
