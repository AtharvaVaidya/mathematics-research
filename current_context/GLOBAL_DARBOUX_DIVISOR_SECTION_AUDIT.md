# Global divisor descent and an exact translation stress test

Date: 25 July 2026

This note sharpens the remaining global section problem for the reciprocal
Darboux powers
\[
S=\Xi^5,\qquad Y=T^N,\qquad N=5m+2.
\]
It has two conclusions.

First, divisibility of the divisors of \(S\) and \(Y\) carries no descent
information: it is automatic, both upstairs and after taking norms.
The useful global condition is instead **invariance of the complete
principal divisor under every conjugate**.  For the genuine endpoint
pair, invariance of the divisor of either \(S\) or \(Y\) forces the whole
comparison branch to descend.

Second, the genuine endpoint, the exact symplectic equation, polynomial
support containment, and the normalized identity branch at infinity do
not by themselves force this invariance.  A constant target translation
gives an exact global countermodel to that weaker package.  It fails the
forced inner-vertex conditions of an actual Keller reduction.  Thus a
successful proof must use those inner vertices, or equivalent global
component-incidence information, rather than only the outer Newton edge.

Work over an algebraically closed field \(\Bbbk\) of characteristic zero.
Put
\[
p=2m+1,\qquad q=3m+1,\qquad N=5m+2,
\tag{1}
\]
and let
\[
P_0=\xi^2A(t),\qquad Q_0=\xi^3B(t)
\tag{2}
\]
be a genuine reciprocal endpoint pair:
\[
\deg A=p,\quad \deg B=q,\quad
A(0)B(0)[t^p]A[t^q]B\ne0,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
3A'B-2AB'=t^{N-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
As in `RECIPROCAL_DARBOUX_LIOUVILLE_AUDIT.md`,
\[
3Q_0\,dP_0-2P_0\,dQ_0=\frac SN\,dY,
\qquad
dP_0\wedge dQ_0=-\frac1{5N}dS\wedge dY.
\tag{5}
\]

## 1. Divisibility is automatic

Let \(M/K\) be the finite separable function-field extension generated by
a comparison branch, and let \(\widetilde M/K\) be a normal closure.
On every normal projective model of \(\widetilde M\),
\[
\operatorname{div}(Y)=N\operatorname{div}(T),\qquad
\operatorname{div}(S)=5\operatorname{div}(\Xi).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(N\)- or \(5\)-divisibility upstairs is a tautology.

Taking norms does not improve it:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{div}_K\!\left(\operatorname{N}_{M/K}Y\right)
 &=N\operatorname{div}_K\!\left(\operatorname{N}_{M/K}T\right),\\
\operatorname{div}_K\!\left(\operatorname{N}_{M/K}S\right)
 &=5\operatorname{div}_K\!\left(\operatorname{N}_{M/K}\Xi\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently any argument that only counts boundary multiplicities
modulo \(N\) or \(5\) cannot prove field descent.

## 2. The divisor-invariance section theorem

Let \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(\widetilde M/K)\).  Principal divisors below
are taken on a normal projective \(G\)-model of \(\widetilde M\).

> **Divisor-invariance section theorem.**  Put
> \[
> J=SY=\Xi^5T^N.
> \]
> Under (1)--(4), any one of the conditions
> \[
> \sigma\operatorname{div}(Y)=\operatorname{div}(Y)
> \quad(\sigma\in G),
> \tag{8}
> \]
> or
> \[
> \sigma\operatorname{div}(S)=\operatorname{div}(S)
> \quad(\sigma\in G)
> \tag{9}
> \]
> or
> \[
> \sigma\operatorname{div}(J)=\operatorname{div}(J)
> \quad(\sigma\in G)
> \tag{9a}
> \]
> implies
> \[
> S,Y,\Xi,T\in K.
> \tag{10}
> \]

This converts the remaining section problem into a precise boundary
incidence statement: it is enough to show that all conjugates of one
canonical Darboux power have the same zero and pole divisor.

### Proof from the divisor of \(Y\)

Condition (8) gives
\[
\sigma(Y)=c_\sigma Y,\qquad c_\sigma\in\Bbbk^\times.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(Y=T^N\), the rational function \(\sigma(T)/T\) has zero divisor,
and hence
\[
\sigma(T)=\lambda T,\qquad \lambda^N=c_\sigma
\tag{12}
\]
for some \(\lambda\in\Bbbk^\times\).

Put \(\alpha=3Q\,dP-2P\,dQ\in\Omega^1_{K/\Bbbk}\).  From
\(\alpha=(S/N)dY\), applying \(\sigma\) gives
\[
\sigma(S)=c_\sigma^{-1}S.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore
\[
\sigma(\Xi)=\mu\Xi,\qquad \mu^5=c_\sigma^{-1}.
\tag{14}
\]
The fixed equation \(P=\Xi^2A(T)\) now yields
\[
\mu^2A(\lambda T)=A(T).
\tag{15}
\]
The nonzero constant and leading coefficients of \(A\) give
\[
\mu^2=1,\qquad \lambda^p=1.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus \(\mu=\pm1\) and \(c_\sigma=\mu\).  If \(\mu=-1\), then
\(\lambda^N=-1\).  But
\[
2N-5p=-1,
\tag{17}
\]
so \(\lambda^p=1\) and \(\lambda^{2N}=1\) imply \(\lambda=1\), a
contradiction.  Hence \(\mu=c_\sigma=\lambda=1\).  Every element of
\(G\) fixes \(S,Y,\Xi,T\), proving (10).

### Proof from the divisor of \(S\)

Condition (9) gives
\[
\sigma(S)=c_\sigma S,\qquad
\sigma(\Xi)=\mu\Xi,\qquad \mu^5=c_\sigma.
\tag{18}
\]
The Liouville identity gives
\[
d(\sigma(Y))=c_\sigma^{-1}dY,
\]
and hence
\[
\sigma(Y)=c_\sigma^{-1}Y+a,\qquad a\in\Bbbk.
\tag{19}
\]
Writing \(T'=\sigma(T)\), the fixed endpoint equations imply
\[
A(T')=\mu^{-2}A(T),\qquad
B(T')=\mu^{-3}B(T).
\tag{20}
\]
The birational endpoint lemma
\(\Bbbk(A(T),B(T))=\Bbbk(T)\) shows that \(T'\in\Bbbk(T)\);
applying the same statement to \(\sigma^{-1}\) shows
\(\Bbbk(T')=\Bbbk(T)\).  Thus \(T'\) is a Möbius transformation of
\(T\).  Since the nonconstant polynomial \(A(T')\) is a polynomial in
\(T\), this Möbius transformation has no finite pole, so
\[
T'=\lambda T+\beta.
\tag{21}
\]
Equation (19) becomes
\[
(\lambda T+\beta)^N=c_\sigma^{-1}T^N+a.
\tag{22}
\]
Because \(N\ge2\), comparison of the \(T^{N-1}\) coefficient gives
\(\beta=0\), and then
\[
a=0,\qquad \lambda^N=c_\sigma^{-1}.
\tag{23}
\]
The constant and leading coefficients in (20) again give
\(\mu^2=1\) and \(\lambda^p=1\).  Since
\(c_\sigma=\mu^5=\mu\), the same arithmetic (17) excludes
\(\mu=-1\).  Hence \(\mu=c_\sigma=\lambda=1\), and (10) follows.

### Proof from the divisor of \(J\)

Condition (9a) gives
\[
\sigma(J)=c_\sigma J,\qquad c_\sigma\in\Bbbk^\times.
\tag{23a}
\]
Because \(\sigma\) has finite order, \(c_\sigma\) is a root of unity.
Using \(J=SY\), the Liouville identity in (5) can be written
\[
\alpha:=3Q\,dP-2P\,dQ=\frac JN\,d\log Y.
\tag{23b}
\]
Applying \(\sigma\) gives
\[
d\log\sigma(Y)=c_\sigma^{-1}d\log Y.
\tag{23c}
\]
Choose a prime divisor \(E\) with
\(\operatorname{ord}_E(Y)\ne0\).  Taking logarithmic residues in (23c)
shows
\[
\operatorname{ord}_E(\sigma Y)
=c_\sigma^{-1}\operatorname{ord}_E(Y).
\tag{23d}
\]
Both orders are nonzero integers.  Hence \(c_\sigma\in\mathbf Q\), and
the only rational roots of unity are
\[
c_\sigma=\pm1.
\tag{23e}
\]

If \(c_\sigma=1\), then (23c) gives
\(\sigma(Y)=aY\) for a constant \(a\).  Hence
\(\sigma(T)=\eta T\), and \(\sigma(J)=J\) then gives
\(\sigma(\Xi)=\varepsilon\Xi\).  Comparing the nonzero constant
coefficients in both fixed endpoint equations gives
\(\varepsilon^2=\varepsilon^3=1\), while the top coefficients give
\(\eta^p=\eta^q=1\).  Since \(3p-2q=1\), one has
\(\varepsilon=\eta=1\).  Thus \(\sigma\) fixes the whole endpoint
branch; this is the elementwise proof of the product form of the
Darboux-power descent theorem.

Suppose instead that \(c_\sigma=-1\).  Equation (23c) gives
\[
\sigma(Y)=\frac aY,\qquad a\in\Bbbk^\times.
\]
Writing \(T'=\sigma(T)\), and absorbing an \(N\)-th root of unity into
\(\lambda\), this is
\[
T'=\frac{\lambda}{T},\qquad \lambda^N=a.
\tag{23f}
\]
The relation \(\sigma(J)=-J\) then gives
\[
\sigma(\Xi)^5=-\lambda^{-N}\Xi^5T^{2N}.
\tag{23g}
\]
Apply \(\sigma\) to \(P=\Xi^2A(T)\), and raise the resulting equality
to the fifth power.  After using (23g), one obtains
\[
\lambda^{-2N}T^{4N}A(\lambda/T)^5=A(T)^5.
\tag{23h}
\]
The least exponent of \(T\) on the left is
\[
4N-5p=10m+3>0,
\tag{23i}
\]
whereas the right side has nonzero constant coefficient \(A(0)^5\).
This contradiction excludes \(c_\sigma=-1\).  Thus every element of
\(G\) fixes \(J\), and the product descent theorem proves (10).

The hypothesis here is intrinsic only when interpreted
valuation-theoretically: equality of the principal divisors means
equality at every divisorial valuation of \(\widetilde M\).  It may be
checked on any common normal projective \(G\)-model, and is unchanged
after passing to a higher such model.  By contrast, equality of the
visible boundary coefficients on one non-Galois compactification is not
enough; conjugation can expose additional divisors after normalizing the
closure and resolving its action.

## 3. An exact global translation countermodel

Fix \(c\in\Bbbk^\times\), and on the rational surface
\(\Bbbk(\xi,t)\) define
\[
P=P_0=\xi^2A(t),\qquad
Q=Q_0+c=\xi^3B(t)+c.
\tag{24}
\]
This preserves the exact symplectic equation:
\[
dP\wedge dQ=dP_0\wedge dQ_0
=-\xi^4t^{N-1}d\xi\wedge dt.
\tag{25}
\]

The normalized comparison branch is obtained from
\[
\widetilde\xi^2A(T)=\xi^2A(t),\qquad
\widetilde\xi^3B(T)=\xi^3B(t)+c.
\tag{26}
\]
Set \(\epsilon=\xi^{-1}\) and \(r=\widetilde\xi/\xi\).  Then
\[
r^2A(T)=A(t),\qquad
r^3B(T)=B(t)+c\epsilon^3.
\tag{27}
\]
At \(\epsilon=0\), the solution \((r,T)=(1,t)\) has implicit Jacobian
\[
\det
\begin{pmatrix}
2A&A'\\
3B&B'
\end{pmatrix}
=2AB'-3A'B=-t^{N-1}\ne0
\tag{28}
\]
in \(\Bbbk(t)\).  Therefore (27) has a unique formal solution
\[
r=1+O(\epsilon^3),\qquad T=t+O(\epsilon^3).
\tag{29}
\]
This is exactly a normalized identity sheet at the outer infinity end.

Nevertheless this sheet is not rational over \(\Bbbk(\xi,t)\).
Suppose it were.  It would define a dominant rational self-map \(H\)
with
\[
P_0\circ H=P_0,\qquad Q_0\circ H=Q_0+c.
\tag{30}
\]
The outer extension
\[
\Bbbk(P_0,Q_0)\subset\Bbbk(\xi,t)
\tag{31}
\]
is finite: the quotient
\[
R(t)=\frac{Q_0^2}{P_0^3}=\frac{B(t)^2}{A(t)^3}
\tag{32}
\]
recovers \(t\) up to the finite map \(R\), and then
\(\xi=Q_0A(t)/(P_0B(t))\).  Equation (30) makes the image field
\(H^*\Bbbk(\xi,t)\) have the same degree over
\(\Bbbk(P_0,Q_0)\) as \(\Bbbk(\xi,t)\), so \(H^*\) is surjective.
Thus \(H\) would be a birational automorphism lifting the target
translation
\[
\tau(p,q)=(p,q+c).
\tag{33}
\]

Such a lift cannot exist.  Put
\[
L=\frac{B(0)^2}{A(0)^3}.
\tag{34}
\]
From (4),
\[
R'(t)=-\frac{t^{N-1}B(t)}{A(t)^4},
\tag{35}
\]
so \(t=0\) has ramification index \(N\) and maps to the cusp branch
divisor
\[
C_0:\quad q^2=Lp^3.
\tag{36}
\]
A lift of \(\tau\) must preserve the finite branch-divisor set of the
finite function-field extension (31).  Its iterates would therefore
force this finite set to contain the whole orbit
\[
C_j:\quad (q-jc)^2=Lp^3,\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{37}
\]
These curves are pairwise distinct in characteristic zero, a
contradiction.  Hence the normalized sheet (29) is not rational.
By the Darboux-power descent theorem, none of \(S,Y,J\) descends.

## 4. Polynomial support and the exact missing input

Return to
\[
z=xy,\qquad w=xy^2,\qquad t=w^{-1},\qquad \xi=zw^m.
\tag{38}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
P_0=\frac{z^2U(w)}w,\qquad
Q_0=\frac{z^3V(w)}w.
\tag{39}
\]
Every monomial of (39) is polynomial in \(x,y\):
\[
z^2w^{j-1}=x^{j+1}y^{2j},\qquad
z^3w^{j-1}=x^{j+2}y^{2j+1}.
\tag{40}
\]
Consequently (24) is a polynomial pair in the original plane and
\[
dP\wedge dQ=x^2\,dx\wedge dy.
\tag{41}
\]
Its support is contained in the bounded five-block polygon, and the
constant \(c\) is an allowed lower-block monomial.

This is not a counterexample to the all-scale five-block theorem and not
a Keller counterexample.  It omits the forced inner vertices of a
genuine reduced Keller completion (in particular the prescribed
degree-\(8\) and degree-\(12\) lower blocks).  Those hypotheses are
exactly where the all-scale obstruction acts.

The countermodel proves the following sharp negative statement:

> Genuine endpoint data, the reciprocal Wronskian, exact symplecticity,
> a normalized global infinity sheet, and containment in the bounded
> Newton polygon do not force Darboux-power descent.

What remains plausible is stronger and more geometric:

> **Boundary-orbit target.**  Use the forced inner Newton vertices (or
> the corresponding original Keller boundary divisors) to prove that
> \(\operatorname{div}(T^N)\), equivalently
> \(\operatorname{div}(\Xi^5)\), or directly
> \(\operatorname{div}(Z^5/W^2)\), is \(G\)-invariant rather than
> acquiring the translated-cusp orbit in (37).

By the divisor-invariance section theorem, that boundary statement would
complete global field descent.  Mere multiplicity divisibility cannot.

are checked by `verify_global_darboux_divisor_section.py`.
