# The first nonlinear cusp cancellation in the degree-six weighted lift

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

For the degree-five Gallagher seed, let
\[
F=(A,B,C):\mathbb A^3\longrightarrow\mathbb A^3
\]
be the generic-degree-six Keller map audited in
`WEIGHTED_LIFT_DEGREE_SIX_GRAPH_PROJECTION_NO_GO.md`.  On an arbitrary
nonconstant polynomial graph \(z=g(x,y)\), the highest forms are
\[
A_0=q_6y^6C_0^4,\qquad
B_0=p_5y^5C_0^4,\qquad
C_0=x^3g_m,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(g_m\) is the highest homogeneous part of \(g\),
\[
p_5=-\frac3{46},\qquad q_6=-\frac5{92}.
\tag{2}
\]

There is a small but important correction to the naive cusp
suggestion.  The forms in (1) do **not** satisfy a relation between
\(A_0^5\) and \(B_0^6\): those two forms have different ordinary
degrees.  Their primitive toric relation is
\[
p_5^6A_0^5C_0^4-q_6^5B_0^6=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Define the corresponding target polynomial
\[
E=p_5^6A^5C^4-q_6^5B^6.
\tag{4}
\]
The cancellation in (3) is exact at the first level, but the displayed
binomial itself has a nonzero next form.  On every nonconstant graph,
\[
E_0=\kappa y^{29}C_0^{23},
\qquad
\kappa=7p_5^6q_6^5\ne0.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, the first nonlinear subduction produces exponent
vector \((29,23)\), and
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}29&23\\5&4\end{pmatrix}=1.
\tag{6}
\]
This unimodular jump makes the first cusp cancellation more rigid,
not less.

The exact leading Jacobians are
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(E_0,C_0)
 &=-29\kappa y^{28}C_0^{23}\partial_xC_0,\\
J(E_0,B_0)
 &=-\kappa p_5y^{33}C_0^{26}\partial_xC_0,\\
J(E_0,A_0)
 &=22\kappa q_6y^{34}C_0^{26}\partial_xC_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
They are nonzero and have three distinct degrees.  Consequently:

1. neither \((E,A)\), \((E,B)\), nor \((E,C)\) restricts to a plane
   Keller map on any nonconstant polynomial graph;
2. more generally, \(E\) paired with any nonzero linear target form
   has nonconstant restricted Jacobian;
3. adding a linear target form to \(E\) does not change this
   conclusion when the second target coordinate is linear.

The raw binomial \(A^5-\lambda B^6\) also fails when paired with
\(B\) or \(C\), for every \(\lambda\), but for the simpler reason that
it does not make the actual leading cancellation.

There are four further polynomial subtractions in the same denominator
slice.  Carrying all of them out produces a maximal first-stage
subduction \(T\) with
\[
T_0=\lambda_0y^{25}C_0^{19},\qquad \lambda_0\ne0.
\tag{8}
\]
This form is outside the semigroup generated by the three forms in
(1), and it too has nonconstant Jacobian with every nonzero linear
target form.

The next toric relation is \(T_0C_0\sim B_0^5\).  Subducting it exposes
the first term that remembers more than \(y\) and \(C_0\):
\[
U_0=\theta x^{49}y^{20}g_m^{17},\qquad\theta\ne0.
\tag{9}
\]
The pair \((U,C)\) is impossible on every graph.  For the pivots \(B\)
and \(A\), the only graphs on which even the highest Jacobian can
vanish have respectively
\[
\begin{aligned}
g_m&=c\,x^{8k+7}y^{5k+5},&m&=13k+12,\\
g_m&=c\,x^{4k-1}y^{11k+4},&m&=15k+3,\quad k\ge1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the nonlinear search has been reduced to two sparse infinite
Newton rays rather than a blind coefficient problem.

This closes the first nonlinear target cancellation suggested by the
toric highest forms and identifies the exact second-stage resonance.
It is not an all-nonlinear-projection theorem: lower terms on the two
rays in (10), or two target polynomials whose successive initial forms
remain algebraically dependent, are still outside the argument.

## 1. Exact seed formulas

Write
\[
u=1+xy,\qquad
\gamma=1+a\,xy+x^2z,\qquad
w=u\gamma .
\tag{11}
\]
For the collision-atlas seed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(w)&=p_5w^5+p_4w^4+\cdots,\\
q(w)&=q_6w^6+q_5w^5+\cdots,
\end{aligned}
\qquad
\begin{aligned}
p_5&=-\frac3{46},&p_4&=\frac{35}{92},\\
q_6&=-\frac5{92},&q_5&=\frac7{23}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The definitions of the weighted lift can be rewritten exactly as
\[
A=\frac{q(w)+w\gamma}{x^2\gamma^2},
\qquad
B=\frac{p(w)+\gamma}{x\gamma},
\qquad
C=x\gamma .
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore (4) has the exact rational presentation
\[
E=
\frac{
 p_5^6\bigl(q(w)+w\gamma\bigr)^5
 -q_6^5\bigl(p(w)+\gamma\bigr)^6
}{x^6\gamma^6}.
\tag{14}
\]
The expression is polynomial because it is (4); presentation (14)
is used only to expose its filtration.

The coefficient of \(w^{30}\) in the numerator of (14) is zero.
The coefficient of \(w^{29}\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&5p_5^6q_6^4q_5-6q_6^5p_5^5p_4\\
&\quad
=p_5^6q_6^5
\left(5\frac{q_5}{q_6}-6\frac{p_4}{p_5}\right)
=7p_5^6q_6^5
=\kappa\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Terms involving the added \(w\gamma\) or \(\gamma\), and all remaining
seed terms, have strictly smaller graph-substituted ordinary degree.

## 2. Why the conclusion is uniform in the graph

Let
\[
g=g_m+\text{lower terms},\qquad m\ge1.
\tag{16}
\]
Then
\[
u_0=xy,\qquad
\gamma_0=x^2g_m,\qquad
w_0=x^3yg_m,\qquad
C_0=x^3g_m.
\tag{17}
\]
After division by the denominator in (14), the term (15) gives
\[
\kappa\frac{w_0^{29}}{x^6\gamma_0^6}
=\kappa\frac{u_0^{29}\gamma_0^{23}}{x^6}
=\kappa y^{29}C_0^{23},
\tag{18}
\]
which proves (5).

This deduction does not assume that \(g_m\) is a monomial or squarefree.
Moreover,
\[
\partial_x C_0\ne0.
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, \(C_0=x^3g_m\) is a nonzero polynomial divisible by \(x^3\);
in characteristic zero it cannot be independent of \(x\).

For monomials \(M=y^\alpha C_0^\beta\) and
\(N=y^\rho C_0^\sigma\), direct differentiation gives
\[
J(M,N)
=(\beta\rho-\alpha\sigma)
y^{\alpha+\rho-1}C_0^{\beta+\sigma-1}
\partial_xC_0.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (7) follow from (20) and the exponent vectors
\[
E_0:(29,23),\quad
A_0:(6,4),\quad
B_0:(5,4),\quad
C_0:(0,1).
\tag{21}
\]

Their degrees are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\deg J(E,C)&=24m+99,\\
\deg J(E,B)&=27m+113,\\
\deg J(E,A)&=27m+114.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
The degrees are distinct for every \(m\ge1\).  Thus if
\(L=\ell_AA+\ell_BB+\ell_CC\) is nonzero, the highest nonzero term
of \(J(E,L)\) is selected successively by
\(\ell_A,\ell_B,\ell_C\), and can never be a constant.

If \(L_1,L_2\) are linear target forms and \(L_2\ne0\), then
\[
J(E+L_1,L_2)=J(E,L_2)+J(L_1,L_2).
\tag{23}
\]
The degrees in (22) are strictly larger than the degrees of all
linear-projection Jacobians from the preceding audit, so the second
summand cannot cancel the first.

## 3. The raw \(A^5-\lambda B^6\) candidate

Since
\[
\deg A_0=4m+18,\qquad
\deg B_0=4m+17,
\tag{24}
\]
one has
\[
\deg A_0^5=20m+90,\qquad
\deg B_0^6=24m+102.
\tag{25}
\]
Thus \(A^5-\lambda B^6\) has leading term
\(-\lambda B_0^6\) when \(\lambda\ne0\), not a cancelled leading
form.

Pairing it with \(B\) gives the exact chain-rule identity
\[
J(A^5-\lambda B^6,B)=5A^4J(A,B),
\tag{26}
\]
whose highest form is nonzero.  Pairing it with \(C\) gives
\[
J(A^5-\lambda B^6,C)
=5A^4J(A,C)-6\lambda B^5J(B,C).
\tag{27}
\]
For \(\lambda\ne0\), the second summand has degree \(25m+103\),
strictly above the first summand's degree \(21m+91\); for
\(\lambda=0\), the first summand is itself nonzero.  Hence neither
pair can be plane Keller.

## 4. Maximal subduction in the first denominator slice

Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_1&=-\frac{8505}{18948593792},&
c_2&=-\frac{79515}{151588750336},\\
c_3&=\frac{1587485}{303177500672},&
c_4&=-\frac{1079635}{303177500672},
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
T={}&E+c_1A^4BC^3+c_2A^3B^2C^2\\
   &\quad+c_3A^2B^3C+c_4AB^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
Every added monomial has the same denominator \(x^6\gamma^6\) in
the presentation (13).  Successively, they cancel the pure terms
\(w^{29},w^{28},w^{27},w^{26}\).  Exact expansion leaves
\[
\lambda_0=
\frac{258473511}{31221670147323559936}
\tag{30}
\]
as the coefficient of \(w^{25}\), and every other numerator monomial
has smaller graph weight for every \(m\ge1\).  Consequently
\[
T_0=\lambda_0\frac{w_0^{25}}{x^6\gamma_0^6}
=\lambda_0y^{25}C_0^{19}.
\tag{31}
\]

There is no nonnegative solution of
\[
6r+5s=25,\qquad 4r+4s+t=19.
\tag{32}
\]
Indeed, the first equation forces \((r,s)=(0,5)\), after which the
second would require \(t=-1\).  Thus (31) is not in the initial
semigroup generated by \(A_0,B_0,C_0\): \(T\) is a genuine new SAGBI
generator, not another disguised old monomial.

The determinants of \((25,19)\) with the exponent vectors of
\(C_0,B_0,A_0\) are \(-25,-5,14\).  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(T_0,C_0)
 &=-25\lambda_0y^{24}C_0^{19}\partial_xC_0,\\
J(T_0,B_0)
 &=-5\lambda_0p_5y^{29}C_0^{22}\partial_xC_0,\\
J(T_0,A_0)
 &=14\lambda_0q_6y^{30}C_0^{22}\partial_xC_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
Their degrees are respectively
\[
20m+83,\qquad23m+97,\qquad23m+98.
\tag{34}
\]
As before, their distinct degrees make \(T\) incompatible with every
nonzero linear pivot.

## 5. The second subduction and its exact resonant rays

The new exponent satisfies
\[
(25,19)+(0,1)=5(5,4).
\tag{35}
\]
Accordingly start with
\[
p_5^5TC-\lambda_0B^5.
\tag{36}
\]
Its common denominator is \(x^5\gamma^5\).  There are four pure
highest terms which can still be removed by a unique linear
combination
\[
d_0A^4C^3+d_1A^3BC^2+d_2A^2B^2C+d_3AB^3.
\tag{37}
\]
For reference, the exact coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_0&=-\frac{10204102899}{11489574614215070056448},\\
d_1&=\frac{5559334317}{249773361178588479488},\\
d_2&=-\frac{1028649031155}{11489574614215070056448},\\
d_3&=\frac{16337519127}{359049206694220939264}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{38}
\]
Call the resulting target polynomial \(U\).  The first surviving
numerator term is no longer pure in \(w\):
\[
\theta w^{20}\gamma^2,\qquad
\theta=-\frac{36905625}{6077984970919772059860992}.
\tag{39}
\]
It is the unique highest graph-weight term for every \(m\ge1\).
After dividing by \(x^5\gamma^5\), this gives precisely
\[
U_0=\theta x^{49}y^{20}g_m^{17}.
\tag{40}
\]

Direct differentiation with \(C_0=x^3g_m\) gives
\[
J(U_0,C_0)=
-2\theta x^{51}y^{19}g_m^{17}
\left(30g_m+10x\partial_xg_m+y\partial_yg_m\right).
\tag{41}
\]
The operator in parentheses acts on
\(x^iy^{m-i}\) by the positive integer \(30+m+9i\); it has no kernel
on a nonzero homogeneous polynomial.  Thus \((U,C)\) is impossible
on every polynomial graph.

For the other two original pivots,
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(U_0,B_0)
&=\theta p_5x^{60}y^{24}g_m^{20}
\left(5g_m+5xg_{m,x}-8yg_{m,y}\right),\\
J(U_0,A_0)
&=\theta q_6x^{60}y^{25}g_m^{20}
\left(54g_m+22xg_{m,x}-8yg_{m,y}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{42}
\]
Each parenthesized operator is diagonal on degree-\(m\) monomials.
The first has a kernel exactly when
\[
13i=8m-5,
\tag{43}
\]
which is equivalent to
\[
m=13k+12,\qquad i=8k+7.
\tag{44}
\]
The second has a kernel exactly when
\[
15i=4m-27,
\tag{45}
\]
which is equivalent, with \(i\ge0\), to
\[
m=15k+3,\qquad i=4k-1,\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{46}
\]
Because a fixed degree has only one monomial with that \(x\)-exponent,
(44) and (46) prove the two necessary monomial forms in (10).

There is a useful explanation for the \(B\)-ray.  Use coordinates
\((x,u)\), where \(u=1+xy\); then
\[
J_{x,y}(R,S)=xJ_{x,u}(R,S).
\tag{47}
\]
The highest seed sectors of \(U\) and \(B\) are proportional to
\[
M=x^{-5}u^{20}\gamma^{17},\qquad
N=x^{-1}u^5\gamma^4.
\tag{48}
\]
Their Jacobian vanishes exactly when
\[
5x\partial_x\gamma-3u\partial_u\gamma-5\gamma=0.
\tag{49}
\]
The monomial solutions are
\[
\gamma=c\,x^{3k+4}u^{5k+5}.
\tag{50}
\]
Accordingly, the formal leading graph suggested by the \(k\)-th
\(B\)-ray is
\[
g=c\,x^{3k+2}(1+xy)^{5k+5}+\text{lower corrections}.
\tag{51}
\]
For \(k=0\), its first two homogeneous pieces are
\[
c x^7y^5+5c x^6y^4,
\tag{52}
\]
which also follows directly from the next homogeneous Jacobian
equation: the degree-eleven part is forced to vanish and the
degree-ten part is forced to be \(5cx^6y^4\).

This gives a compact ansatz for the next audit.  It is not yet a
contradiction because lower seed sectors enter before the recursion
reaches degree zero.  The unavoidable identity
\[
\gamma=1+a(u-1)+x^2g\!\left(x,\frac{u-1}{x}\right)
\tag{53}
\]
shows where an obstruction would have to occur: the terms
\(a u+(1-a)\), with \(a=-57/34\), do not solve (49), but they may
interact with lower \(p,q\) sectors.

## 6. What remains

The first toric stage has the four relevant exponent vectors:
\[
(0,1),\quad(5,4),\quad(6,4),\quad(29,23).
\tag{54}
\]
The last two-step determinant
\[
29\cdot4-23\cdot5=1
\tag{55}
\]
is a promising structural datum for a SAGBI or valuation treatment.
The completed first slice adds \((25,19)\); the second subduction then
leaves the two-variable toric chart and exposes \(x\) explicitly in
(40).  It is not by itself a proof for arbitrary pairs
\(R(A,B,C),S(A,B,C)\).

The honest surviving direction is now more precise: solve the
lower-order graph Darboux equations on the two Newton rays (44) and
(46), beginning with \(k=0\) in the \(B\)-ray and \(k=1\) in the
\(A\)-ray.  Outside those rays, the second-stage leading Jacobian
already rules out the corresponding pivot.  A blind coefficient
search over arbitrary graphs is not indicated.
