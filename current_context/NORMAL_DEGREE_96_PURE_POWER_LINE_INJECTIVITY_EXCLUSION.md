# The pure-power \((9,6)\) cube chart is excluded by one-line injectivity

Date: 26 July 2026

## Outcome

The cyclic-descent note leaves one nonconstant cube-\(h\) family:
\[
 u=x-a,\qquad r=\gamma u^{k+1},\qquad
 6A_5=\alpha+\beta u^{-k},
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(k\geq1\) and \(\beta\gamma\ne0\).  If \(K\) is the field
generated by the five depressed sextic coefficients, cyclic descent
gives
\[
 K=\mathbf C(v),\qquad v=u^m,\qquad k=mn
 \tag{2}
\]
for integers \(m,n\geq1\).

This note excludes (1) from a noninvertible Keller map.  The key is a
local estimate at \(v=0\), not finiteness of the normalized map.
If
\[
 \rho=\max\left\{
 0,
 \frac{\operatorname {pole}_0(a)}2,
 \frac{\operatorname {pole}_0(b)}3,
 \frac{\operatorname {pole}_0(c)}4,
 \frac{\operatorname {pole}_0(d)}5,
 \frac{\operatorname {pole}_0(q)}6
 \right\},
 \tag{3}
\]
then the exact invariant-level analysis gives
\[
 \boxed{n\ge2\rho.}
 \tag{4}
\]
In fact every surviving finite pole profile has ratio at least three;
the weaker bound (4) is all that is needed.

Undoing the rational depression, the original degree-six coordinate
has the form
\[
 G(u,y)=g\bigl(v,\gamma u^{mn+1}y+t(u)\bigr).
 \tag{5}
\]
Polynomiality of \(G\), together with (4), makes
\[
 G(0,y)=g_0+g_1y.
 \tag{6}
\]
If \(g_1\ne0\), this coordinate separates points of the line \(u=0\).
If \(g_1=0\), the constant-Jacobian identity forces
\(F(0,y)\) to be affine and nonconstant.  Thus the original Keller
map is injective on \(u=0\).

Gwoździewicz's injectivity-on-one-line theorem then makes the map a
polynomial automorphism.  It therefore cannot be the noninvertible
map in the normal-degree reduction.  Combined with the two
constant-\(h\) exclusions, this closes the last cube-\(h\) chart at
normal \(y\)-degrees \((9,6)\).

## 1. Cyclic quotient and the pole of the Laurent time

Write
\[
 g=w^6+aw^4+bw^3+cw^2+dw+q,
 \tag{7}
\]
and let \(f\) be the full seven-constant approximate-root polynomial.
The cyclic reduction proves
\[
 a,b,c,d,q\in\mathbf C[v,v^{-1}]
 \tag{8}
\]
and
\[
 [f,g]_{v,w}=\frac{\lambda}{m\gamma v^{n+1}},
 \qquad
 6A_5=\alpha+\beta v^{-n}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Consequently \(A_5\) has pole order exactly \(n\) at \(v=0\) and is
regular at every other finite point and at \(v=\infty\).

Put \(z=v^{-1}\).  The Jacobian identity becomes
\[
 [f,g]_{z,w}=-\frac{\lambda}{m\gamma}z^{n-1}.
 \tag{10}
\]
No polynomiality of \(f,g\) in \(z\) will be assumed below.  Their
Laurent coefficients may have poles at the other end \(z=0\); only
their orders at \(v=0\) enter the argument.

## 2. The invariant pole bound

We record the precise consequence of the already computed boundary
systems.

> **Pole-profile lemma.**  On a rational invariant-level curve whose
> terminal function has the one-pole form in (9), either all five
> coefficients are regular at \(v=0\), or the weighted coefficient
> pole \(\rho>0\) satisfies \(n\ge2\rho\).

Here is the case audit.

### 2.1 At least one extra upper constant is nonzero

Let \(k_*\in\{8,7,5,4,2,1\}\) be the greatest exponent with
\(\kappa_{k_*}\ne0\), and put \(\delta=9-k_*\).  The exact weighted
boundary calculation in
`NORMAL_DEGREE_96_CUBE_CONSTANT_H_EXTRA_KAPPA_EXCLUSION.md`
has only:

1. the pure cusp, with
   \[
   \operatorname {pole}(A_5)=14\rho;
   \]
2. a balanced or singular corrected profile, with
   \[
   \operatorname {pole}(A_5)=(14-\delta)\rho.
   \]

The twenty-four projective-chart Gröbner certificates and the
secondary-cone calculation prove that the displayed terminal
coefficient never vanishes.  Since
\[
 14-\delta\in\{13,12,10,9,7,6\},
 \tag{11}
\]
one has \(n\ge6\rho\) throughout this case.

### 2.2 Only \(j\) remains and \(U=0\)

On \(U=0,V\ne0\), the complete outer Newton table for the centered
variables \(C,D\) is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(\operatorname {pole}C,\operatorname {pole}D)&
\operatorname {pole}(A_5)&\rho&
\operatorname {pole}(A_5)/\rho\\ \hline
(s,4s)&9s&3s&3\\
(-3s,-s)&6s&2s&3\\
(-s,-s)&2s&2s/3&3\\
(-s,-4s)&2s&2s/3&3\\
(2s,-3s)&7s&s/2&14 .
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
Here \(s>0\); negative pole order means a zero.  The horizontal edge
has bounded coefficients.  Exact substitution of every edge equation
shows that the listed leading coefficient of \(A_5\) is nonzero.

When \(U=V=Z=0\), the non-elementary coefficient curve is
\[
 C^3=6Q^2+8jQ+\frac83W.
 \tag{13}
\]
For \(W\ne j^2\), its projective normalization has genus one and
cannot have the rational function field \(K=\mathbf C(v)\).  For
\(W=j^2\), it is the cusp, whose profile has
\[
 n=7e,\qquad \rho=e/2.
 \tag{14}
\]

When \(U=V=0,Z\ne0\), elimination has discriminant
\[
 38263752\,C^{20}(3C^3-8W+8j^2).
 \tag{15}
\]
Again the normalization has genus one unless \(W=j^2\).  In the
exceptional rational case, with \(\epsilon^2=1/6\),
\[
 A_5=\frac{4Z^2}{27}t^{-6}-\frac{\epsilon}{16}t^7.
 \tag{16}
\]
It has two distinct poles.  Because \(t\) and \(v\) both generate the
coefficient function field, they differ by a Möbius transformation;
thus (16) cannot become the one-pole function in (9).  This is the
sole local only-\(j\) profile with ratio below two, and the global
one-pole time removes it.

### 2.3 Only \(j\) remains and \(U\ne0\)

Solving the first and fourth invariant equations for \(a,b\), then
eliminating \(Q\), gives the exact plane equation \(P(C,D)=0\) whose
unique positive upper face is
\[
 -243D^2(27C^5-32UD^2)^2.
 \tag{17}
\]
Its nonconstant pole branch has
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname {pole}(C,D,Q,a,b)&=(2,5,8,6,9)e,\\
\operatorname {pole}(c,d,q)&=(12,15,18)e,\\
\operatorname {pole}(A_5)&=12e.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus \(\rho=3e\) and the ratio is four.  The lower branch \(D=0\)
has
\[
 \operatorname {pole}(C,Q,A_5)=(2,3,7)e,
 \qquad \rho=e/2,
 \tag{19}
\]
so its ratio is fourteen.

The remaining outer edges are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{parameters}&
(\operatorname {pole}C,\operatorname {pole}D)&
\operatorname {pole}(A_5)&
\operatorname {pole}(A_5)/\rho\\ \hline
V\ne0&(2e,-3e)&7e&14\\
V\ne0&(-e,-e)&4e&4\\
V=0,\ Z\ne0&\text{right double edge}&7e&14\\
V=0,\ Z\ne0&(-e,-3e)&4e&4 .
\end{array}
\tag{19a}
\]
On the right double edge, the first equation is \(9C^2D=8Z\).
Writing
\[
 C=C_0x^2,\qquad
 D=\frac{8Z}{9C^2}+D_1x^{-9}
\]
gives the exact next leading rows
\[
\begin{aligned}
 [x^2]P&=-C_0\left(
 177147C_0^9D_1^2-32768U^2Z^2\right),\\
 [x^7]A_5&=\frac{243C_0^8D_1}{4096UZ}\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19b}
\]
Thus the unreduced double edge also has ratio fourteen.  If
\(V=Z=0\), the lower edge is the \(D=0\) cusp already listed above;
the remaining horizontal edge is bounded.

The denominator chart \(C\to0\) has two invariant limits.  In the
unbounded one,
\[
 D^2\longrightarrow-\frac{4U}{27},\qquad
 CQ\longrightarrow-\frac{8U}{27},
 \tag{20}
\]
and, if \(C\) vanishes to order \(e\),
\[
 \operatorname {pole}(a)=2e,\qquad
 \rho=e,\qquad
 \operatorname {pole}(A_5)=4e.
 \tag{21}
\]
The other limit \(D^2\to-4U/9\) is bounded.  At \(C=0\), the
equations for \((D,Q,a,b)\), ordered as
\((I_{10},I_{11},I_{12},I_{13})\), have nonzero triangular
diagonal
\[
 -\frac92D,\quad-\frac92D,\quad\frac34D^2,\quad-\frac U3.
\]
The formal implicit-function theorem therefore supplies a unique
bounded branch.

The exceptional coefficient \(2UD-CV\) is checked separately in the
same elimination.  It also cannot hide a nearby high-order
denominator branch.  Indeed,
\[
 [Q]E_{11}=144C(2UD-CV),\qquad
 [Q^2]E_{12}=-1944C^2U.
\]
If \(C\) is a local unit and \(C,D\) are bounded, a pole of \(Q\)
would make the displayed \(Q^2\)-term uniquely dominant in \(E_{12}\),
which is impossible.  If \(C\to0\) while \(D\) is a unit, then
\(2UD-CV\) is a unit.  Finally, if \(C,D\to0\), writing
\(p=\operatorname {ord}C>0\) and
\(-q=\operatorname {ord}Q<0\), the same \(Q^2\)-term compared with
the \(CQ\)-term in \(E_{12}\) forces \(q\le p\); this is exactly the
lower Newton chart already listed in (19a), not a new denominator
profile.

If \(C,D\) both have poles, high-order cancellation in
\(2UD-CV\) would require \(V\ne0\) and equal pole orders for \(C,D\).
The Newton polygon of the denominator-free resultant \(P(C,D)\) has
no positive equal-order outer edge, so such an arc is impossible.
On every listed positive edge the two terms of \(2UD-CV\) have
different orders.  When \(V=0\), the denominator is the single
monomial \(2UD\), so it cannot acquire a cancellation order at all.

For \(V\ne0\), the Newton polygon has vertices
\[
 (0,2),(2,0),(7,0),(10,2),(0,6).
\]
After setting \(V=0\), the only changed edges are the two special
rows of (19a), so the displayed charts exhaust the compactification.
Their minimum ratio is four.

Equations (11)--(21) prove the pole-profile lemma.  Notice that it
does not assert a false universal one-place bound on arbitrary
rational invariant curves: the two-ended curve (16) is the exact
counterexample, and is excluded using the global time.

## 3. Undoing depression: the center cannot be too singular

The original degree-six coordinate is recovered by
\[
 G(u,y)=g\bigl(u^m,\gamma u^s y+t(u)\bigr),
 \qquad s=k+1=mn+1,
 \tag{22}
\]
for a rational depressing center \(t(u)\).

Let
\[
 e=\max\{0,-\operatorname {ord}_{u=0}t\}.
\]
The coefficient of \(w^j\) in (7) has \(u\)-pole order at most
\[
 m(6-j)\rho.
 \tag{23}
\]
If \(e>m\rho\), then in
\[
 G(u,0)=g(u^m,t(u))
 \tag{24}
\]
the term \(t^6\) has pole order \(6e\), while every other term has
strictly smaller pole order:
\[
 m(6-j)\rho+je<6e
 \qquad (j<6).
\]
It therefore cannot cancel.  This contradicts
\(G(u,0)\in\mathbf C[u]\), and proves
\[
 \boxed{e\le m\rho.}
 \tag{25}
\]

Write
\[
 G(u,y)=\sum_{i=0}^6G_i(u)y^i.
\]
Taylor expansion of (22) gives
\[
 G_i(u)=
 \frac{(\gamma u^s)^i}{i!}
 \partial_w^i g(u^m,t(u)).
 \tag{26}
\]
Using (23) and (25), every summand in the last derivative has
\(u\)-pole order at most \(m(6-i)\rho\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname {ord}_{u=0}G_i
 \ge i(mn+1)-m(6-i)\rho.
 \tag{27}
\]
The pole-profile lemma gives \(n\ge2\rho\), and therefore, for every
\(i\ge2\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname {ord}_{u=0}G_i
&\ge m\bigl(in-(6-i)\rho\bigr)+i\\
&\ge m(3i-6)\rho+i>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Thus \(G_i(0)=0\) for \(i\ge2\), proving (6).  If \(\rho=0\), the
same proof gives \(e=0\), and (27) is positive without using (4).

## 4. The source line is injective

Write
\[
 G(0,y)=g_0+g_1y.
\]
If \(g_1\ne0\), equality of the two-coordinate images of two points
on \(u=0\) already implies equality of their \(G\)-coordinates, and
hence equality of the points.

Suppose \(g_1=0\).  Restrict the Keller identity
\[
 F_uG_y-F_yG_u=\lambda\ne0
 \tag{29}
\]
to \(u=0\).  Since \(G_y(0,y)=0\), it becomes
\[
 -F_y(0,y)G_u(0,y)=\lambda.
 \tag{30}
\]
Both factors lie in \(\mathbf C[y]\).  Their product is a nonzero
constant, so each is a nonzero constant.  Consequently
\[
 F(0,y)=f_0+f_1y,\qquad f_1\ne0.
 \tag{31}
\]
The restriction \((F,G)|_{u=0}\) is again injective.

Gwoździewicz proved that a plane polynomial map over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic zero, with nonzero constant Jacobian,
is a polynomial automorphism if it is injective on one affine line:
J. Gwoździewicz, *Injectivity on one line*,
[arXiv:alg-geom/9305008](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9305008).
Applying the theorem to (6) or (31) proves that the original map is
an automorphism.  This contradicts its role as a minimal
noninvertible Keller map and excludes the pure-power chart.

## Consequence and scope

The normal-degree \((9,6)\) reduction has three terminal parts:

1. the connected noncube chart, excluded in
   `NORMAL_DEGREE_ARITHMETIC_FRONTIER_AND_96_CONNECTED_REDUCTION.md`;
2. the full constant-\(h\) cube chart, excluded in the two
   constant-\(h\) notes;
3. the nonconstant pure-power cube chart, excluded here.

Thus no noninvertible Keller map survives the \((9,6)\) normal-degree
frontier.  This is a closure of that degree case, not a proof of the
Jacobian conjecture in arbitrary degree.

The exact symbolic checks are in
`verify_normal_degree_96_pure_power_line_injectivity_exclusion.py`.
They verify the coordinate changes, the threshold-two Taylor
inequalities, the complete \(U=0,V\ne0\) edge ratios, the exceptional
two-ended time, and the affine-line dichotomy.
