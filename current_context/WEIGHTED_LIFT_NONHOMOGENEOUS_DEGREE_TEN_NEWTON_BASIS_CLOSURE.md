# Arbitrary nonhomogeneous targets through degree ten

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

Let \(F=(A,B,C)\) be the exact Gallagher weighted lift, let \(U\)
be its exact second-subduction coordinate, and let \(R_{\le2}\) be
an arbitrary polynomial in \(A,B,C\) of target degree at most two.
On every polynomial graph, every nonconstant target polynomial
\(Q(A,B,C)\) of target degree at most ten satisfies
\[
\boxed{
J_{x,y}\bigl(U+R_{\le2},Q\bigr)\notin\mathbf C^\times.
}
\tag{1}
\]

This extends the degree-nine theorem by an exact finite-dimensional
change of basis.  The four primitive cusp collisions through degree
ten are replaced first by
\[
T,\quad AT,\quad BT,\quad CT.
\]
The last two are then replaced by the exact second and third
subductions
\[
U,\qquad W.
\]
After the coefficient of \(U\) is removed modulo the **actual first
coordinate** \(U+R_{\le2}\), the remaining augmented Newton atoms are
\[
\boxed{
T,\quad AT,\quad W,
}
\tag{2}
\]
together with ordinary monomials outside the four cusp multiples.

At a regular boundary all these atoms have distinct projected Newton
labels for every graph degree.  At a pole boundary the only repeated
label is shared by \(W\) and \(A^2B^3\).  That two-dimensional group
has the exact top polynomial
\[
f^{20}\left(a u^{27}+b u^{25}\right),
\tag{3}
\]
whose boundary Wronskian is never zero.  No SAGBI termination theorem
is used.

This is a theorem about polynomial-graph restrictions of the
three-dimensional weighted lift.  It is not a proof of the plane
Jacobian conjecture.

## 1. Boundary notation

Put
\[
u=1+xy,\qquad
\gamma=\sum_{j=\rho}^{M}x^j f_j(u),\qquad f=f_\rho\ne0.
\tag{4}
\]
For a regular boundary, \(\rho=0\),
\[
L=\deg f\ge1,\qquad
f(1)=1,\qquad f'(1)=-\frac{57}{34}.
\tag{5}
\]
For a pole boundary, \(\rho<0\),
\[
\nu=\operatorname{ord}_{u=1}f\ge2-\rho,\qquad
L+\rho+1\ge3.
\tag{6}
\]
The exact leading boundary forms of the original generators and the
second subduction are
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=x^{-2}\bigl(q_6u^6f^4+\cdots\bigr),\\
B&=x^{-1}\bigl(p_5u^5f^4+\cdots\bigr),\\
C&=x f,\\
U&=x^{-5}\bigl(\theta u^{20}f^{17}+\cdots\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\qquad p_5q_6\theta\ne0.
\tag{7}
\]
At a regular boundary every omitted seed term loses at least
\(L+1\) in \(u\)-degree.  At a pole boundary it has strictly later
\(x\)-order.

For an ordinary monomial \(A^aB^bC^c\), define
\[
\beta=-2a-b+c,\qquad
h=6a+5b,\qquad
q=4a+4b+c=h+\beta.
\tag{8}
\]
Its regular top is
\[
x^\beta u^h f^q,
\tag{9}
\]
up to a nonzero constant.  At a pole its \(x\)-order is
\[
E=\beta+\rho q.
\tag{10}
\]

## 2. The exact degree-ten basis

The kernel of \((a,b,c)\mapsto(\beta,h)\), equivalently of
\((a,b,c)\mapsto(\beta,q)\), is generated by
\[
(5,-6,4).
\tag{11}
\]
Among nonconstant ordinary monomials of target degree at most ten,
the repeated Newton labels are exactly the four pairs
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{lower cusp side}&\text{upper cusp side}\\ \hline
B^6&A^5C^4\\
AB^6&A^6C^4\\
B^7&A^5BC^4\\
B^6C&A^5C^5.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]

Let \(T\) be the exact first maximal cusp subduction,
\[
\begin{aligned}
T={}&p_5^6A^5C^4-q_6^5B^6
 +c_1A^4BC^3+c_2A^3B^2C^2\\
&\quad+c_3A^2B^3C+c_4AB^4,
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
with the exact nonzero seed constants already audited in
`WEIGHTED_LIFT_FIRST_NONLINEAR_CUSP_SUBDUCTION.md`.  Its leading
coefficient on \(A^5C^4\) is \(p_5^6\ne0\).  Multiplying (13) by
\(1,A,B,C\) therefore replaces the four upper-side monomials in
(12) by
\[
T,\quad AT,\quad BT,\quad CT
\tag{14}
\]
through an invertible triangular change of basis.

The exact second subduction has the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
U={}&p_5^5TC-\lambda_0B^5
+d_0A^4C^3+d_1A^3BC^2\\
&\quad+d_2A^2B^2C+d_3AB^3,
\end{aligned}
\qquad p_5^5\ne0,
\tag{15}
\]
so \(CT\) can be replaced by \(U\).  The exact next saturation
polynomial is
\[
\begin{aligned}
W={}&q_6^5BT-p_5\lambda_0A^5C^3
+r_1A^4BC^2\\
&\quad+r_2A^3B^2C+r_3A^2B^3,
\end{aligned}
\qquad q_6^5\ne0,
\tag{16}
\]
so \(BT\) can be replaced by \(W\).

Equations (13)--(16) are all of target degree at most ten.  Relative
to the four removed monomials, the diagonal of the total basis change
is
\[
p_5^6,\qquad p_5^6,\qquad
q_6^5p_5^6,\qquad p_5^{11},
\tag{17}
\]
and is nonzero.  Thus every target of degree at most ten has a unique
expression
\[
Q=c+\mu U+\lambda_TT+\lambda_{AT}AT+\lambda_WW
 +\sum\nolimits' \lambda_{abc}A^aB^bC^c,
\tag{18}
\]
where the prime omits the four upper-side monomials in (12).

There is one necessary caution when \(R_{\le2}\ne0\).  It is not
valid merely to discard \(\mu U\), because
\(J(U+R_{\le2},U)=J(R_{\le2},U)\).  Instead put
\[
P=U+R_{\le2},\qquad Q^\flat=Q-\mu P.
\tag{19}
\]
Then
\[
J(P,Q^\flat)=J(P,Q),
\tag{20}
\]
and \(Q^\flat\) has no \(U\)-coefficient; subtraction in (19) only
adjusts ordinary coefficients of degree at most two.  If
\(Q^\flat\) is constant, the Jacobian is zero.  Otherwise its support
consists of the atoms (2) and the retained ordinary monomials.

## 3. Exact augmented boundary atoms

The exact boundary polynomials give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{atom}&\beta&\text{regular top }u^hf^q&
(\beta,h,q)\\ \hline
T&-6&\lambda_0u^{25}f^{19}&(-6,25,19)\\
AT&-8&q_6\lambda_0u^{31}f^{23}&(-8,31,23).
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]
Every non-top term in either row loses at least one explicit
\(u\)-power and at least one \(f\)-power.  It therefore loses at
least \(L+1\) at a regular boundary and has later \(x\)-order at a
pole boundary.

The exact frontier of \(W\) is
\[
\kappa_{19}u^{26}f^{19}
+\kappa_{20}u^{25}f^{20},
\qquad \kappa_{19}\kappa_{20}\ne0.
\tag{22}
\]
The \(f^{20}\)-term displayed in (22) is the unique term of maximal
\(f\)-power in \(W\).  Consequently it is the unique polar top.
For a regular boundary of degree \(L\ge2\), it is also the unique
degree top because
\[
(25+20L)-(26+19L)=L-1>0.
\tag{23}
\]
For \(L=1\), (5) forces
\[
f(u)=\frac{91}{34}-\frac{57}{34}u.
\tag{24}
\]
The two terms in (22) tie in degree, but their exact combined
\(u^{45}\)-coefficient is nonzero.  Thus \(W\) has regular projected
label
\[
\begin{cases}
(-7,\,25+20L),&L\ge2,\\
(-7,\,45),&L=1.
\end{cases}
\tag{25}
\]

## 4. Regular-boundary separation

After the four omissions in (12), the ordinary labels
\((\beta,h)\) are pairwise distinct.  Neither
\[
(-6,25),\qquad(-8,31)
\tag{26}
\]
is the label of an ordinary monomial, so \(T\) and \(AT\) are also
singletons.

It remains to compare \(W\) with ordinary atoms of boundary order
\(\beta=-7\).  For \(L\ge2\), equality of projected degrees would
give
\[
(L+1)h-7L=25+20L,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
(L+1)(h-27)=-2,
\tag{27}
\]
which is impossible because \(L+1\ge3\).  For \(L=1\), equality
would require \(h=26\).  The equation
\[
6a+5b=26,\qquad c=-7+2a+b\ge0
\tag{28}
\]
has no nonnegative solution.  Thus every augmented atom has a
distinct regular projected label.

Choose the minimum \(\beta\) in the support of \(Q^\flat\), and at
that order choose the unique atom of largest regular degree.  If its
top has \(x\)-order \(\beta_V\) and \(u\)-degree \(D_V\), while
\[
U_{\rm top}=\theta x^{-5}u^{D_U},
\qquad D_U=20+17L,
\tag{29}
\]
then the leading Wronskian characteristic is
\[
-5D_V-\beta_VD_U.
\tag{30}
\]
For the three new atoms, (30) equals
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
T&7L-5\\
AT&21L+5\\
W&19L+15
\end{array}
\qquad(L\ge2),
\tag{31}
\]
and is nonzero.  At \(L=1\), the \(W\)-characteristic is
\[
-5\cdot45+7\cdot37=34.
\tag{32}
\]

For an ordinary leading atom, the deviation argument from the
degree-eight Newton theorem applies: if (30) vanishes, the first
non-monomial term of \(f\) produces a nonzero coefficient at degree
drop at most \(L\), while the next standard atom is lower by at least
\(L+1\).  The only possible new interference is \(W\) on the
\(\beta=-7\) face.  If an ordinary atom of height \(h\) lies above
\(W\), its degree excess is
\[
(L+1)(h-27)+2.
\tag{33}
\]
For \(h\ge28\), this is greater than \(L\).  The remaining case
\(h=27\) is exactly \(A^2B^3\), whose characteristic is
\[
19L+5\ne0.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus \(W\) cannot enter a deviation coefficient.  The unique
earliest regular Wronskian is nonzero.

## 5. Pole-boundary separation

Put
\[
\alpha=-5+17\rho,\qquad E=\beta+\rho q.
\tag{35}
\]
The polar labels \((\beta,q)\) of all retained ordinary atoms,
\(T\), and \(AT\) are pairwise distinct.  The sole exception after
adjoining \(W\) is
\[
(\beta_W,q_W)=(-7,20)
=(\beta_{A^2B^3},q_{A^2B^3}).
\tag{36}
\]
Because the \(f^{20}\)-term of \(W\) is unique, the exact top of this
two-dimensional group is (3), with \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\).

At the globally earliest \(x\)-order \(E_0\), group the target atoms
by \(q\), and let \(q_{\min}\) be the smallest nonzero group.  First
suppose it is a singleton standard atom
\[
V=c\,u^hf^q,\qquad h=q-\beta.
\tag{37}
\]
Up to a nonzero monomial factor, its Wronskian with
\(u^{20}f^{17}\) is
\[
B\,u f'+C\,f,
\qquad
B=\alpha q-17E_0,\quad
C=\alpha h-20E_0.
\tag{38}
\]
If \(B\ne0\), its order at \(u=1\) is
\[
(17+q)\nu-1.
\tag{39}
\]
If \(B=0\), then
\[
\beta=-\frac{5q}{17},\qquad
C=2E_0=\frac{2\alpha q}{17}\ne0,
\tag{40}
\]
and the order is \((17+q)\nu\).  Every group of larger \(q\) has
order at least
\[
(17+q+1)\nu-1>(17+q)\nu,
\tag{41}
\]
because \(\nu\ge3\).  Hence the singleton group cannot be cancelled.

Now suppose the exceptional group (3) is the \(q_{\min}\)-group.
Writing
\[
P(u)=a u^{27}+b u^{25},
\tag{42}
\]
direct differentiation gives, up to a nonzero monomial factor,
\[
19u f'P
+f\bigl(\alpha uP'-20E_0P\bigr).
\tag{43}
\]
If \(P(1)\ne0\), the first surviving coefficient has order
\(37\nu-1\) and is proportional to \(19\nu P(1)\).
If \(P(1)=0\), then \(b=-a\ne0\) and
\[
P=a u^{25}(u^2-1)
\tag{44}
\]
has a simple zero at \(u=1\).  The coefficient at order \(37\nu\)
is proportional to
\[
(19\nu+\alpha)P'(1).
\tag{45}
\]
The graph constraint (6) gives
\[
19\nu+\alpha
\ge19(2-\rho)-5+17\rho
=33-2\rho>0.
\tag{46}
\]
Thus (43) is nonzero in both cases.  A group with \(q\ge21\) starts
at order at least \(38\nu-1>37\nu\), so it cannot interfere.
This proves polar separation without an endpoint-at-infinity
argument.

## 6. First-coordinate perturbations and conclusion

At a regular boundary every nonconstant monomial of \(R_{\le2}\)
has boundary order at least \(-4>-5\).  At a pole boundary it has
\(\beta\ge-4\), \(q\le8\), and hence
\[
E_R-\alpha=(\beta+5)+\rho(q-17)>0.
\tag{47}
\]
Therefore \(J(R_{\le2},Q^\flat)\) is later than the isolated
coefficient of \(J(U,Q^\flat)\) at both kinds of boundary.

If the isolated coefficient occurs at \(x\)-order zero, it is still
not a nonzero constant: the regular coefficient has positive
\(u\)-degree, while the polar coefficients in (39)--(45) have
positive vanishing order.  Combining this observation with
(19)--(20) proves (1).

The argument uses only the exact generators \(T,U,W\) needed by the
degree-ten vector space.  It does not assert that
\(\{A,B,C,T,U,W\}\) is a complete SAGBI basis, and it makes no claim
in degree eleven.
