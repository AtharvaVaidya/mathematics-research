# Rational descent for the seven-mode radial branch

Date: 24 July 2026

## 1. Separate the Belyi root from the Kummer lift

Let \(\Bbbk\) be an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, put
\(L=\Bbbk(z,w)\), and let
\[
P_0=z^2\frac{U(w)}w,\qquad
Q_0=z^3\frac{V(w)}w,\qquad
R(w)=\frac{wV(w)^2}{U(w)^3},
\]
Given a bounded rational pair \((P,Q)\), the
normalized formal comparison branch \(T=(Z,W)\) is characterized by
\[
P_0(Z,W)=P,\qquad Q_0(Z,W)=Q.
\tag{1}
\]
Eliminating \(Z\) gives the one-variable equation
\[
R(W)=\frac{Q^2}{P^3}.
\tag{2}
\]
Conversely, if a root \(W\) of (2) belongs to \(L\), then
\[
Z=\frac{Q\,U(W)}{P\,V(W)}
\tag{3}
\]
belongs to \(L\) and satisfies both equations in (1).  Indeed (2) gives
\[
Q^2U(W)^3=P^3WV(W)^2,
\]
which verifies (1) after substituting (3).  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad T\in L^2\ \Longleftrightarrow\
       \text{the normalized formal root }W\text{ of (2) lies in }L.\quad}
\tag{4}
\]
This is the exact rational-monodromy criterion.  It is stronger and more
precise than integrality of \(W\) on the normalization of the
correspondence.

Clearing denominators, the root equation is
\[
\mathcal G(T)
=P^3\,T\,V(T)^2-Q^2U(T)^3=0.
\tag{5}
\]
On any normal affine chart \(A\subset L\) on which a unit multiple of
\(\mathcal G\) is monic, the chosen \(W\) is integral over \(A\).  If (4)
has already been proved, normality then gives \(W\in A\).  Formula (3)
similarly gives \(Z\in A\) once its displayed denominators are units, or
after checking their divisorial valuations.  Thus normality is useful for
the **no-pole step after field descent**, but cannot prove field descent.

## 2. Exact fifth-power valuation criterion

The original coordinate is a fifth root in Darboux coordinates
\[
r=\frac{z^5}{5w},\qquad s=\frac1w,\qquad z^5=\frac{5r}{s}.
\tag{6}
\]
Suppose a rational symplectic branch has already produced rational
candidate images \(\mathcal R,\mathcal S\in L\) of \(r,s\).  Then it lifts
to rational \(z,w\)-coordinates exactly when
\[
W=\mathcal S^{-1}\in L,\qquad
D:=\frac{5\mathcal R}{\mathcal S}\in L^{\times5}.
\tag{7}
\]
Over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero constant field, the
second condition is equivalent to
\[
\nu_E(D)\equiv0\pmod5
\quad\text{for every prime divisor }E
\tag{8}
\]
of the rational surface.  This is simply unique factorization in
\(L\), including the valuations at the compactification boundary.
The normalized identity jet selects the unique fifth root congruent to
\(z\), rather than one of its four other scalar multiples.

There is a useful base-field version.  Put \(L_0=\Bbbk(r,s)\) and
\(q=5r/s\), so \(L_0(z)/L_0\) is the Kummer extension \(z^5=q\).  A
rational self-map \(\tau\) of \(L_0\) lifts to this extension iff
\[
\tau(q)=q^j h^5
\quad\text{for some }j\in\{1,2,3,4\},\ h\in L_0^\times.
\tag{9}
\]
For the normalized identity branch, \(j=1\), so the exact criterion is
\[
\frac{\tau(q)}q\in L_0^{\times5}.
\tag{10}
\]
Necessity is immediate.  Sufficiency sends \(z\) to \(hz^j\).  The
exponent \(j=0\) cannot occur for an automorphism, since an automorphism
preserves the subgroup of fifth powers and \(q\) is not a fifth power.
Conversely,
the kernel of
\[
L_0^\times/L_0^{\times5}
\longrightarrow
L_0(z)^\times/L_0(z)^{\times5}
\]
is the cyclic subgroup generated by the class of \(q\), which proves (9).

If \(D\) is known by ring integrality to be a monic polynomial of degree
five in \(z\),
\[
D=z^5+a_4z^4+a_3z^3+a_2z^2+a_1z+a_0
\quad (a_i\in \Bbbk(w)),
\tag{11}
\]
then the infinite-looking valuation test becomes four identities:
\[
\begin{aligned}
5a_3&=2a_4^2,\\
25a_2&=2a_4^3,\\
125a_1&=a_4^4,\\
3125a_0&=a_4^5.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Indeed a monic degree-five fifth power must be
\((z+a_4/5)^5\), and (12) is also sufficient.  More generally, for a
reduced rational function, (8) says exactly that every numerator and
denominator factor occurs with multiplicity divisible by five.

## 3. The first finite obstruction: the weight-five resonance

For the seven radial potentials
\[
H_{d,C}=-\frac{z^{5-d}C(w)}{(5-d)w},
\]
the first Kummer-resonant commutators occur at \(d+e=5\).  Their
Hamiltonians are independent of \(z\):
\[
h_{C,D}(w)=
\frac{(wC'-C)D}{5-d}
+\frac{C(D-wD')}{5-e}.
\tag{13}
\]
In Darboux coordinates the time-\(\epsilon\) flow of \(h(w)\) fixes
\(s\) and translates \(r\).  Consequently
\[
z^5\longmapsto z^5+\epsilon B_h(w),\qquad
B_h(w)=5w^3h'(w)
\tag{14}
\]
up to the harmless global Hamiltonian sign convention.  The polynomial
in (14) is a fifth power in \(K(w)[z]\) iff \(B_h=0\): its \(z^4,\ldots,z\)
coefficients vanish, so (12) reduces to \(B_h=0\).  Equivalently, if
\(B_h\ne0\), then
\[
\gcd(z^5+B_h,5z^4)=1
\]
and every zero divisor has valuation one.

This calculation applies when an **exact ordered rational
factorization through weight five** isolates the resonant translation
factor (14).  Under that hypothesis, the factor has a rational Kummer
lift if and only if its class \(h\in K(w)/K\) vanishes.  Merely knowing
that the full time-one map is rational—even after rational lower-weight
factors have been removed—does not establish that the isolated flow
(14) is rational: rationality need not pass from a time-one map to the
flow of its leading logarithmic symbol.  Thus this is a genuine finite
Kummer-integrality obstruction for a factorized branch, not an
unconditional consequence of rationality of the whole seven-mode
branch.

For the normalized \(k=1\) outer pair, order the modes as
\[
C_{1,-}=1,\ C_{1,+},\
C_{2,-}=w,\ C_{2,+},\
C_{3,-}=w,\ C_{3,+},\
C_{4,+}=w^2.
\]
The \(d+e=5\) bracket \(C_{2,-}\) with \(C_{3,-}\) is zero.  The other
five polynomials \(B_h\), in the order
\[
(1-,4+),(1+,4+),(2-,3+),(2+,3-),(2+,3+),
\]
are, up to the common Hamiltonian sign convention,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&-\frac{25}{2}w^4,\\
&-\frac{50}{27}w^5
\bigl(54w^3+675w^2+3450w+9250\bigr),\\
&-\frac9{25}w^5(2w+5),\\
&\frac6{175}w^5(3w^2+16w+35),\\
&-\frac6{875}w^6(15w+28).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Their coefficient matrix in degrees \(4,\ldots,8\) has determinant
\[
\frac{34992}{1225}\ne0.
\tag{16}
\]
Hence, for such an exact rational factorization, the first resonant
Kummer obstruction is exactly the five
bilinear conditions
\[
x_{1,-}x_{4,+}
=x_{1,+}x_{4,+}
=x_{2,-}x_{3,+}
=x_{2,+}x_{3,-}
=x_{2,+}x_{3,+}=0.
\tag{17}
\]
This is a low-weight structural obstruction, obtained without a
coefficient Gröbner basis.  It does not yet eliminate the components on
which only mutually commuting low modes, or a single endpoint family,
survive.  Those surviving components identify exactly what a higher
rational-descent step must address.

The same fixed degree-\(4,\ldots,8\) endpoint minor has rank five for
the complete \(k=2\) coefficient field and for all five \(k=3\) outer
Hurwitz points.  At \(p=32003\), its determinant on the two rational
factors and the irreducible cubic factor is respectively
\[
5231,\qquad -9661,\qquad
-4276+6179t-9676t^2,
\tag{18}
\]
and every value is nonzero.  Since the \(k=3\) outer Hurwitz scheme is
finite étale over \(\mathbf Z_{(32003)}\), the determinant is a unit on
the whole localized outer scheme.  It follows that, on every
characteristic-zero outer branch of the bounded \((72,108)\) problem,
rationality of the isolated weight-five Kummer factor forces the five
bilinear conditions (17).  Thus the rank calculation for this
conditional obstruction is branch-independent at the actual scale, not
an artifact of the displayed \(k=1\) model.

The complete \(k=2\) quadratic field also has nonzero determinant
\[
3138+7521s\pmod {32003}.
\tag{19}
\]
More generally, put
\[
\begin{aligned}

\tag{20}

The outer ODE gives the displayed coefficients of \(V\).  In the five

\]
For example,

These quantities are nonzero on every tested factor.  The contact identity

coefficient, but does not by itself make it visibly nonzero: \(s_2\) is a

ODE force (22).  The admissible formal endpoint specialization
\(u_2=u_3=0\) makes \(\Delta_k=0\).  Proving (22) nonzero for every \(k\)
therefore requires a genuinely global passport or common-root argument.
At present, all-\(k\) nonvanishing is a conjecture supported by the complete
\(k=1,2,3\) fibers.

\end{gathered}
\tag{27}
\]
These are the six cases left after imposing the conditional factorized
obstruction.  They are not known to contain every rational comparison
branch until rational endpoint factorization has been proved.

## 4. Conditional resolution of all six resonant strata

There is a uniform **candidate** nonresonant endpoint obstruction on every
stratum in (27).  It becomes a theorem after an exact rational
factorization isolates the indicated principal flow.  First consider
\(w=0\).  If \(X_0\ne0\), its principal Hamiltonian is
\[
H_0=-\frac{X_0z^4}{4w}.
\]
Writing \(m=w/z\), its exact principal flow is
\[
Z=z\left(1-\frac{3X_0t}{4}m\right)^{-1/3},
\qquad
W=w\left(1-\frac{3X_0t}{4}m\right)^{-4/3}.
\tag{28}
\]
The binomial has a simple divisor, so this map is rational only if
\(X_0=0\).  This cube-root obstruction is independent of \(k\).

After \(X_0=0\), the two universal low modes \(C=w\), with coordinates
\(X_2,X_4\), have Hamiltonian
\[
H_{\rm low}=-\frac{X_2z^3}{3}-\frac{X_4z^2}{2}.
\]
In the canonical coordinates
\[
q=\frac{z^5}{5},\qquad p=-\frac1{2w^2},\qquad
\Omega=dq\wedge dp,
\]
their flow fixes \(q\) and translates \(p\).  Equivalently,
\[
W=w\left(
1-2t(X_2z^{-2}+X_4z^{-3})w^2
\right)^{-1/2}.
\tag{29}
\]
The quadratic binomial in (29) is squarefree unless
\[
X_2=X_4=0.
\tag{30}
\]
The \(w\)-linear jet of the high \(d=1\) mode may join (29), but it has a
different power \(z^{-1}\) and cannot cancel either coefficient in (30).
Thus the square obstruction kills the two low \(C=w\) modes universally
in \(k\).

What remains consists only of high modes.  Put
\[
\xi=zw^k,\qquad N=5k+2,\qquad
y=\frac{w^{-N}}N,\qquad q_\infty=\frac{\xi^5}{5}.
\]
Then
\[
\Omega=dy\wedge dq_\infty.
\tag{31}
\]
The four high principal Hamiltonians combine as
\[
F(\xi)=-
\left(
\frac{X_1\xi^4}{4}+\frac{X_3\xi^3}{3}
+\frac{X_5\xi^2}{2}+X_6\xi
\right).
\tag{32}
\]
Their flow fixes \(\xi\) and translates \(y\), hence, up to the harmless
global Hamiltonian sign convention,
\[
W=w\left[
1-Nt\,w^N
\frac{X_1\xi^3+X_3\xi^2+X_5\xi+X_6}{\xi^4}
\right]^{-1/N}.
\tag{33}
\]
As a polynomial in \(w\), the factor in brackets is squarefree whenever
its displayed numerator is nonzero.  It is therefore an \(N\)-th power in
\(\Bbbk(\xi,w)\) only if
\[
X_1=X_3=X_5=X_6=0.
\tag{34}
\]
This is universal for every \(k\ge1\); its exponent is exactly the outer
contact order \(5k+2\).

For clarity, the candidate first obstruction on each stratum is:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{coordinates set to zero by (27)}
&\text{possible survivors}
&\text{successive factorized tests}\\ \hline
X_2,X_3,X_6&X_0,X_1,X_4,X_5&\text{cube, square, infinity}\\
X_3,X_5,X_6&X_0,X_1,X_2,X_4&\text{cube, square, infinity}\\
X_4,X_5,X_6&X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3&\text{cube, square, infinity}\\
X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3&X_4,X_5,X_6&\text{square, infinity}\\
X_0,X_1,X_3,X_5&X_2,X_4,X_6&\text{square, infinity}\\
X_0,X_1,X_4,X_5&X_2,X_3,X_6&\text{square, infinity}
\end{array}
\tag{35}
\]

Thus one obtains the following factorized rigidity statement:

> **Factorized seven-mode rigidity.**  If the normalized formal
> seven-mode symplectic branch admits a factorization in which the
> successive \(w=0\) cube and square factors and the infinity
> \(N\)-th-root factor are themselves rational, then all seven mode
> parameters vanish.

Each displayed binomial has a prime divisor of multiplicity one,
contradicting the required third-, second-, or \(N\)-th-power valuation.
No coefficient elimination is hidden in this factorized statement.

There is, however, a second gap beyond monodromy descent.  Rationality of
a single time-one map does not imply rationality of the full flow of its
leading logarithmic symbol.  Conjugating a rational tangent-to-identity
map gives only its infinitesimal associated-graded symbol; extracting the
finite exponential would require fractional iteration.  The elementary
warning is that \(w\mapsto w+w^3\) is rational although its logarithm
starts with \(w^3\partial_w\), whose pure flow contains a square root.
Within the present symplectic geometry, the maps
\[
(z,w)\longmapsto(zg(r)^2,wg(r)^5),\qquad r=z^5/w^2,
\]
from `RADIAL_PARAHORIC_GEOMETRY.md` give the same warning: the time-one
map is rational for rational \(g\), whereas fractional time involves
nonrational powers \(g^{2t},g^{5t}\).
Therefore a Rees-initial-map argument alone does **not** justify applying
(28), (29), or (33) to an arbitrary rational comparison branch.

The exact next lemma is consequently:

> **Rational endpoint factorization.**  On each of the six strata (27),
> show that rational descent of the whole symplectomorphism forces
> rational descent of the first cube, square, or \(N\)-th-root endpoint
> factor.

If this factorization lemma holds, (28)--(35) resolve every stratum
uniformly in \(k\).  Without it, the formulas identify the first
nonresonant Kummer obstruction but do not yet constitute a proof that the
whole branch is irrational.  Equation (4) remains the separate monodromy
criterion for descent of the chosen Belyi root \(W\).

## 5. Rational descent automatically has no interior poles

Although monodromy descent is still open, the second half of the proposed
bridge can be proved conditionally on it.  Let \(E\) be a prime divisor in
the torus \(zw\ne0\), and suppose the rational comparison branch has
\[
a=\nu_E(Z),\qquad b=\nu_E(W).
\tag{36}
\]
Since the source volume form is a unit at \(E\), symplecticity gives
\[
4a-3b+\nu_E(dZ\wedge dW)=0.
\]
For two rational functions on a smooth surface,
\[
\nu_E(dZ\wedge dW)\ge a+b-1.
\]
Consequently
\[
5a-2b\le1.
\tag{37}
\]

Regularity of
\[
P=P_0(Z,W)=Z^2U(W)/W,\qquad
Q=Q_0(Z,W)=Z^3V(W)/W
\]
supplies the opposite inequalities.  Write
\(\deg U=2k+1,\deg V=3k+1\).

- If \(b<0\), the leading terms of \(U(W),V(W)\) are unique, and
  regularity gives \(a+kb\ge0\).  Hence
  \[
  5a-2b\ge-(5k+2)b\ge5k+2>1,
  \]
  contradicting (37).
- If \(b=0\), coprimality of \(U,V\) means they cannot both vanish at the
  residue of \(W\).  At least one of \(P,Q\) therefore gives \(a\ge0\),
  and (37) forces \(a=0\).
- If \(b>0\), the nonzero constants \(U(0),V(0)\) give
  \[
  2a-b\ge0,\qquad3a-b\ge0.
  \]
  Combining these with (37) leaves the single integral possibility
  \[
  (a,b)=(1,2).
  \tag{38}
  \]

Thus every torus divisor has
\[
\nu_E(Z)\ge0,\qquad \nu_E(W)\ge0.
\tag{39}
\]
In particular a rationally descended branch has no interior denominator:
\(Z,W\in\Bbbk[z^{\pm1},w^{\pm1}]\).  Normality is not needed after this
direct valuation argument.  The only possible nontrivial interior divisor
is the zero type (38), on which \(Z,W\) vanish to orders \(1,2\); it is an
exceptional weighted-zero curve, not a pole.

This proves the no-interior-pole half of the
monodromy-and-divisor bridge from `RADIAL_SYMPLECTIC_BRIDGE.md`.
What remains after field descent is to control the four compactification
boundaries and, if necessary, exclude the exceptional zero type (38).
The integer inequalities are checked in
`route_bd_radial_rational_descent_criterion.py`.

## 6. The exceptional zero type is incompatible with the seven modes

The remaining type (38) can be classified globally.  At every torus prime
divisor, (36)--(38) give either
\[
(\nu_E(Z),\nu_E(W))=(0,0)
\quad\text{or}\quad(1,2).
\]
Therefore
\[
\operatorname {div}_{\mathbf G_m^2}(W/Z^2)=0.
\]
The units of the Laurent ring are monomials, so
\[
\frac{W}{Z^2}=c z^m w^n.
\tag{40}
\]
The normalized identity jet \(Z/z\to1,\ W/w\to1\) at \(z=\infty\)
forces
\[
c=1,\qquad m=-2,\qquad n=1.
\]
Consequently every rationally descended branch has the form
\[
Z=zh,\qquad W=wh^2,
\qquad h\in\Bbbk[z^{\pm1},w^{\pm1}].
\tag{41}
\]

Substitution of (41) into \(T^*\Omega=\Omega\) gives the linear Euler
equation
\[
h+(z\partial_z+2w\partial_w)h=1.
\tag{42}
\]
Put \(t=w/z^2\).  The Laurent solutions of (42) are exactly
\[
h=1+z^{-1}\phi(t),
\qquad \phi\in\Bbbk[t^{\pm1}].
\tag{43}
\]
The identity jet at \(z=\infty\), with \(w\) fixed, removes the negative
powers of \(t\), so in fact \(\phi\in\Bbbk[t]\).

Suppose \(\phi\ne0\), and let \(c_nt^n\) be its first nonzero monomial.
The first term of the formal comparison branch is
\[
\delta z=c_nz^{-2n}w^n,\qquad
\delta w=2c_nz^{-2n-1}w^{n+1}.
\tag{44}
\]
It has deficit
\[
d=2n+1
\]
and scalar-potential representative
\[
C=2c_nw^{n-1}.
\tag{45}
\]
This is never one of the seven bounded kernel classes:

- \(n=0\) gives \(d=1,\ C\propto w^{-1}\), outside the polynomial
  endpoint lattice;
- \(n=1\) gives \(d=3,\ C\) constant, whereas the two \(d=3\) classes are
  \(C=w\) and a polynomial vanishing to order at least two;
- \(n\ge2\) gives \(d\ge5\), where the universal kernel theorem says
  \(\ker\mathcal L_d=0\).

Thus \(\phi=0\), hence \(h=1\) and
\[
T=(z,w).
\tag{46}
\]

This removes the endpoint-factorization issue for a rationally descended
bounded branch: the whole map is classified directly from its divisors,
not by exponentiating its leading logarithmic symbol.  Combining
(4), (36)--(46) gives the sharp bridge:

> **Radial rational-descent criterion.**  The normalized seven-mode
> comparison branch belongs to \(\Bbbk(z,w)\) if and only if its selected
> Belyi root \(W\) belongs to \(\Bbbk(z,w)\).  When this happens,
> boundedness and symplecticity force \(T\) to be the identity and all
> seven mode parameters to vanish.

Accordingly, the only unresolved step in this route is the field/monodromy
statement in (4).  Interior divisors, endpoint radicals, and the six
resonant coordinate strata require no further coefficient elimination
after that descent is known.

## 7. The sole possible ramification divisor over \(x\ne0\)

The passport localizes the remaining monodromy to one explicit divisor.
Put
\[
\rho=\frac{Q^2}{P^3},\qquad
D=\{Q^2-LP^3=0\},\qquad N=5k+2.
\tag{47}
\]
At a generic component of \(Q=0\), the function \(\rho\) has order two.
The order-two points of the outer cover have local equation
\[
(W-\alpha)^2=\text{unit}\cdot\rho.
\]
After pullback this becomes a unit times \(t^2\), so it splits into
unramified branches.  The simple point \(W=0\) is also unramified.  The
same argument at \(P=0\) cancels the order-three points:
\[
(W-\beta)^3=\text{unit}\cdot\rho^{-1},
\qquad \rho^{-1}\sim t^3.
\]

The only passport point not cancelled by the exponents \(2,3\) in
\(\rho\) is \(W=\infty\) over \(\rho=L\).  There
\[
R(W)-L=\text{unit}\cdot W^{-N}+\cdots.
\]
The divisor \(D\) is generically reduced: away from \(P=Q=0\), the
independence of \(dP,dQ\) makes the gradient of \(Q^2-LP^3\) nonzero.
Hence on the infinity sheet the normalized correspondence has
\[
e_D=N,\qquad \nu_D(W)=-1.
\]
Since \(P,Q\) are units at the generic point of \(D\), the two coordinate
equations give
\[
2\nu_D(Z)+(2k)\nu_D(W)=0,\qquad
3\nu_D(Z)+(3k)\nu_D(W)=0,
\]
and therefore the exact signature
\[
\boxed{\ (e_D,\nu_D(Z),\nu_D(W))=(5k+2,k,-1).\ }
\tag{48}
\]
The tame different exponent is \(N-1\), and (48) saturates the
differential valuation inequality:
\[
5\nu_D(Z)-2\nu_D(W)=N=(N-1)+1.
\]

There is one important affine-boundary qualification.  For the full
case-c support the line \(x=0\) is **not** contracted.  Write
\[
p(y)=P(0,y),\qquad q(y)=Q(0,y).
\]
The required vertices give
\[
\deg p=8,\qquad \deg q=12,
\]
with nonzero leading coefficients \(a,b\).  Consequently, at the generic
point of \(x=0\),
\[
\nu_x(P)=\nu_x(Q)=\nu_x(\rho)=0.
\]
The forced top edge
\[
p_8(z)=a(z-t)^8,\qquad q_{12}(z)=b(z-t)^{12}
\]
does imply the exact asymptotic relation
\[
\lim_{y\to\infty}\frac{q(y)^2}{p(y)^3}
=\frac{b^2}{a^3}=L.
\]
Equivalently, the degree-\(24\) term of \(q^2-Lp^3\) cancels.  This is
contact with the target cusp direction at the unique parameter end
\(y=\infty\); it does **not** prove the much stronger identity
\(q^2=Lp^3\), and hence does not prove
\(F(x=0)\subset C_L\).

The correspondence is generically unramified along \(x=0\) unless the
residue \(q(y)^2/p(y)^3\in\Bbbk(y)\) is identically one of the three branch
values.  If it is identically \(L\), however, \(x=0\) is a component of
\(D\), and the reduced-divisor calculation leading to (48) does not apply
there because \(dP,dQ\) become dependent.  To keep the monodromy argument
independent of this unresolved boundary behavior, remove the line and put
\[
U=\{x\ne0\}\simeq\mathbf G_m\times\mathbf A^1.
\]
There are no other branch values of \(R\).  If type (48) is absent, the
integral closure in the selected component is unramified in codimension
one over \(U\).  Purity makes its restriction a connected finite étale
cover of \(U\).  Every such cover in characteristic zero is Kummer:
\[
u^e=x.
\tag{49}
\]
At the selected radial completion \(t=1/w\), with \(z\) a unit,
\[
x=z^2t,\qquad \nu_t(x)=1.
\]
The chosen formal branch already lies in \(\Bbbk(z)((t))\), whereas a
nontrivial cover (49) has ramification index \(e\) at this valuation.
Hence \(e=1\).  Thus:

> **Single-signature monodromy criterion.**  The selected formal branch
> descends rationally if and only if its component never meets the
> infinity sheet over \(D\), equivalently if it has no divisorial
> valuation of type (48).  The proof is made on \(x\ne0\); the split
> radial completion then rules out the only possible Kummer cover.

Here “type (48)” refers only to components of \(D\cap U\).  A component
supported on \(x=0\) is deliberately outside this statement.

At infinity, \(R-L\) has one contact-\(N\) branch and \(k+1\) simple
branches.  The normalized identity sheet is formally one of the simple
branches.  The remaining geometric problem is therefore concrete:
exclude global analytic continuation along \(D\) from a simple sheet to
the unique infinity sheet.  This is a curve-incidence or monodromy
separation theorem, not a seven-variable coefficient calculation.

## 8. Cusp-complement covering reformulation

There is an equivalent global route to the single-signature problem.
Let
\[
C_L=\{q^2=Lp^3\}\subset\mathbf A^2_{p,q},\qquad
Y=\mathbf A^2\setminus C_L.
\]
The complex fundamental group has the trefoil presentation
\[
\pi_1(Y)=
\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^3\rangle=B_3.
\tag{50}
\]
Its center is generated by \(a^2=b^3\), and the quotient by the center is
\[
C_2*C_3\simeq{\rm PSL}_2(\mathbf Z).
\]
The outer dessin is exactly a transitive permutation representation of
this quotient: the order-two and order-three generators have passports
\[
(2^{3k+1},1),\qquad(3^{2k+1}),
\]
and their product has passport
\[
(5k+2,1^{k+1}).
\tag{51}
\]
For \(k=3\), the five Hurwitz points are the five such normalized
degree-\(21\) representations.

This gives a conditional covering lemma, with a boundary hypothesis that
must not be suppressed.

> **Cusp-complement cover lemma.**  Suppose the nonproper-value set of
> the bounded map \(F\) is contained in \(C_L\), and suppose additionally
> that
> \[
> F(x=0)\subset C_L.
> \tag{52a}
> \]
> Then \(F^{-1}(Y)\subset U\), and
> \[
> F:F^{-1}(Y)\longrightarrow Y
> \]
> is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite étale.  If its peripheral
> \(B_3\)-representation is the fixed outer dessin (51), this cover is
> isomorphic to the corresponding \(F_0\)-cover.  The induced function
> field isomorphism is precisely the rational comparison branch.

Thus an alternative way to prove monodromy descent is to prove both the
boundary containment (52a) and
\[
S_F\subseteq C_L
\tag{53}
\]
for the nonproper-value curve \(S_F\), and to identify the peripheral
representation from the fixed outer edge.  Conversely, any failure of
rational descent must manifest as either an extra asymptotic curve
\(\Lambda\not\subset C_L\), a failure of the boundary containment (52a), or
a change in the peripheral subgroup.

For a full five-block completion, one extra asymptotic curve is visible
before any elimination.  Holding \(w\ne0\) fixed and taking \(z\to0\)
gives \(x=z^2/w\to0,\ y=w/z\to\infty\), while
\[
(P,Q)\longrightarrow(a_0(w),b_0(w)).
\]
Hence \(S_F\) contains the closure of the image of
\((a_0,b_0)\).  Containment of this curve in \(C_L\) is the strong
identity
\[
b_0^2=L a_0^3,
\]
equivalently \(a_0=\alpha h^2,\ b_0=\beta h^3\) with
\(\beta^2=L\alpha^3\).  This has not been derived from the bounded
equations.  Moreover, the full a/b and case-c Newton polygons have generic
mixed volumes \(45\) and \(141\), rather than the outer-model degree
\(21\); equality of the actual covering degree also needs proof.

The normalization of \(D=F^{-1}(C_L)\) makes the two cusp-containment
hypotheses concrete.  On its smooth
part over \(x\ne0\), put
\[
t=\frac{Q}{\sqrt L\,P}.
\]
Then
\[
P=t^2,\qquad Q=\sqrt L\,t^3,
\tag{53}
\]
and \(t:D^\nu\to\mathbf G_m\) is étale wherever the source point remains
finite.  A missing finite nonzero \(t\)-value is exactly an end of
\(D^\nu\) on an extra nonproper curve.  If a radial component has no such
missing values, its map to \(\mathbf G_m\) is finite étale; the split
radial valuation then forces its Kummer degree to be one.

The undeformed model passes this test transparently.  There
\[
Q_0^2-LP_0^3
=\frac{z^6}{w^3}\bigl(wV(w)^2-LU(w)^3\bigr),
\]
and the bracketed polynomial has \(k+1\) simple roots \(\gamma\).  The
components \(w=\gamma\) have
\[
t=\frac{zV(\gamma)}{\sqrt L\,U(\gamma)},
\]
so each radial component maps with degree one and has no missing finite
\(t\)-value.

The nonlinear-shear countermodel fails at exactly this point.  For
\[
F_0=(x^{21},q),\qquad F=(x^{21}+q^2,q),
\]
restriction to (53) gives
\[
x^{21}=t^2(1-Lt^4).
\tag{54}
\]
The four roots of \(1-Lt^4\) are missing finite \(t\)-values at \(x=0\).
Equivalently the model has the extra nonproper curve
\[
\Lambda=\{p=q^2\},
\]
which is not the cusp \(C_L\).  Those four omitted values are precisely
what permits the Kummer monodromy.  Hence the shear is a sharp stress test
for (52), not a counterexample to the cusp-complement cover lemma.

The remaining publishable target is now either:

1. first upgrade the displayed forced-edge limit to the global boundary identity
   \(q^2=Lp^3\), then prove that every other end of the radial component of
   \(D^\nu\) has \(t=0\) or \(t=\infty\); or
2. avoid boundary containment and prove the single-signature criterion
   directly over \(U\); or
3. prove directly that an extra nonproper curve, or a noncuspidal image of
   \(x=0\), creates a forbidden Newton vertex in \(P\) or \(Q\).

The component count at the identity does not itself prove separation.
The exact pullback \(\rho=R(w)+z^{-1}\) has normalized germ
\(W=w+(zR'(w))^{-1}+O(z^{-2})\), while its cusp divisor
\[
z\bigl(wV(w)^2-LU(w)^3\bigr)+U(w)^3=0
\]
is irreducible and contains the \(k+1\) finite branches at \(z=\infty\).
The generic root correspondence is also irreducible, so the same component
contains the contact-\((5k+2)\) infinity sheet.  This is not a bounded
symplectic completion, but it proves that the Newton branches and passport
alone are insufficient.  The full audit is in
`RADIAL_CUSP_COMPLEMENT_AUDIT.md`.
