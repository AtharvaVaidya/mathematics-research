# Fixed-plane residue parity: the remaining conductor theorem

Date: 24 July 2026

This note isolates the exact statement still needed after the formal and
rational conductor calculations in `FIXED_SOURCE_PLANE_ROUTE.md`.

## 1. The residue square class

On
\[
X^\circ=\operatorname {Spec}\mathbf Q[v^{\pm1},c^{\pm1}]
\]
put \(x=v^2\).  Restriction to the conductor
\[
C^\circ:\quad c=v^2/9
\]
sends
\[
r_v\,d\log v+r_c\,d\log c
\longmapsto (r_v+2r_c)\,d\log v.
\]
Its kernel is generated by \(d\log(x/c)\).

For every conductor-adapted monomial Darboux chart constructed in
Section 1.5 of `FIXED_SOURCE_PLANE_ROUTE.md`, the exact boundary-residue
correction gives
\[
[\beta]=[U\,dV-t\,dc]
=\frac{2(2k+1)}3[d\log(x/c)].
\]
After multiplying by \(3/2\), its logarithmic divisor is an odd multiple
of the divisor of \(x/c\).  For this family the mod-two residue invariant
is therefore well-defined and equals
\[
\epsilon(\beta)
=\left[\frac{x}{c}\right]
\in \mathbf Q(v,c)^\times/\mathbf Q(v,c)^{\times2}.
\tag{P1}
\]
It is nontrivial.  In the explicit \(k=1\) model the finite residues are
\[
\operatorname {Res}_{v=0}\beta=4,\qquad
\operatorname {Res}_{c=0}\beta=-2,
\]
and the line at infinity has residue \(-2\).

A polynomial Darboux pair would make \(\beta\) a closed polynomial
one-form, hence an exact polynomial one-form.  Its residue vector and
\(\epsilon(\beta)\) would both be zero.  The desired all-degree theorem
would therefore follow from the following two-part statement.  The first
part is essential: for a completely arbitrary rational Darboux pair, the
residues of \((3/2)\beta\) are not known a priori to be integers, so (P1)
cannot yet be defined.

> **Residue-parity conjecture.** For every rational Darboux pair in the
> fixed-plane pinch fraction field that descends along the conductor and
> satisfies \(\operatorname {Res}_{c=0}(u\,dw)=2/3\):
>
> 1. every logarithmic residue of \((3/2)\beta\) is integral;
> 2. the resulting integral residue divisor is congruent modulo two to
>    \(\operatorname {div}(x/c)\).

The formal conductor identities prove only that \([\beta]\) lies in the
kernel of restriction.  They prove neither quantization nor oddness.

## 2. Why the conductor-fixed condition is essential

A quadratic symplectic extension by itself has no parity obstruction.  In
an arbitrary canonical field \(\mathbf Q(q,p)\), put
\[
U=q^2,\qquad V=\frac{p}{2q}.
\]
Then \(dU\wedge dV=dq\wedge dp\) and
\[
U\,dV-q\,dp=-\frac12d(qp)
\]
is rationally exact.  The involution \(q\mapsto-q,\ p\mapsto p\) fixes
\(U\) but negates \(V\).  Thus this toy model fails exactly the
fixed-subfield condition on the conductor.  The quadratic discriminant
alone is insufficient.

Even requiring both canonical coordinates to be anti-invariant is not
enough without global endpoint regularity.  Take the canonical field
\(\mathbf Q(Q,P)\), the conductor curve
\[
QP=a,
\]
and the involution \((Q,P)\mapsto(-Q,-P)\).  The two functions
\[
U=Q^2,\qquad V=\frac{P}{2Q}
\]
are invariant, symplectic, and satisfy
\[
U\,dV=Q\,dP-d\left(\frac{QP}{2}\right).
\tag{P2}
\]
Parametrize the conductor by \(Q=v,\ P=a/v\) and put \(c=v^2/9\).
Then
\[
U=9c,\qquad V=\frac{a}{18c},\qquad
\operatorname {Res}_{c=0}(U\,dV)=-\frac a2.
\]
Choosing \(a=-4/3\) gives exactly the required residue \(2/3\), while
\[
\operatorname {Res}_{v=0}(Q\,dP)=\frac43,
\]
the same residue as \(t\,dc\) on the fixed-plane conductor.  Nevertheless
(P2) is rationally exact.  Thus the quadratic sign line, the
fixed-subfield condition, and the boundary action together still do not
force a nonzero Liouville class.

The expected \(Q^{-1}\) term is present, but its \(d\log Q\) contribution
to \(U\,dV\) cancels the identical contribution already present in the
source Liouville form \(Q\,dP\).  What fails in this toy model is global
regularity at the two conductor endpoints: both \(P=a/Q\) on the
conductor and \(V=P/(2Q)\) use the anti-invariant coordinate in the
denominator.  Any valid parity theorem must therefore include the divisor
of the fixed source Liouville form and the global polynomial extension
across the endpoints.  A trace/discriminant theorem for the quadratic
residue field alone cannot suffice.

## 3. Elementary postcompositions cannot cancel it

Let \((A,B)\) be the explicit rational model, and put
\[
I=AB,\qquad \kappa=I|_C=-2/3.
\]
The following exact calculations show that the obvious nonmonomial
postcompositions do not remove its residue.

### Polynomial symplectic postcomposition

If \((A',B')=\Psi(A,B)\) for a polynomial plane automorphism of Jacobian
one, then
\[
A'\,dB'-A\,dB
\]
is a closed polynomial one-form on the \((A,B)\)-plane.  It is exact, so
the logarithmic residue class is unchanged.

### Canonical scaling

For a rational function \(f\), the transformation
\[
A'=Af(I),\qquad B'=\frac{B}{f(I)}
\]
is symplectic wherever it is defined, and
\[
A'\,dB'-A\,dB=-I\,d\log f(I).
\tag{P4}
\]
If \(f\) is regular and nonzero at \(\kappa\), then \(I=\kappa+O(v)\)
along \(v=0\), so (P4) has zero \(v=0\) residue.  Changing that residue
requires a zero or pole of \(f\) at \(I=\kappa\), which makes the
postcomposition noninvertible or singular on the conductor itself.

### Elementary shears

For \(B'=B+h(A)\),
\[
A'\,dB'-A\,dB=A h'(A)\,dA.
\]
The only Laurent term of \(h\) that contributes a logarithmic residue on
the boundary hyperbola is \(h_{-1}A^{-1}\).  The same coefficient changes
the forced boundary action, so preserving
\(\operatorname {Res}(u\,dw)=2/3\) forces \(h_{-1}=0\).  The symmetric
statement holds for \(A'=A+g(B)\).

Thus polynomial symplectic maps, regular canonical scalings, and regular
elementary shears preserve the nonzero residue.  Any cancellation must
come from an essentially non-elementary rational symplectic
postcomposition singular at a conductor endpoint, or from a different
function-field subextension rather than a postcomposition of the
degree-two model.

## 4. Why Jung--van der Kulk does not close the gap

The pair sought in the fixed-plane ring cannot be birational: a
birational Keller map of the affine plane is an automorphism and could not
identify the two conductor branches.  Hence a putative pair defines a
proper subfield
\[
\mathbf Q(U,V)\subsetneq\mathbf Q(t,c).
\]
Jung--van der Kulk classifies polynomial automorphisms of a fixed rational
coordinate field; it does not reduce arbitrary generically finite
symplectic subfields to the degree-two monomial model.  This is precisely
the nonmonomial gap in the residue-parity conjecture.

Geometrically, the conductor valuation has residue-field degree two:
\[
\mathbf Q(c)\subset\mathbf Q(v),\qquad v^2=9c.
\]
The two endpoints \(c=0,\infty\) are the ramification points of this
quadratic residue extension.  Its trace matrix in the basis \((1,v)\) is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&18c\end{pmatrix},
\]
with discriminant \(36c\).  This sign/discriminant line explains the odd
class in the monomial models, but the exact toy model above proves that it
does not determine the Liouville class by itself.

The remaining theorem must compare this sign line with the **fixed global
Liouville divisor** and prove that a descended pair regular on the affine
source cannot realize the endpoint cancellation available in the toy
model.  That global endpoint-regularity step is not yet proved.
