# Collision conductor, residual pullback, and the exact global gap

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

This is a **no-go result for collision-only global arguments**, not
an exclusion theorem for polynomial Keller completions.

Let \(d\in\mathbf C[x]\) be monic of degree \(g\geq1\), let
\(u\in\mathbf C^*\), and put
\[
 \gamma(x)=(p(x),q(x))
 =\bigl(d(x)^2+ux,d(x)^3\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
Let \(C\) be the reduced image curve and let \(H(U,V)\) be its
implicit equation, normalized to be monic in \(U\).  This note proves
an exact conductor form of the cube-root collision construction.

Fix a primitive cube root \(\zeta\), and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \phi_\eta(x)&=x+\frac{1-\eta^2}{u}d(x)^2,\\
 K_\eta(x)&=
 \frac{d(\phi_\eta(x))-\eta d(x)}{d(x)}
 \qquad(\eta=\zeta,\zeta^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Then the normalization of \(C\) is \(\mathbb A^1_x\), its affine
conductor pulled back to the normalization is generated by
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \kappa(x)=\frac{u^{3g-1}}3K_\zeta(x)K_{\zeta^2}(x).
 \;}
\tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \deg\kappa=4g^2-2g.
\tag{4}
\]
Its zero divisor is exactly the collision divisor on the source
normalization, with conductor multiplicities.

Now suppose, hypothetically, that a polynomial Keller map
\[
 F=(P,Q):\mathbb A^2_{x,y}\longrightarrow\mathbb A^2_{U,V},
 \qquad J(P,Q)=1,
\tag{5}
\]
satisfies \(F(x,0)=\gamma(x)\).  There is a polynomial \(R\) such that
\[
 H(P,Q)=yR,
\tag{6}
\]
and the complete boundary value of the residual factor is
\[
 \boxed{\;R(x,0)=-\kappa(x).\;}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the line \(y=0\) meets the residual pullback in a scheme of
length \(4g^2-2g\), and the two explicit cube-root collision
polynomials account for every one of those intersections.

This is the strongest consequence of the collision data alone.  It
does **not** give a global contradiction.  The normalization of the
residual pullback is an étale quasi-finite curve over
\(\mathbb A^1\).  A proper component is merely another polynomial
lift of \(\gamma\); a nonproper component can delete points over
finite normalization values.  Neither alternative contradicts
étaleness, one-place-at-infinity geometry, or injectivity-on-a-line
results.  Consequently the tempting step

\[
 \text{“one place at infinity”}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{“the residual cover is finite and impossible”}
\]

is false twice: finiteness is not forced, and a finite component is
not impossible.  Any successful global obstruction must additionally
control the full Keller correspondence or the finite asymptotic
values of its residual components.

## 1. Birationality and the implicit resultant

The three degrees
\[
 \deg p=2g,\qquad \deg q=3g,\qquad
 \deg(p^3-q^2)=4g+1
\tag{8}
\]
force the parametrization to be birational.  Indeed, polynomial
Lüroth gives a polynomial \(h\) such that
\[
 \mathbf C(p,q)=\mathbf C(h).
\]
Its degree divides the degree of every nonconstant element of
\(\mathbf C[p,q]\).  Therefore it divides \(2g\), \(3g\), and
\(4g+1\), hence it is one.

It follows that \(\mathbf C[x]\) is the normalization of
\(\mathbf C[p,q]\).  Finiteness is immediate because \(x\) is
integral over \(\mathbf C[q]\): it satisfies the monic equation
\(d(T)^3-q=0\).

The implicit equation has the exact resultant presentation
\[
 H(U,V)=
 \operatorname{Res}_T
 \bigl(V-d(T)^3,\ U-d(T)^2-uT\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]
The birationality just proved shows that this resultant is not a
proper power.  It is irreducible, monic of \(U\)-degree \(3g\), and
has total degree \(3g\).  Projectively, \(C\) has one place at
infinity: the degree-\(3g\) homogenized parametrization maps the
single point \(x=\infty\) to \([0:1:0]\).

The affine normalization is unramified.  In fact,
\[
 p'=2dd'+u,\qquad q'=3d^2d',
\tag{10}
\]
and a zero of \(q'\) is a zero of either \(d\) or \(d'\), at which
\(p'=u\).  Hence
\[
 \gcd(p',q')=1.
\tag{11}
\]
All affine branches of \(C\) are therefore smooth; its affine
singularities arise only by identification or tangential contact of
distinct normalization points.

## 2. Exact resultant derivative and conductor factorization

For generic \(x\), specialize (9) at \(V=q(x)\).  Since
\[
 d(T)^3-d(x)^3
 =\prod_{\eta^3=1}\bigl(d(T)-\eta d(x)\bigr),
\]
the roots contributing to the resultant split into three groups.
Differentiating the factor corresponding to the root \(T=x\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H_U(\gamma(x))
 &=u^{g-1}d'(x)\,
   u^g\bigl(d(\phi_\zeta(x))-\zeta d(x)\bigr)\\
 &\qquad\qquad\cdot
   u^g\bigl(d(\phi_{\zeta^2}(x))-\zeta^2 d(x)\bigr)\\
 &=u^{3g-1}d'(x)d(x)^2
   K_\zeta(x)K_{\zeta^2}(x).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Both sides are polynomials, so the generic computation is a
polynomial identity.  Dividing by \(q'=3d^2d'\) gives
\[
 H_U(\gamma)=\kappa q'.
\tag{13}
\]
Differentiating \(H(\gamma)=0\), and using (11), gives the companion
identity
\[
 H_V(\gamma)=-\kappa p'.
\tag{14}
\]

The quotient in (2) is a polynomial because
\(\phi_\eta\equiv x\pmod d\).  Its degree is
\[
 \deg K_\eta=2g^2-g.
\tag{15}
\]
The local calculation at any root of \(d\) shows that
\(\gcd(K_\eta,d)=1\), so no root introduced by dividing by \(d\) is
a genuine collision.

Equations (13)--(14) also identify \(\kappa\) intrinsically.  In the
completed local ring at an affine image point, write the reduced
equation as a product of its smooth branch equations.  Along one
normalization branch, the primitive normal derivative of that
product is the product of all the other branch equations.  Its
order is the sum of the pairwise intersection multiplicities, which
is precisely the conductor exponent of that branch.  Because
\((p',q')\) is primitive by (11), (13)--(14) say that this primitive
normal derivative is \(\kappa\).  Thus the conductor ideal in
\(\mathbf C[x]\) is \((\kappa)\).

There is also an elementary set-theoretic reading.  If
\(\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)\) and \(a\ne b\), then
\[
 d(b)=\eta d(a)
\]
for a nontrivial cube root \(\eta\); the case \(d(a)=0\) would force
\(a=b\).  The first coordinate then forces
\[
 b=\phi_\eta(a),\qquad K_\eta(a)=0.
\tag{16}
\]
Conversely every root of \(K_\eta\) gives exactly such a distinct
partner.  Formula (3) strengthens this bijection to the full
scheme-theoretic collision divisor, including tangencies and
multiple collisions.

## 3. Pullback-gradient identity

Equation \(H(P(x,0),Q(x,0))=0\) gives the factorization (6).
Differentiate it in \(y\) and restrict to \(y=0\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R(x,0)
 &=H_U(\gamma)P_y(x,0)+H_V(\gamma)Q_y(x,0)\\
 &=\kappa\bigl(q'P_y-p'Q_y\bigr)\\
 &=-\kappa J(P,Q)(x,0)\\
 &=-\kappa.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
This proves (7).

Since \(F\) is étale and \(H=0\) is reduced, its pullback
\(H(P,Q)=0\) is reduced.  Hence \(yR\) is squarefree.  In
particular \(R\) is squarefree and has no common component with
\(y\).  Notice that \(R(x,0)\) can nevertheless have multiple
roots: those record tangency between the two reduced divisors, not a
multiple component of \(R=0\).

The formula accounts exactly for all affine boundary intersections:
\[
 I\bigl(V(y),V(R)\bigr)
 =\dim_{\mathbf C}\frac{\mathbf C[x]}{(\kappa)}
 =4g^2-2g.
\tag{18}
\]

There is also a useful global Hamiltonian form.  Put
\[
 f=H(P,Q)=yR,\qquad
 \mathcal D=J(f,\,\cdot\,).
\]
The full equation \(J(P,Q)=1\), not merely its boundary restriction,
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D(P)&=-H_V(P,Q),&
 \mathcal D(Q)&=H_U(P,Q),\\
 \mathcal D(y)&=yR_x,&
 \mathcal D(R)&=-RR_x.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18a}
\]
Thus \(y\) and \(R\) are global Darboux polynomials with opposite
cofactors.  On the boundary,
\[
 \mathcal D(x)=-f_y=\kappa.
\tag{18b}
\]
Equivalently, the target dualizing differential pulls back on the
normalization line to \(dx/\kappa\).  These identities incorporate
the global symplectic equation, but they supply no sign, positivity,
or degree bound on the residual components: the opposite cofactors
in (18a) are automatic for the product \(f=yR\), not a new first
integral transverse to its zero fiber.

## 4. Normalization of the residual correspondence

Introduce the normalization fiber product
\[
 E=\left\{
 P(x,y)=p(t),\quad Q(x,y)=q(t)
 \right\}\subset\mathbb A^3_{x,y,t}.
\tag{19}
\]
Projection
\[
 \pi:E\longrightarrow\mathbb A^1_t
\tag{20}
\]
is étale: the relative Jacobian of the two equations in (19) with
respect to \(x,y\) is \(J(P,Q)=1\).  Base change of the finite
normalization \(\mathbb A^1_t\to C\) gives a finite map
\[
 E\longrightarrow V(H(P,Q)).
\tag{21}
\]
It is the normalization of the reduced pullback divisor.

The diagonal section
\[
 E_{\rm diag}=\{(x,y,t)=(t,0,t)\}
\tag{22}
\]
is both open and closed in \(E\): a section of an étale morphism is
open, and a section of a separated morphism is closed.  Hence
\[
 E=E_{\rm diag}\sqcup E_{\rm res}.
\tag{23}
\]
If \(\gamma(a)=\gamma(b)\) with \(a\ne b\), then
\[
 (a,0,b)\in E_{\rm res}.
\tag{24}
\]
Thus the cross-lifts of all roots of the conductor occur on the
residual normalization.

For every irreducible component \(\Gamma\) of \(E_{\rm res}\),
\[
 \pi_\Gamma:\Gamma\to\mathbb A^1_t
\tag{25}
\]
is an étale quasi-finite morphism of smooth curves.  Zariski's main
theorem gives an open immersion
\[
 \Gamma\hookrightarrow\overline\Gamma
\]
with \(\overline\Gamma\to\mathbb A^1\) finite.

There are exactly two global possibilities.

1. If \(\pi_\Gamma\) is proper, it is finite étale.  A connected
   finite étale cover of \(\mathbb A^1_\mathbf C\) is trivial, so
   \(\Gamma\simeq\mathbb A^1\) and \(t\) is its coordinate.  This
   only gives a polynomial normalization lift
   \(\sigma(t)\) satisfying
   \[
   F(\sigma(t))=\gamma(t).
   \tag{26}
   \]
   Such a second sheet is expected for a noninjective étale map and
   is not a contradiction.
2. If \(\pi_\Gamma\) is not proper, then
   \(\overline\Gamma\setminus\Gamma\) contains a point over a finite
   value \(t_0\).  Along that deleted branch, at least one of the
   source coordinates \(x,y\) tends to infinity while
   \[
   F(x,y)\longrightarrow\gamma(t_0).
   \tag{27}
   \]
   Thus \(\gamma(t_0)\) is a finite asymptotic value of \(F\).

The one place of \(C\) at projective infinity controls \(t=\infty\);
it says nothing that excludes the deleted finite values in the
second alternative.

## 5. What the global nonproper-value set does and does not add

Let \(S_F\) be the nonproper-value set of the hypothetical Keller
map.  The boundary collision makes \(F\) noninvertible.  Hence
\(S_F\ne\varnothing\): otherwise \(F\) would be finite étale over
\(\mathbb A^2\), and therefore an automorphism.

There are two exact cases.

* If \(C\subset S_F\), then irreducibility makes \(C\) a component
  of \(S_F\).  The qualitative Chau--Jelonek restrictions do not
  exclude it.  The curve \(C\) is polynomially parametrized, has
  normalization \(\mathbb A^1\), is singular because of the
  collisions, and has one point at infinity.  These are precisely
  the permitted qualitative features of a nonproper-value
  component.  Degree-ratio restrictions may exclude this case after
  the ambient degrees of \(P,Q\) are fixed incompatibly, but an
  arbitrary completion has no such incompatible degree hypothesis.
* If \(C\not\subset S_F\), then \(C\cap S_F\) is finite.  Away from
  this finite set, the restriction of \(F\) over \(C\) is part of
  the standard finite étale complement cover.  This supplies
  properness only after puncturing the normalization and does not
  eliminate either the diagonal sheet or additional residual
  sheets.  Every finite deleted value in (27) lies in
  \(C\cap S_F\).

Thus the standard global theorem gives a clean dichotomy but no
contradiction.  The first case is numerically and topologically
admissible.  In the second, punctures allow nontrivial étale covers,
and the finite residual lifts from (26) remain admissible even if all
punctures could be removed.

This also explains why the affine fiber product (19) cannot by
itself detect a whole dicritical component at infinity.  Such a
component represents sheets absent from the generic affine fiber;
it need not be the completion of an existing affine component of
\(E_{\rm res}\).  Zariski's-main factorization for the components
that do exist must not be used to rule out generic sheet loss.

## 6. Hostile audit of the tempting global shortcuts

### Quasi-finite is not finite, even when surjective

The elementary map
\[
 s\longmapsto t=s^3-3s
\tag{28}
\]
from
\(\mathbb A^1_s\setminus\{1,-1\}\) to \(\mathbb A^1_t\) is étale:
its derivative \(3(s^2-1)\) is a unit on the source.  It is
surjective, because the deleted critical values retain the other
preimages
\[
 t=-2:\ s=-2,\qquad t=2:\ s=2.
\]
It is not finite.  Therefore neither étaleness nor surjectivity of a
residual component supplies the missing properness.

### Finite is not impossible

If a residual component is finite, (26) is simply another sheet
over the normalization.  Its image in the source may meet \(y=0\)
at the cross-lifts (24), and different \(t\)-labels keep the
normalization points separate.  There is no resulting line on which
\(F\) is injective.  In fact the distinguished line itself is
noninjective by construction.

The explicit nodal model in
`NONEQUIVARIANT_NODAL_PULLBACK_STRUCTURE_AND_LOCAL_MODEL.md` realizes
a finite étale residual normalization, crosswise conductor gluing,
and all corresponding transverse intersections inside a polynomial
map that is étale on a neighborhood of the full pullback divisor.
Its Jacobian is not globally constant, which pinpoints the genuinely
global information absent from the divisor argument.

This is not merely an analogy with an unrelated curve.  When \(g=1\),
write \(d=x+a\) and
\[
 T=d+\frac u2.
\]
After affine target changes, (1) becomes
\[
 \left(T^2,\ T^3+\frac{3u^2}{4}T\right).
\tag{29}
\]
Since \(u\ne0\), a constant rescaling over \(\mathbf C\) turns (29)
into the nodal parametrization \((t^2,t^3-t)\) used in that note.
Thus the exact local-algebraic countermodel already applies, up to
affine changes, to the degree-one member of the present family.  Any
purported all-\(d\) contradiction based only on conductor pullback,
finite residual normalization, or crosswise node gluing is therefore
formally refuted in the first nontrivial case.

### One place at infinity does not remove finite asymptotic values

The unique infinity point of the target curve is relevant only to
the completion of \(\mathbb A^1_t\) at \(t=\infty\).  The missing
points in Zariski's-main factorization lie over finite \(t_0\), and
are exactly the finite asymptotic phenomena in (27).  Conflating
these two boundaries is the main false step in the proposed
one-place argument.

## 7. Exact remaining global problem

The conductor calculation closes the finite boundary bookkeeping:
there are no unaccounted residual intersections to discover.
However, even a theorem excluding every finite asymptotic value
along \(C\) would leave the proper alternative (26), which is not
contradictory by itself.

Therefore the missing statement is stronger than a sole
properness-at-infinity lemma.  One must prove a genuinely global
restriction such as:

* the residual Keller correspondence admits neither an additional
  polynomial normalization lift nor a deleted finite branch; or
* the full equation \(J(P,Q)=1\), away from the pullback divisor,
  forces one of those two alternatives to violate global degree,
  monodromy, or the structure of the nonproper-value set.

No such theorem is proved here.  The exact new theorem is (3) and
(7): the explicit collision polynomial is the complete conductor
and the complete residual boundary trace.  The global component
step remains open and cannot be replaced by quasi-finite/finite or
one-place shortcuts.
