# Arbitrary nonhomogeneous targets through degree nine

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

Let \(F=(A,B,C)\) be the exact Gallagher weighted lift, and let \(U\)
be its exact second-subduction coordinate.  On every polynomial graph,
for every nonconstant target polynomial \(Q(A,B,C)\) of target degree
at most nine and every first-coordinate perturbation \(R_{\le2}\),
\[
\boxed{
J_{x,y}\bigl(U+R_{\le2},Q\bigr)\notin\mathbf C^\times.
}
\tag{1}
\]

This strengthens the degree-eight theorem in
`WEIGHTED_LIFT_NONHOMOGENEOUS_NEWTON_VERTEX_CLOSURE.md`.
The new point is that the sole degree-nine failure of the primitive
Newton-vertex criterion can be eliminated exactly using the already
known first cusp subduction \(T\).  No completion after \(T\), and no
coefficient search, is needed.

This is a theorem about polynomial-graph restrictions of this
three-dimensional Keller map.  It is not a proof of the plane
Jacobian conjecture.

## 1. The only collision through degree nine

For a target monomial \(A^aB^bC^c\), put
\[
\beta=-2a-b+c,\qquad h=6a+5b,\qquad q=h+\beta.
\tag{2}
\]
The kernel of \((a,b,c)\mapsto(\beta,h)\), equivalently of
\((a,b,c)\mapsto(\beta,q)\), is generated by
\[
(5,-6,4).
\tag{3}
\]
If two nonnegative triples differing by (3) both have target degree
at most nine, the triple on the negative-\(B\) side has degree at
least six, while the other has degree larger by three.  Equality in
both bounds is forced.  Thus the only pair is
\[
B^6,\qquad A^5C^4.
\tag{4}
\]

Write
\[
E=p_5^6A^5C^4-q_6^5B^6,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(p_5,q_6\ne0\) are the exact seed coefficients.  The maximal
first denominator-slice subduction is
\[
\begin{aligned}
T={}&E+c_1A^4BC^3+c_2A^3B^2C^2\\
&\quad+c_3A^2B^3C+c_4AB^4.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The exact constants \(c_i\) are recorded and verified in
`WEIGHTED_LIFT_FIRST_NONLINEAR_CUSP_SUBDUCTION.md`.

Given an arbitrary \(Q\) of degree at most nine, first discard its
constant term, which does not affect the Jacobian.  Then use (6) once
to eliminate its \(A^5C^4\)-coefficient.  This rewrites \(Q\)
exactly as
\[
Q=\mu T+\sum\lambda_{abc}A^aB^bC^c,
\tag{7}
\]
where \(A^5C^4\) is absent from the ordinary-monomial support.
Some adjusted coefficients in (7) may vanish; this causes no problem.
By (4), all remaining ordinary monomials have distinct
\((\beta,h)\) and distinct \((\beta,q)\).

## 2. \(T\) is an isolated augmented Newton atom

At a regular source boundary, the exact leading template of \(T\) is
\[
T=x^{-6}\left(\lambda_0u^{25}f^{19}
+ \text{terms lower by at least }L+1\right),
\qquad \lambda_0\ne0,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(L=\deg f\ge1\).  Thus its augmented invariants are
\[
(\beta_T,h_T,q_T)=(-6,25,19).
\tag{9}
\]
There is no ordinary target monomial with these invariants: the
equation
\[
6a+5b=25,\qquad c=-6+2a+b\ge0
\tag{10}
\]
has no nonnegative solution.

The exact numerator of \(T\), before division by \(x^6\gamma^6\),
has top monomial \(u^{25}\gamma^{25}\).  If another numerator
monomial is \(u^i\gamma^{i+j}\), its losses relative to (8) are
\[
25-i\ge1,\qquad25-i-j\ge1.
\tag{11}
\]
After \(\gamma=f\), its degree loss is therefore at least \(L+1\).
At a pole boundary \(\rho<0\), (11) says its \(\gamma\)-power after
division is strictly below \(q_T=19\), so it has strictly later
\(x\)-order.

Consequently \(T\) satisfies the same exact top-and-gap hypotheses as
an ordinary Newton atom.  It collides with no ordinary atom in (7):
equality of \((\beta,h)\) gives (10), and equality of \((E,q)\), where
\(E=\beta+\rho q\), gives the same equations.

## 3. Regular and polar closure

At a regular boundary, the top coefficient of
\(J(U,T)\) is a nonzero scalar times
\[
-5(25+19L)+6(20+17L)=7L-5.
\tag{12}
\]
This never vanishes for an integer \(L\ge1\).  More intrinsically,
the boundary Wronskian contains
\[
u^{44}f^{35}\bigl(-5f+7uf'\bigr),
\tag{13}
\]
and the polynomial in parentheses cannot vanish for a nonzero
polynomial \(f\).

Now apply the regular Newton-vertex proof to the augmented support
(7).  Every aggregate vertex is a singleton: ordinary-ordinary
collisions were removed in Section 1, and \(T\) is isolated by
Section 2.  If the earliest atom is ordinary, the existing theorem
applies.  If it is \(T\), (12) applies.  In either case the regular
boundary coefficient is nonzero.

For a pole boundary, use
\[
E=\beta+\rho q,\qquad \alpha=-5+17\rho.
\tag{14}
\]
The two-endpoint proof from the earlier Newton-vertex theorem depends
only on an atom's exact \((E,q)\) top and the fact that all non-top
terms have later \(x\)-order.  Section 2 proves both properties for
\(T\).  Every \((E,q)\)-aggregate in (7) is again a singleton.
The least-\(q\) order at \(u=1\), or if its characteristic coefficient
vanishes the greatest-\(q\) degree at infinity, therefore gives a
nonzero coefficient exactly as before.

Finally, every monomial of \(R_{\le2}\) is later than \(U\) at both
regular and pole boundaries, by the inequalities already proved for
the degree-eight theorem.  It cannot enter the isolated earliest
Jacobian coefficient.  This proves (1).

## 4. Scope

The argument uses one exact rewriting by \(T\); it does not claim a
terminating SAGBI basis.  In degree ten, multiples of the primitive
collision and interactions with \(CT\sim B^5\) require a new analysis.
That analysis is carried out in
`WEIGHTED_LIFT_NONHOMOGENEOUS_DEGREE_TEN_NEWTON_BASIS_CLOSURE.md`,
using the exact augmented atoms \(T,AT,W,U\).  The separate cubic-graph
audit shows that the later \(V\)-branch is genuinely new on every cubic
boundary polynomial, but neither later result is needed for the
degree-nine theorem.

The accompanying exact verifier is
`verify_weighted_lift_nonhomogeneous_degree_nine_cusp_closure.py`.
