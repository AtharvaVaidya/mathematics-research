# Route A: quartic transposition monodromy forbids four-punctured coordinate fibers

Date: 26 July 2026

## Outcome

Assume the one-boundary, one-deficit quartic survivor from
`ROUTE_A_DEGREE_FOUR_RETAINED_SHEET_AUDIT.md`.  Thus the reduced branch
curve \(\Delta\) is irreducible, its normalization is
\[
\nu:\mathbf A^1_t\longrightarrow\Delta,
\]
generic inertia is a transposition, there is exactly one deficient
unibranch value with local orbit partition \(3+1\), and every
multibranch value has local orbit partition \(2+2\).

If a geometric generic coordinate fiber is rational, its smooth
completion cannot have exactly four punctures.  More precisely,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\overline {P^{-1}(\eta)}\simeq\mathbf P^1
&\Longrightarrow
\#\bigl(\overline {P^{-1}(\eta)}\setminus P^{-1}(\eta)\bigr)\ge6,\\
\overline {Q^{-1}(\eta)}\simeq\mathbf P^1
&\Longrightarrow
\#\bigl(\overline {Q^{-1}(\eta)}\setminus Q^{-1}(\eta)\bigr)\ge6.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]

The previous Riemann--Hurwitz theorem gave the lower bound four and,
in the one-boundary case, the exact formula
\[
s_P=2k_P+2,\qquad
\deg(P\circ\nu)=k_P+2.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus equality \(s_P=4\) would force \(\deg(P\circ\nu)=3\).
The new input is a cubic-projection dichotomy.

At the unique \(3+1\) point, the cubic polynomial \(P\circ\nu\) is
critical.  If it is totally ramified there, the cusp captures all three
points of a nearby vertical branch fiber, so its nontransitive local
monodromy generates the global image.  Otherwise the cubic has one
other critical point.  At the image of that point:

1. a smooth branch germ identifies two vertical meridians;
2. a nondeficient unibranch germ gives the same transposition on those
   two meridians; or
3. a multibranch germ captures all three vertical branch points and
   has local image \(2+2\).

In the first two cases the global quartic image is generated by at
most two transpositions; in the third it is a quotient of a
nontransitive local image.  None can act transitively on four sheets.

This removes the entire four-puncture equality case in the
one-boundary survivor.  It does **not** remove rational fibers with six
or more punctures, positive-genus fibers, or the two-boundary
zero-deficit survivor.

## 1. A full-degree local-to-global lemma

Let \(C\subset\mathbf A^2_{x,y}\) be an irreducible reduced curve such
that projection to \(x\) is finite of degree \(n\).  Let
\[
\mu:\widetilde C\longrightarrow C
\]
be its normalization.  Suppose \(q\in C\) lies above \(x=x_0\), and
that the entire divisor of \(x-x_0\) on \(\widetilde C\) is supported
over \(q\).  Equivalently, all \(n\) points of a nearby regular
vertical fiber enter one Milnor ball \(B\) about \(q\).

Affine Zariski--van Kampen says that meridians about the \(n\) points
of a regular vertical fiber generate
\[
\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus C).
\tag{3}
\]
Choose the regular fiber sufficiently close to \(x_0\).  All of its
curve points and a geometric meridian basis lie in \(B\).  Therefore
the natural map
\[
\pi_1(B\setminus C)\longrightarrow
\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus C)
\tag{4}
\]
is onto.

Consequently every finite-cover monodromy representation of the
global complement has exactly the same image as its restriction to
the local complement.  In particular, if the local image is
nontransitive, so is the global image.

Only the support condition is essential here.  The germ need not be
an \(A_2\) cusp.  For example, a \((3,4)\) cusp can itself have a
transitive \(S_4\) transposition image; what obstructs the present
quartic survivor is the prescribed local orbit partition \(3+1\), not
unibranchness alone.

## 2. The cubic critical-point dichotomy

Let
\[
p(t)=P\circ\nu(t)
\]
have degree three, and let \(t_0\) normalize the unique deficient
unibranch point \(q_0\).  The branch germ at \(q_0\) is singular:
otherwise its local complement would be cyclic, generated by the
generic transposition, and its local orbit partition would remain
\(2+1+1\), not \(3+1\).  Hence the normalization is not immersive at
\(t_0\), so
\[
p'(t_0)=0.
\tag{5}
\]

There are two possibilities.

### 2.1 Total ramification at the deficient point

If \(p'\) has a double zero at \(t_0\), then
\[
p(t)-p(t_0)=c(t-t_0)^3,\qquad c\ne0.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(q_0\) supports the entire degree-three vertical divisor.
Section 1 makes its local group surject onto the global complement
group.  The local quartic monodromy has orbit partition \(3+1\), so
the global monodromy is nontransitive.

This is the precise general form of the earlier full-degree cusp
obstruction.  No Puiseux type or braid presentation is required.

### 2.2 A second simple critical point

Otherwise
\[
p'(t)=3c(t-t_0)(t-t_1),\qquad t_1\ne t_0.
\tag{7}
\]
The two critical values are distinct.  Indeed,
\[
p(t_0)-p(t_1)
=\int_{t_1}^{t_0}3c(t-t_0)(t-t_1)\,dt
=-\frac c2(t_0-t_1)^3\ne0.
\tag{8}
\]
Put \(q_1=\nu(t_1)\).  We distinguish the complete list of local
possibilities.

#### Smooth image

Suppose \(q_1\) is a smooth point of \(C\), with no other normalization
point above it.  The restriction of \(p\) to the branch has simple
ramification at \(t_1\).  A nearby vertical line therefore meets the
germ in two points.  Both meridians map to the same generator of
\[
\pi_1(B_1\setminus C)\simeq\mathbf Z.
\tag{9}
\]

Together with the third vertical meridian, these three meridians
generate the global complement group by (3).  Under a representation
sending smooth meridians to transpositions, its image is consequently
generated by at most two transpositions.  Two transpositions in
\(S_4\) generate either \(C_2\), a Klein four subgroup on two disjoint
edges, or an \(S_3\) on three letters.  None is transitive on four
letters.

#### Unibranch singular image

Suppose \(q_1\) is singular and unibranch.  The total orbit deficit is
one and has already occurred at \(q_0\).  Hence the local monodromy at
\(q_1\) has the generic orbit partition
\[
2+1+1.
\tag{10}
\]
It contains a smooth-branch transposition.  A permutation subgroup
with orbit partition (10) therefore fixes the two singleton sheets and
is the unique \(C_2\) on the two-element orbit.  The two nearby
vertical meridians at \(t_1\) have the same transposition image.

The global image is again generated by that transposition and the
image of the third vertical meridian, hence by at most two
transpositions.  It is nontransitive.

#### Multibranch image

Suppose \(q_1\) has more than one analytic branch.  The branch through
\(\nu(t_1)\) contributes intersection multiplicity two to the vertical
fiber.  Since the global vertical degree is three, precisely one other
normalization branch lies over \(q_1\), with multiplicity one.  Thus
all three points of a nearby vertical fiber enter one Milnor ball
about \(q_1\).

Section 1 again gives a local-to-global epimorphism.  The reviewed
quartic collapse theorem says that every multibranch value has two
branches, two disjoint transposition meridians, and local orbit
partition \(2+2\).  Its image is nontransitive, so the global image is
nontransitive.

These cases exhaust the image of \(t_1\).  Therefore a branch
normalization with a degree-three coordinate cannot support connected
quartic transposition monodromy under the one-deficit survivor
hypotheses.

## 3. Application to the four-puncture equality case

Assume the geometric generic \(P\)-fiber is rational and has four
punctures.  The one-boundary Riemann--Hurwitz identities (2) give
\[
k_P=1,\qquad \deg(P\circ\nu)=3.
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(P\circ\nu\) is a nonconstant polynomial, the map
\(\Delta\to\mathbf A^1_P\) is finite.  Its generic degree is three:
the normalization is birational, and adjoining the normalization
does not change the function field.  Thus a generic \(P\)-line
contains exactly the three branch points used in Sections 1--2.

The cubic dichotomy contradicts connected quartic monodromy in every
case.  Hence \(s_P\ne4\).  Formula (2) makes \(s_P\) even, so
\[
s_P\ge6.
\]
Interchanging \(P\) and \(Q\) proves the second assertion in (1).

## 4. A sharp escape from the old lemma, closed by the new one

The polynomial normalization
\[
t\longmapsto
\bigl(t^2-t^3,\ t^3+t^4\bigr)
\tag{12}
\]
is a rational quartic with one \(A_2\) cusp and two ordinary nodes.
Its first coordinate has degree three, but the cusp at \(t=0\) is not
full degree:
\[
t^2-t^3=t^2(1-t).
\tag{13}
\]
Two vertical points enter the cusp, while the third tends to \(t=1\).
Thus the full-degree lemma alone does not classify the minimal profile.

The other critical point is \(t=2/3\), and
\[
\frac d{dt}(t^3+t^4)\bigg|_{t=2/3}\ne0.
\tag{14}
\]
Its image is smooth, so the first case of Section 2.2 applies.  The
curve is therefore an exact stress test: it escapes the old local
surjectivity argument, but the second critical point reduces every
transposition image to one generated by at most two transpositions.

Its implicit equation is
\[
-x^4+2x^3+10x^2y+7xy^2+y^3-2y^2=0,
\tag{15}
\]
and its vertical discriminant is
\[
-x^3(27x-4)(x^2-4x-4)^2.
\tag{16}
\]
The factors record respectively the cusp, the smooth vertical
tangency, and the two nodes.  This example confirms that the new
argument uses the complete cubic critical divisor rather than a
special trigonal normal form.
