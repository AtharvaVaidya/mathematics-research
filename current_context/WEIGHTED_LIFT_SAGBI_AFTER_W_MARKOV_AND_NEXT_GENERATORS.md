# The toric kernel after \(W\), with the graph projection restored

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

There are two distinct statements in this audit, and they must not be
conflated.

First, for the formal three-component boundary vectors
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_C&=(1,0,1),&
v_B&=(-1,5,4),&
v_A&=(-2,6,4),\\
v_T&=(-6,25,19),&
v_U&=(-5,20,17),&
v_W&=(-7,25,20),
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
the integer kernel and a minimal Markov basis are computed exactly.
The cheapest relation involving \(W\),
\[
CW\sim BU,
\tag{2}
\]
subducts to zero.  The next relation
\[
B^4W\sim TU
\tag{3}
\]
produces a formal new frontier
\[
v_V=(-11,40,33).
\tag{4}
\]

Second, a graph of degree \(L\) uses only the projected valuation
\[
\pi_L(x,i,j)=(x,i+Lj).
\tag{5}
\]
Under this actual valuation, (4) is outside the projected old
semigroup for \(L=2\) and every \(L\ge4\), but **not** for \(L=3\).
At \(L=3\) it has precisely the two factorizations
\[
CA^3T,\qquad B^5A^3.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus \(V\) is a valid witness that the displayed basis is incomplete
for \(L=2\) and \(L\ge4\), but no such conclusion follows at \(L=3\).

After adjoining the displayed polynomial \(V\), the exact relation
\(CV\sim U^2\) subducts to zero.  The relation \(UW\sim BV\) produces
an element \(X\) with formal vector
\[
v_X=(-12,45,35).
\tag{7}
\]
Its projection lies outside the projected semigroup generated by
\(C,B,A,T,U,W,V\) for **every** integer \(L\ge2\).  Therefore \(X\)
is a rigorous witness that this displayed set is incomplete.  This
does not say that every SAGBI completion must choose this particular
polynomial as a generator.

For \(L=1\), the tied frontier of \(W\) changes its leading
coefficient.  The specialized relation (2) immediately leaves the
new projected value \((-6,45)\), so the linear-graph branch is
separate.

Nothing here proves that the completion is infinite or that it
terminates after \(X\).

## 1. Formal three-component toric theorem

In the order \(C,B,A,T,U,W\), a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of the kernel of
the matrix with columns (1) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(4,-6,5,0,0,0),\qquad
(1,-5,0,1,0,0),\qquad
(1,-1,0,0,-1,1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Writing lower-case letters for the initial monomials, one minimal
Markov basis is
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
b^5&-&ct,\\
bu&-&cw,\\
c^3a^5&-&bt,\\
b^4w&-&tu,\\
c^2b^4a^5&-&t^2,\\
c^2a^5u&-&tw,\\
ca^5u^3&-&b^3w^3,\\
a^5u^4&-&b^2w^4.
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]

For an exact algebraic proof, start from the lattice-basis ideal
\[
(c^4a^5-b^6,\ ct-b^5,\ cw-bu).
\tag{10}
\]
Its saturation by \(cbatuw\) is the toric ideal.  Exact
lexicographic elimination of the saturation variable gives the same
ideal as (9).  Removing any one of the eight displayed binomials
leaves it outside the ideal of the other seven, proving minimality of
this Markov basis.

The relations involving \(w\) in (9) are
\[
\begin{gathered}
bu-cw,\qquad b^4w-tu,\qquad c^2a^5u-tw,\\
ca^5u^3-b^3w^3,\qquad a^5u^4-b^2w^4.
\end{gathered}
\tag{11}
\]

## 2. The cheapest relation closes exactly

Let \(p_5,\theta,\kappa_{20}\) be the formal leading coefficients of
\(B,U,W\).  Normalize (2) as
\[
S_{CW}=\kappa_{20}BU-p_5\theta CW.
\tag{12}
\]
Its exact reduction is
\[
S_{CW}-\rho_1AB^4-\rho_2T=0,
\tag{13}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho_1&=
-\frac{34098091069723790625}
{12849356976252738230319332164410926000343082860544},\\
\rho_2&=
-\frac{117766993449375}
{42382290729925007165141632705051492352}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
The cancelled exponent pairs are \((26,20)\) and \((25,19)\).

## 3. The formal \(V\)-frontier

Normalize (3) by
\[
S_V=\lambda_0\theta B^4W-p_5^4\kappa_{20}TU.
\tag{15}
\]
Successively use
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(46,35)&C^3B^2A^6\\
(44,35)&C^2A^4U\\
(45,34)&C^2B^3A^5\\
(43,34)&C^2A^3W\\
(44,33)&CB^4A^4\\
(42,33)&CBA^2W\\
(43,32)&CA^3T.
\end{array}
\tag{16}
\]
Call the exact result \(V\).  Its Pareto frontier is
\[
(42,31),\qquad(41,32),\qquad(40,33),
\tag{17}
\]
and
\[
[u^{40}f^{33}]V=
\kappa_V=
\frac{646429908327484130859375}
{7185034270063548786562948887160069885258119973112902428748815183129257967616}
\ne0.
\tag{18}
\]

The role of the seventh correction depends on \(L\):

- for \(L=2\), \((43,32)\) precedes \((40,33)\), so the seventh
  correction is part of the actual leading reduction;
- for \(L=3\), the two pairs tie;
- for \(L\ge4\), \((40,33)\) already precedes \((43,32)\), and the
  seventh correction is only a uniform algebraic cleanup below the
  unreduced leading term.

In all cases the polynomial defined by (15)--(16) has unique
remaining top pair \((40,33)\), but this fact alone does not decide
membership after applying \(\pi_L\).

## 4. Exact projected classification of \(V\)

Suppose nonnegative integers \(c,b,a,t,u,w\) satisfy
\[
c\pi_L(v_C)+b\pi_L(v_B)+a\pi_L(v_A)
+t\pi_L(v_T)+u\pi_L(v_U)+w\pi_L(v_W)
=\pi_L(v_V).
\tag{19}
\]
The first component gives
\[
c=-11+b+2a+6t+5u+7w.
\tag{20}
\]
Substitution in the second component gives the single Diophantine
identity
\[
(L+1)\bigl(5b+6a+25t+22u+27w-44\bigr)
=2(u+w)-4.
\tag{21}
\]
Positivity of projected degrees gives
\[
u,w\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{22}
\]

For \(L=2\), divisibility by \(3\) in (21) forces \(u=w=1\).
The remaining equation is
\[
5b+6a+25t=-5,
\tag{23}
\]
which is impossible.

For \(L\ge4\), the divisor \(L+1\ge5\), while the right side of
(21) lies in \(\{-4,-2,0\}\).  It must therefore be zero.  Again
\(u=w=1\), and (23) follows.  Thus the projected value is outside the
old semigroup for \(L=2\) and all \(L\ge4\).

For \(L=3\), divisibility by \(4\) permits \(u=w=0\), and (21)
becomes
\[
5b+6a+25t=43.
\tag{24}
\]
The projected degree bounds \(t\le1\).  If \(t=0\), reduction modulo
\(5\) gives \((a,b)=(3,5)\); if \(t=1\), it gives
\((a,b)=(3,0)\).  Equation (20) then gives respectively \(c=0\) and
\(c=1\).  These are exactly the two factorizations in (6).

There is genuine coefficient-dependent work left at \(L=3\).  Write
the cubic boundary polynomial as
\[
f(u)=r u^3+d u^2+\cdots,\qquad r\ne0.
\tag{25}
\]
After cancelling the degree-\(139\) term of \(V(f(u))\) with
\(CA^3T\), the degree-\(138\) coefficient is exactly
\[
\kappa_V\,d\,r^{32}.
\tag{26}
\]
It is nonzero when \(d\ne0\), but vanishes on the legitimate
subfamily \(d=0\).  A complete cubic SAGBI reduction must therefore
branch on lower graph coefficients.  This audit stops rather than
claiming a uniform cubic generator.

## 5. The \(X\)-witness is projected-nonmember for every \(L\ge2\)

The formal identity
\[
v_C+v_V=2v_U
\tag{27}
\]
lifts: the normalized \(CV\sim U^2\) polynomial reduces exactly to
zero through eleven displayed monomials.

The equality
\[
v_U+v_W=v_B+v_V=(-12,45,37)
\tag{28}
\]
does not lift to zero with the displayed set.  Cancelling
\((46,36)\) by \(B^3AW\) and \((47,35)\) by \(C^3BA^7\) leaves an
element \(X\) with Pareto frontier
\[
(46,34),\qquad(45,35),
\tag{29}
\]
and nonzero coefficient
\[
[u^{45}f^{35}]X=
\frac{19677099045075849323526515486997127532958984375}
{4753783574128806258117724534569839285153295438529048764295676190272725307777236595256331251079400117251896323723308382663349555429376}.
\tag{30}
\]
The pair \((45,35)\) uniquely leads for every \(L\ge2\).

To test projected membership, let \(v\) be the exponent of \(V\).
Eliminating \(c\) from the two projected equations gives
\[
(L+1)\bigl(
5b+6a+25t+22u+27w+44v-47
\bigr)
=2u+2w+4v-2.
\tag{31}
\]
Projected degree bounds give
\[
u\le2,\qquad w\le1,\qquad v\le1,\qquad t\le1.
\tag{32}
\]
Put \(R=2u+2w+4v-2\).  For \(L\ge8\), one has
\(|R|\le8<L+1\), so (31) forces \(R=0\).  The only nonnegative
triples with \(R=0\) are
\[
(u,w,v)=(0,1,0),\qquad(1,0,0).
\tag{33}
\]
They reduce (31) respectively to
\[
5b+6a+25t=20,\qquad
5b+6a+25t=25.
\tag{34}
\]
The first has only \((b,a,t)=(4,0,0)\), for which (the analogue of)
(20) gives \(c=-1\).  The second has only
\((5,0,0)\) and \((0,0,1)\), giving \(c=-2\) and \(c=-1\).

For \(2\le L\le7\), the finite divisibility check in (31) gives the
same two \(R=0\) cases, plus only cases whose right side in (34) is
negative.  Explicitly, the additional right sides are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
L&\text{additional values}\\ \hline
2&-44,-39\\
3&-23,-18,-23,-66\\
4&\varnothing\\
5&-45,-40\\
6&\varnothing\\
7&-67.
\end{array}
\tag{35}
\]
Hence no projected factorization exists for any \(L\ge2\).  This
makes \(X\) a witness that the displayed set
\(\{C,B,A,T,U,W,V\}\) is incomplete at every such graph degree.

## 6. The separate \(L=1\) branch

The fixed linear graph is
\[
f(u)=\frac{91}{34}-\frac{57}{34}u.
\tag{36}
\]
After specialization, the \((x,\deg_u)\)-vectors of
\(C,B,A,T,U,W\) are
\[
(1,1),\ (-1,9),\ (-2,10),\ (-6,44),\
(-5,37),\ (-7,45).
\tag{37}
\]
The normalized leading terms of \(BU\) and \(CW\) cancel, but the
remainder has degree \(45\) at \(x\)-order \(-6\), with nonzero exact
coefficient.  If \((-6,45)\) belonged to the semigroup in (37),
eliminating the \(C\)-exponent would give
\[
10b+12a+50t+42u+52w=51,
\tag{38}
\]
impossible by parity.

## 7. Exact scope and nonclaims

This audit proves:

1. the formal rank-three toric kernel and a minimal Markov basis
   after adjoining \(W\);
2. exact zero reduction of \(CW\sim BU\);
3. projected nonmembership of the \(V\)-frontier exactly for
   \(L=2\) and \(L\ge4\), with the two \(L=3\) reducers classified;
4. exact zero reduction of \(CV\sim U^2\);
5. projected nonmembership of the \(X\)-frontier for every \(L\ge2\);
   and
6. immediate divergence of the \(L=1\) branch.

It does not prove a uniform \(L=3\) next generator, termination after
\(X\), or an infinite SAGBI completion.
