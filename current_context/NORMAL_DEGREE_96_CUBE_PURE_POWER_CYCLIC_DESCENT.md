# Cyclic descent for the pure-power cube chart

Date: 26 July 2026

## Outcome

Consider the residual family in
`NORMAL_DEGREE_96_CUBE_LAURENT_TIME_REDUCTION.md`.  Put
\[
 u=x-a,\qquad r=\gamma u^{k+1},\qquad
 T=6A_5=\alpha+\beta u^{-k},
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(k\geq1\) and \(\beta\gamma\ne0\).  Let
\[
 K=\mathbf C(a,b,c,d,q)\subset\mathbf C(u)
 \tag{2}
\]
be the field generated by the five depressed sextic coefficients.

This note proves three reductions.

1. There are integers \(m,n\geq1\), with \(k=mn\), such that
   \[
   K=\mathbf C(v),\qquad v=u^m,
   \tag{3}
   \]
   and all five normalized coefficients are Laurent polynomials in
   \(v\).  The normalized pair descends to
   \[
   [f,g]_{v,w}=\frac{\lambda}{m\gamma v^{n+1}},
   \qquad
   T=\alpha+\beta v^{-n}.
   \tag{4}
   \]
   Thus the coefficient normalization is a cyclic monomial cover,
   not an arbitrary rational parametrization.

2. Suppose the five coefficients are regular at \(v=\infty\), as
   predicted by the constant-\(h\) weighted end analysis.  Set
   \(z=v^{-1}\).  Then
   \[
   f,g\in\mathbf C[z,w],\qquad
   [f,g]_{z,w}=-\frac{\lambda}{m\gamma}z^{n-1}.
   \tag{5}
   \]
   If
   \[
   \gcd\bigl(f_w(0,w),g_w(0,w)\bigr)=1,
   \tag{6}
   \]
   then \(n=1\).  In fact the exact degree bounds force
   \(f,g\in\mathbf C[z^n,w]\); this contradicts
   \(K=\mathbf C(z)\) when \(n>1\).

3. Condition (6) is essential: the connected cuspidal model gives a
   primitive polynomial solution of (5) with \(n=7\).  Nevertheless
   that model cannot lift back through (1) to a polynomial pair in
   \(\mathbf C[u,y]\).  Its positive weighted-homogeneous map is
   finite, so a lift would make \(z^3=u^{-3m}\) integral over the
   normal ring \(\mathbf C[u,y]\), which is impossible.

Consequently every unresolved pure-power candidate must have a
common-critical endpoint at \(z=0\), but it cannot be the known
cuspidal endpoint.  This identifies the exact obstruction to a
naive \(\mu_n\)-invariance argument.  It does not yet exclude all
common-critical ramification types.

There is a second warning which is important for subsequent descent:
regularity at \(v=\infty\) cannot be proved by a one-place pole
inequality.  Section 2 gives an exact rational invariant curve on
which the terminal time has a small pole at one end and a second pole
at the other.  The global two-ended form of \(T\), not a local weight
bound, excludes that curve.

## 1. Classification of the coefficient field

The Laurent coefficient \(A_5\) is a polynomial in
\(a,b,c,d,q\), with coefficients in the constant upper-parameter
ring.  Hence
\[
 \mathbf C(T)=\mathbf C(u^k)\subset K\subset\mathbf C(u).
 \tag{7}
\]
The extension
\[
 \mathbf C(u)/\mathbf C(u^k)
 \tag{8}
\]
is cyclic Galois, with group \(\mu_k\) acting by \(u\mapsto\zeta u\).
Every subgroup of \(\mu_k\) is the unique subgroup of its order.
Galois correspondence therefore gives a divisor \(m\mid k\) such
that
\[
 K=\mathbf C(u^m).
 \tag{9}
\]
Write \(v=u^m\) and \(k=mn\).

Before depression, every normalized coefficient is a polynomial
coefficient of the original pair divided by a power of \(r\).
Depression only performs rational additions and multiplications with
the same property.  Thus the only possible finite pole of any of
\(a,b,c,d,q\) is \(u=0\).  Since each coefficient belongs to
\(\mathbf C(v)\), its only possible finite pole as a function of
\(v\) is \(v=0\).  A rational function on \(\mathbf P^1_v\) with
poles contained in \(\{0,\infty\}\) is a Laurent polynomial.  Hence
\[
 a,b,c,d,q\in\mathbf C[v,v^{-1}].
 \tag{10}
\]

Because \(f\) and \(g\) are polynomials in \(w\) whose coefficients
belong to \(K=\mathbf C(v)\),
\[
 [f,g]_{u,w}
 =m u^{m-1}[f,g]_{v,w}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The normalized Keller equation is
\[
 [f,g]_{u,w}=\frac{\lambda}{\gamma u^{k+1}}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Using \(k=mn\) in (11)--(12) proves the first identity in (4).
The second is immediate from (1):
\[
 u^{-k}=v^{-n}.
 \]

## 2. A two-ended obstruction to local pole bounds

On the only-\(j\) level
\[
 U=V=0,\qquad W=j^2,\qquad Z\ne0,
 \tag{13}
\]
let \(\epsilon^2=1/6\) and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C&=t^2,&
 D&=\frac{8Z}{9t^4},\\
 Q&=-\frac{128Z^2}{81t^{10}}-\frac{2j}{3}
      +\epsilon t^3,\\
 a&=\frac{4(-32Z^2+27\epsilon t^{13})}{27t^{12}},&
 b&=-\frac{32Z(-64Z^2+81\epsilon t^{13})}{729t^{18}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Direct substitution in the four centered invariants gives exactly
\[
 I_{10}=0,\qquad I_{11}=0,\qquad I_{12}=j^2,\qquad I_{13}=Z.
\tag{15}
\]
The Laurent time is
\[
 \boxed{
 A_5=\frac{4Z^2}{27}t^{-6}-\frac{\epsilon}{16}t^7.
 }
\tag{16}
\]

At \(t=0\), the uncentered coefficients have weighted pole scale
\[
 \rho=\max\left\{
 \frac{\operatorname{pole}(a)}2,
 \frac{\operatorname{pole}(b)}3,
 \frac{\operatorname{pole}(c)}4,
 \frac{\operatorname{pole}(d)}5,
 \frac{\operatorname{pole}(q)}6
 \right\}=6,
\tag{17}
\]
whereas the local pole order of \(A_5\) is only \(6\).  Thus a proposed
universal inequality such as
\[
 \operatorname{pole}(A_5)\ge4\rho
\tag{18}
\]
is false even on an exact rational invariant curve.

This curve is nevertheless not a pure-power candidate.  Formula
(16) has a pole at each of \(t=0\) and \(t=\infty\), and its
derivative has finite nonzero zeros.  It cannot equal the
Riemann--Hurwitz time
\[
 \alpha+\beta v^{-n},
\]
which has one finite pole and no finite critical point.  The example
therefore strengthens the need to use both ends of the cyclic
monomial curve.

The two-ended formula is exhaustive on this level.  Eliminating
\(a,b,D\) leaves a quadratic equation in \(Q\), whose discriminant is
\[
 38263752\,C^{20}\bigl(3C^3-8W+8j^2\bigr).
\tag{19}
\]
If \(W\ne j^2\), its projective normalization is the smooth
genus-one curve obtained from a double cover branched over the three
roots of \(3C^3-8W+8j^2\) and infinity.  It admits no nonconstant
map from \(\mathbf P^1\), so it cannot be the rational coefficient
curve.  If \(W=j^2\), the normalization is rational and (14)--(16)
give its two choices of sign.  A birational change of its parameter
preserves the two distinct poles of \(A_5\), so neither sign can have
the one-pole form (1).

For comparison, the other apparent local failure occurs on the
connected level
\[
 U=V=Z=0,\qquad
 C^3=6Q^2+8jQ+\frac83W.
\tag{20}
\]
At a simple zero of the quadratic, one can have \(C\to0\) and
\(a\to\infty\) while \(A_5\) remains regular.  When \(W\ne j^2\),
however, the smooth projective curve in (19) has genus one and hence
admits no nonconstant rational parametrization by
\(\mathbf P^1_v\).  When \(W=j^2\), it is the cusp already handled
by the finite-map descent in Section 4.

There is also a useful sharp bound on every remaining \(U=0\)
place.  When \(V\ne0\), elimination gives the plane curve in
\((C,D)\) displayed in the constant-\(h\), only-\(j\) note.  Let
\[
 c=\operatorname{pole}(C),\qquad
 d=\operatorname{pole}(D).
\]
Its outer Newton edges give exactly the following profiles, up to a
positive integer scale \(s\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(c,d)&\operatorname{pole}(A_5)&\rho&
\operatorname{pole}(A_5)/\rho\\ \hline
(s,4s)&9s&3s&3\\
(-3s,-s)&6s&2s&3\\
(-s,-s)&2s&2s/3&3\\
(-s,-4s)&2s&2s/3&3\\
(2s,-3s)&7s&s/2&14 .
\end{array}
\tag{21}
\]
The third profile occurs when \(W\ne j^2\), and the fourth replaces
it when \(W=j^2\).  A horizontal edge has bounded coefficients.
Substitution of the edge equations shows that every displayed
leading coefficient of \(A_5\) is nonzero.  Hence
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{pole}(A_5)\ge3\rho}
\tag{22}
\]
on the entire \(U=0,V\ne0\) level.

Together, (19)--(22) prove the following exact statement needed in a
line-restriction descent:

> **\(U=0\) pole dichotomy.**  On a rational only-\(j\) invariant
> curve with one-pole time (1),
> \[
> \operatorname{pole}(A_5)\ge2\rho
> \]
> at its pole.  The connected cusp (31) satisfies the stronger ratio
> \(14\); its finite-map lift exclusion in Section 4 is an independent
> check.

The exceptional two-ended curve (14) is the only \(U=0\) profile
with local ratio below two, and the one-pole time excludes it
globally.

When \(U\ne0\), the same conclusion is entirely local.  The outer
Newton compactification of the exact resultant has the following
profiles:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{profile}&\operatorname{pole}(A_5)/\rho&\text{condition}\\ \hline
(c,d)=(2s,5s)&4&\text{all }V,Z\\
(c,d)=(2s,-3s)&14&V\ne0\\
(c,d)=(-s,-s)&4&V\ne0\\
\text{right double edge}&14&V=0,\ Z\ne0\\
(c,d)=(-s,-3s)&4&V=0,\ Z\ne0\\
D=0\text{ lower cusp}&14&V=Z=0 .
\end{array}
\tag{23}
\]
On the right double edge, the first equation is
\[
 9C^2D=8Z.
\]
Writing
\[
 C=C_0x^2,\qquad
 D=\frac{8Z}{9C^2}+D_1x^{-9}
\]
gives the exact next leading equations
\[
\begin{aligned}
 [x^2]P&=-C_0\left(
 177147C_0^9D_1^2-32768U^2Z^2\right),\\
 [x^7]A_5&=\frac{243C_0^8D_1}{4096UZ}\ne0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]
This is the ratio-\(14\) entry in (23), and records the singular
edge rather than treating its double leading equation as reduced.

There is one vertical boundary, where \(C\to0\) and \(D\) tends to a
nonzero limit.  Put \(y=CQ\).  The two leading invariant equations
give exactly
\[
 (D^2,y)=\left(-\frac{4U}{9},0\right)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (D^2,y)=\left(-\frac{4U}{27},-\frac{8U}{27}\right).
\tag{25}
\]
At the first point the equations for
\((D,Q,a,b)\), in the order
\((I_{10},I_{11},I_{12},I_{13})\), have nonzero triangular
diagonal
\[
 -\frac92D,\quad-\frac92D,\quad\frac34D^2,\quad-\frac U3.
\]
The formal implicit-function theorem therefore makes every
coefficient bounded.  At the second point,
\[
 a\asymp C^{-2},\qquad A_5\asymp C^{-4},\qquad
 \rho=\operatorname{pole}(C),
\]
so its ratio is \(4\).

Equations (23)--(25), together with the \(U=0\) dichotomy, prove:

> **Only-\(j\) threshold theorem.**  For every rational only-\(j\)
> invariant curve having the one-pole time (1),
> \[
> \boxed{\operatorname{pole}(A_5)\ge2\rho}
> \tag{26}
> \]
> at the time pole.

If at least one extra upper coefficient is nonzero, the verified
constant-\(h\) transverse analysis is valuation-local.  Its only
surviving balanced terminal profile has
\[
 \operatorname{pole}(A_5)=(14-\delta)\rho,\qquad
 \delta\in\{1,2,4,5,7,8\},
\]
and hence has ratio at least \(6\); all other profiles are excluded
before the terminal row.  Thus (26) holds for the full
seven-constant upper form.

## 3. The polynomial quotient at a regular end

Assume now that the coefficients have no pole at \(v=\infty\).
Equation (10) then becomes
\[
 a,b,c,d,q\in\mathbf C[v^{-1}]=\mathbf C[z].
 \tag{27}
\]
Changing from \(v\) to \(z=v^{-1}\) in (4) gives (5).

The following elementary ramification lemma is the useful point.

> **Ramification lemma.**  Let \(P,Q\in\mathbf C[z,w]\), with
> \[
> \deg_wP=9,\qquad \deg_wQ=6,
> \]
> and assume that the coefficients of \(w^9,w^8\) in \(P\), and of
> \(w^6,w^5\) in \(Q\), are constant.  Suppose
> \[
> [P,Q]_{z,w}=c z^{n-1},\qquad c\ne0.
> \tag{28}
> \]
> If \(\gcd(P_w(0,w),Q_w(0,w))=1\), then
> \[
> P,Q\in\mathbf C[z^n,w].
> \tag{29}
> \]

To prove the lemma, write
\[
 P=\sum_{i\ge0}z^iP_i(w),\qquad
 Q=\sum_{i\ge0}z^iQ_i(w).
 \tag{30}
\]
For every \(i>0\), the constant-leading-coefficient hypotheses give
\[
 \deg P_i\le7,\qquad \deg Q_i\le4.
 \tag{31}
\]
Assume inductively that every \(P_i,Q_i\) with \(0<i<r\) and
\(n\nmid i\) vanishes.  If \(n\nmid r\), the coefficient of
\(z^{r-1}\) in (14) receives no contribution from two positive
indices divisible by \(n\).  Its only possible terms are therefore
\[
 r\bigl(P_rQ_0'-P_0'Q_r\bigr)=0.
 \tag{32}
\]
The coprimality assumption implies
\[
 P_r=H P_0',\qquad Q_r=H Q_0'
 \tag{33}
\]
for some \(H\in\mathbf C[w]\).  But
\[
 \deg P_0'=8,\qquad \deg Q_0'=5,
 \]
so (17) forces \(H=0\).  This proves inductively that all exponents
not divisible by \(n\) vanish, which is (15).

Apply the lemma to \(P=f,Q=g\).  If \(n>1\), all coefficients of
\(g\), and hence the field they generate, lie in
\(\mathbf C(z^n)\).  This contradicts
\[
 K=\mathbf C(v)=\mathbf C(z).
 \tag{34}
\]
Thus a squarefree endpoint forces \(n=1\).  In that case (5) has
constant nonzero Jacobian and is exactly a constant-\(h\) polynomial
coefficient chart, already excluded by the two constant-\(h\)
theorems.

## 4. Why common-critical endpoints require separate descent

The coprimality condition cannot simply be omitted.  On the cuspidal
connected level, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(z,w)&=w^9+6zw^7+21z^2w^5+35z^3w^3
          +\frac{63}{2}z^4w,\\
 Q(z,w)&=w^6+4zw^4+10z^2w^2+6z^3.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Then
\[
 [P,Q]_{z,w}=-567z^6.
\tag{36}
\]
This is (14) with \(n=7\), and its coefficients generate
\(\mathbf C(z)\), not \(\mathbf C(z^7)\).  At \(z=0\),
\[
 P_w=9w^8,\qquad Q_w=6w^5,
\]
so the failure occurs exactly through a common critical point.

Although (21) defeats the bare ramification lemma, it cannot arise
from a pure-power polynomial lift.  The pair (21) is positive
weighted-homogeneous for
\[
 \operatorname{wt}(z,w)=(2,1).
\]
Its common zero is only the origin.  On the two axes this is
immediate.  Away from the axes, putting \(X=w^2/z\) reduces a common
zero to
\[
\begin{aligned}
 X^3+4X^2+10X+6&=0,\\
 X^4+6X^3+21X^2+35X+\frac{63}{2}&=0,
\end{aligned}
\]
whose resultant is \(567/8\).

Therefore \(P,Q\) form a homogeneous system of parameters in
\(\mathbf C[z,w]\), and \(\mathbf C[z,w]\) is module-finite over
\(\mathbf C[P,Q]\).  In particular \(z^3\) is integral over
\(\mathbf C[P,Q]\).

In a putative lift, \(z=v^{-1}=u^{-m}\), while \(P,Q\) are target
transforms of the original polynomial coordinates.  Hence
\[
 \mathbf C[P,Q]\subset\mathbf C[u,y].
\]
The element \(z^3=u^{-3m}\) lies in
\(\operatorname{Frac}\mathbf C[u,y]\) and would be integral over
\(\mathbf C[u,y]\).  Since \(\mathbf C[u,y]\) is normal, this would
force
\[
 u^{-3m}\in\mathbf C[u,y],
\]
an impossibility.

## Closure by the terminal-pole threshold

The squarefree-end and finite-leading-map arguments above are useful
independent obstructions, but they are not needed to classify every
common-critical endpoint.  The companion note
`NORMAL_DEGREE_96_PURE_POWER_LINE_INJECTIVITY_EXCLUSION.md` completes
the chart by a stronger global argument.  It compactifies every
seven-constant invariant curve, proves the sharp one-pole inequality
\[
 n\ge2\rho,
\]
and uses polynomiality of the original lift to show that one coordinate
restricts affinely and injectively to \(u=0\).  The one-line injectivity
theorem then forces the Keller map to be an automorphism.

No properness or finiteness of the full normalized map is assumed in
either argument.  Finiteness is used here only for the explicit
homogeneous cusp (21), where it follows from the checked common-zero
computation.
