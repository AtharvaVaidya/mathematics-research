# Nonlinear audit of the repeated-root Kummer modes

Date: 26 July 2026

## Outcome

Let
\[
R=\prod_i(X-\alpha_i)^{e_i},\qquad
d=\gcd(g,e_1,\ldots,e_r),\qquad
\kappa=\frac gd
=\operatorname{lcm}_i\frac{g}{\gcd(g,e_i)},
\]
and write
\[
R=H^d,\qquad \deg H=\kappa,\qquad
p=da,\qquad q=db.
\]
Here \(H=\prod_i(X-\alpha_i)^{e_i/d}\).  The displayed lcm identity
follows prime by prime, and shows that \(\kappa\) is exactly the
Kummer period \(\kappa_R\) from the fixed-base first-layer theorem.
The fixed-base Kummer monomials found in the first-layer calculation,
including the sub-\(g\) modes when \(d>1\), do **not** die under their
mutual nonlinear interactions.  They span the exact polynomial
zero-bracket cone
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm Kum}
 &=\sum_{r=0}^{p}A_r\tau^{\kappa r}H^{p-r},\\
Q_{\rm Kum}
 &=\sum_{s=0}^{q}B_s\tau^{\kappa s}H^{q-s},
\end{aligned}}
\tag{1}
\]
and every pair in (1) has identically zero homogenized bracket.

There is nevertheless a sharp first obstruction to turning this cone
into a Keller pair:

> **Top-form escape theorem.**  If \(\kappa>1\), no reciprocal Keller
> pair can have both of its highest ordinary total-degree forms of
> Kummer type.  Equivalently, after dehomogenization, its two top forms
> cannot both lie in \(\mathbb C[x^\kappa]\).

Indeed, after dehomogenization those top forms are
\[
p_0(x)=A(x^\kappa),\qquad q_0(x)=B(x^\kappa).
\]
Their derivatives share \(x^{\kappa-1}\), whereas the first
nonzero-scalar equation is the Bezout identity
\[
p_1q_0'-q_1p_0'=-J.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus, for every repeated-root candidate with \(\kappa>1\), at least
one dehomogenized top form must lie outside
\(\mathbb C[x^\kappa]\), or equivalently at least one top homogeneous
form must lie outside the appropriate homogeneous part of
\(\mathbb C[X^\kappa,\tau^\kappa]\).  The Kummer modes can propagate,
but cannot by themselves supply the Keller scalar.

When \(\kappa=1\), the universal derivative factor
\(x^{\kappa-1}\) disappears; the additional condition
\(\gcd(p_0',q_0')=1\) is still required.  The subsequent equations
are exactly the ordinary two-variable constant-Jacobian recurrence,
not a smaller Kummer subsystem.  An explicit degree-\((4,6)\) branch
with a first \(Q\)-Kummer term passes (2) and the next equation but
fails at defect three.  This is an exact worked obstruction, not a
classification of all degree-\((4,6)\) top forms.

## 1. Why all Kummer interactions vanish

Put \(T=\tau^\kappa\), and let \(F(H,T)\), \(G(H,T)\) be homogeneous
of degrees \(p,q\), respectively.  Since
\[
n=\kappa p,\qquad m=\kappa q,
\]
the pair \(P=F(H,T)\), \(Q=G(H,T)\) is exactly (1).  Direct
differentiation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr K_X(P,Q)
=\kappa H'\bigl[
T(F_HG_T-F_TG_H)+pFG_H-qGF_H
\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Euler's identities
\[
HF_H+TF_T=pF,\qquad HG_H+TG_T=qG
\]
make the expression in brackets zero.  This proves
\[
\boxed{\mathscr K_X(P_{\rm Kum},Q_{\rm Kum})=0}
\tag{4}
\]
for arbitrary coefficients in (1), not only infinitesimally.

Every monomial in (1) satisfies the reciprocal bound:
\[
\deg_X H^{p-r}=\kappa(p-r)=n-\kappa r
\]
and similarly for \(Q\).  Thus (4) is an exact
reciprocal-compatible zero-bracket family.  It is not a Keller pair,
because the required right side is nonzero.

The fixed-base first-layer mode
\[
\tau^jR^{\,b-j/g}
\]
is precisely the term with \(j=\kappa s\):
\[
\tau^{\kappa s}H^{q-s}.
\]
Consequently (1) is an exact zero-bracket cone generated by all pure
fixed-base Kummer monomials while the common factor \(H\) is kept
fixed.  It is closed under arbitrary choices of the displayed
coefficients.  No maximality among zero-bracket families is claimed,
and general nonlinear corrections are not asserted to remain in this
cone.

## 2. The total-degree defect recurrence

Write \(P_r,Q_s\) for the ordinary homogeneous pieces of total
degrees \(n-r,m-s\).  The Euler identities for these two pieces give
the exact bilinear formula
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr K_X(P_r,Q_s)
=rP_r(Q_s)_X-sQ_s(P_r)_X.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Dehomogenize by
\[
P_r(X,\tau)=\tau^{n-r}p_r(X/\tau),\qquad
Q_s(X,\tau)=\tau^{m-s}q_s(X/\tau).
\]
At total defect \(k\), the Keller equation is
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r+s=k}
\left(rp_rq_s'-sq_sp_r'\right)
=
\begin{cases}
-J,&k=1,\\
0,&k\ne1.
\end{cases}}
\tag{6}
\]
The \(k=0\) equation vanishes identically.  The first forcing layer is
therefore exactly (2).  It has a bounded polynomial solution if and
only if
\[
\gcd(p_0',q_0')=1.
\tag{7}
\]
The usual Bezout representatives already obey
\(\deg p_1\le n-1\), \(\deg q_1\le m-1\).

For \(k\ge2\), isolate the new pieces:
\[
\begin{aligned}
k(p_kq_0'-q_kp_0')
=-\sum_{\substack{r+s=k\\r,s>0}}
(rp_rq_s'-sq_sp_r').
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
This is the promised structural nonlinear recurrence.

There is also a useful audit of its scope.  With the defect-bookkeeping
series
\[
p(t,x)=\sum_r t^rp_r(x),\qquad
q(t,x)=\sum_s t^sq_s(x),
\]
all of (6) is equivalent to
\[
\boxed{p_tq_x-q_tp_x=-J.}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus, after the first derivative-gcd test, the recurrence is the full
constant-Jacobian problem in the variables \((t,x)\).  Treating it as
a small linear Kummer correction system would merely disguise the
original problem.

## 3. Proof of top-form escape

The highest ordinary homogeneous part of \(H(X)\) is \(X^\kappa\)
after monic normalization.  Hence the dehomogenized top forms of (1)
are
\[
p_0(x)=\sum_r A_rx^{\kappa(p-r)}=A(x^\kappa),
\qquad
q_0(x)=B(x^\kappa).
\]
If \(\kappa>1\), then
\[
x^{\kappa-1}\mid p_0',
\qquad
x^{\kappa-1}\mid q_0'.
\]
The left side of (2) is divisible by \(x^{\kappa-1}\) and cannot be
the nonzero constant \(-J\).  This proves the theorem.  In homogeneous
reciprocal coordinates, the same statement says that at least one top
form lies outside the relevant degree piece of
\(\mathbb C[X^\kappa,\tau^\kappa]\).

For the smallest two-factor example
\[
g=4,\qquad R=X^2(X-1)^2,
\]
one has \(d=2,\kappa=2,H=X(X-1)\).  Pure Kummer propagation gives
top forms in \(x^2\), so (2) fails before any higher nonlinear
equation is reached.

For
\[
g=2,\qquad R=X^2,
\]
one has \(d=2,\kappa=1,H=X\).  The cone (1) now contains arbitrary
binary homogeneous forms of degrees \(4\) and \(6\), and (7) can hold.
This explains exactly why the one-root case survives the top-form
test.

## 4. An exact surviving-first-layer branch that later fails

Take \(n=4,m=6\) and
\[
p_0=x^4+x,\qquad q_0=x^6+x^5.
\tag{10}
\]
The \(x^5\) term in \(q_0\) is the first \(Q\)-Kummer mode.  The
\(x\) term in \(p_0\) splits the common derivative factor, and
\[
\gcd(4x^3+1,\,6x^5+5x^4)=1.
\]
All solutions of (2), with \(J=1\), form a one-parameter affine
line.  Writing the parameter as \(h\), one convenient representative
is
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_1={}&\frac23hx^3+\frac16h
       -\frac{400}{71}x^2+\frac{144}{71}x-\frac{120}{71},\\
q_1={}&hx^5+\frac56hx^4
       -\frac{600}{71}x^4-4x^3+1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The defect-two equation has a unique solution \(p_2,q_2\).  At
defect three, however, the endpoint map
\[
(p_3,q_3)\longmapsto
3(p_3q_0'-q_3p_0'),
\qquad
\deg p_3\le1,\quad\deg q_3\le3,
\]
has a one-dimensional cokernel.  Applied to the exact source built
from (11) and the unique defect-two solution, its value is
\[
\boxed{-\frac{245844523008}{25411681}\ne0,}
\tag{12}
\]
independently of \(h\).  Hence this branch cannot extend to a Keller
pair.

The number in (12) depends on the displayed normalization of the
cokernel row; its nonvanishing is the invariant statement.

## 5. Scope and next target

This audit proves:

1. the pure fixed-base Kummer monomials span an exact nonlinear
   zero-bracket cone;
2. when \(\kappa>1\), at least one dehomogenized top form of a Keller
   pair must lie outside \(\mathbb C[x^\kappa]\);
3. when \(\kappa=1\), the first escape condition is only the Bezout
   derivative test, and later defects contain the genuine
   constant-Jacobian difficulty; and
4. one natural \((4,6)\) split branch fails at defect three.

It does **not** exclude all repeated-root boundaries.  In particular,
common-factor deformations can introduce top terms outside (1), and
the theorem says those escape terms are necessary, not impossible.
The promising next calculation is therefore the first mixed
compact-face/Kummer escape, with the top derivative gcd already
removed, rather than further enumeration inside the zero-bracket
cone.
