# The first unresolved SAGBI saturation produces a new generator

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

The boundary generators
\[
A,\ B,\ C,\ T,\ U
\]
do not form a complete SAGBI basis for the regular source-boundary
valuation.  The apparent relation \(BU\sim T\) seen after projecting
to only the \((x,u)\)-coordinates is spurious: the full
\((x,u,\gamma)\)-exponent vectors are
\[
\begin{aligned}
v_C&=(1,0,1),&
v_B&=(-1,5,4),&
v_A&=(-2,6,4),\\
v_T&=(-6,25,19),&
v_U&=(-5,20,17).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus
\[
v_B+v_U=(-6,25,21)\ne v_T,
\tag{2}
\]
and \(BU\) precedes \(T\) by \(2L\) after a boundary polynomial of
degree \(L\) is substituted.

The genuine first unaudited saturation relation is
\[
BT\sim A^5C^3.
\tag{3}
\]
Its exact subduction, followed by the three available monomial
subtractions on the same boundary face, leaves a new Pareto frontier
\[
\boxed{
(-7,26,19),\qquad(-7,25,20).
}
\tag{4}
\]
Neither vector lies in the semigroup generated by (1).  For
\(L\ge2\), the second vector uniquely dominates.  For \(L=1\), the
two terms tie, but the fixed linear boundary polynomial prevents
their coefficients from cancelling.  Hence a genuinely new
generator is required.

This is a precise obstruction to claiming that the known two
subductions already give a terminating global normal form.  It does
not prove that the full SAGBI completion is infinite; it shows only
that at least one further generator, with a graph-degree-dependent
initial face, is unavoidable.

## 1. Exact saturation polynomial

Use
\[
p_5=-\frac3{46},\qquad q_6=-\frac5{92},\qquad
\lambda_0=
\frac{258473511}{31221670147323559936},
\tag{5}
\]
and let \(T\) be the exact maximal first subduction.  Its regular
boundary initial term is
\[
T_{\rm in}=\lambda_0u^{25}f^{19}.
\tag{6}
\]
The normalized saturation polynomial
\[
S_0=q_6^5BT-p_5\lambda_0A^5C^3
\tag{7}
\]
cancels the common initial monomial \(u^{30}f^{23}\) at boundary
order \(-7\).

Its next three uniform leading monomials have exponent pairs
\[
(29,22),\quad(28,21),\quad(27,20).
\tag{8}
\]
They are represented respectively by
\[
A^4BC^2,\qquad A^3B^2C,\qquad A^2B^3.
\tag{9}
\]
Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_1&=-\frac{1289186932405}{367666387654882241806336},\\
r_2&=\frac{967724214885}{183833193827441120903168},\\
r_3&=\frac{280059055635}{367666387654882241806336},
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
and define
\[
W=S_0+r_1A^4BC^2+r_2A^3B^2C+r_3A^2B^3.
\tag{11}
\]
Exact expansion cancels all three pairs in (8).

## 2. The new boundary frontier

The maximal incomparable terms in the regular boundary polynomial of
\(W\) are
\[
\kappa_{19}u^{26}f^{19},\qquad
\kappa_{20}u^{25}f^{20},
\tag{12}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{19}
&=-\frac{124730099934975}
{18931415844142301862996144553984},\\
\kappa_{20}
&=\frac{1423828125}
{8946793877193904472115380224}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Both are nonzero.  If \(L\ge2\), their \(u\)-degrees after
\(f=cu^L+\cdots\) differ by
\[
(25+20L)-(26+19L)=L-1>0,
\tag{14}
\]
so the \((25,20)\)-term uniquely leads.

If \(L=1\), the fixed jets force
\[
f(u)=\frac{91}{34}-\frac{57}{34}u.
\tag{15}
\]
The combined \(u^{45}\)-coefficient is
\[
\left(-\frac{57}{34}\right)^{19}
\left(\kappa_{19}
-\frac{57}{34}\kappa_{20}\right),
\tag{16}
\]
and
\[
\kappa_{19}-\frac{57}{34}\kappa_{20}
=-\frac{2206277077800825}
{321834069350419131670934457417728}\ne0.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the new boundary initial never disappears.

## 3. Nonmembership in the known semigroup

Let nonnegative exponents \((c,b,a,t,v)\) multiply
\((C,B,A,T,U)\).  Solving for the first vector in (4) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
c&=\frac45a+t-1,&
b&=1-\frac65a-5t,&
v&=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
If \(t\ge1\), then \(b<0\); if \(t=0\), nonnegativity of \(b\)
forces \(a=0\), and then \(c=-1\).

For the second vector in (4), the solutions have
\[
\begin{aligned}
c&=\frac45a+t-\frac95,&
b&=\frac{26}{5}-\frac65a-5t,&
v&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
Integrality forces \(a=1+5k\).  If \(k=0\), nonnegativity of \(c\)
requires \(t\ge1\), which makes \(b<0\); for \(k\ge1\), already
\(b<0\).  Hence neither vector is in the semigroup.

The next structural task is therefore to adjoin \(W\), compute the
new toric kernel in the full three-component valuation, and audit its
primitive saturation relations.  A two-component projection must not
be used, because it invents the false relation (2).
