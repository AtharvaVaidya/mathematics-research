# Route A: a full-degree cusp obstructs quartic monodromy

Date: 26 July 2026

## Outcome

Let
\[
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_4(t)&=(t^3,t^4+t^2),\\
\gamma_5(t)&=(t^3,t^5+t^4+t^2),\\
\gamma_6(t)&=(t^3,t^6+t^5+t^4+t^2)
\end{aligned}
\]
and let \(\Delta_D\) be the corresponding irreducible affine plane
curve.  Then there is no transitive homomorphism
\[
\boxed{
\rho:\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus\Delta_D)\longrightarrow S_4
}
\]
which sends smooth branch meridians to transpositions.

The point is global although the decisive relation is local.
Projection to \(x=t^3\) has degree three, and all three points of a
nearby generic vertical fiber enter the cusp at the origin.
Affine Zariski--van Kampen therefore makes the local cusp group
surject onto the global complement group.  Two meridians of an
\(A_2\) cusp satisfy
\[
uvu=vuv.
\]
Two transpositions satisfying this relation are either equal or are
adjacent and generate an \(S_3\) on only three letters.  Their image
cannot act transitively on four sheets.

This argument does **not** determine
\(\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus\Delta_D)\).  In particular, no explicit
global braid factorization or cyclic-complement claim is used here.

## 1. The trigonal projection and its cusp

The implicit equations are
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_4(x,y)
&=x^4+3x^2y+x^2-y^3,\\
F_5(x,y)
&=-x^5-4x^4-3x^3y-3x^3-3x^2y-x^2+y^3,\\
F_6(x,y)
&=x^6-2x^5-3x^4y+x^4+3x^3y+3x^3\\
&\qquad+3x^2y^2+3x^2y+x^2-y^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
They have degree three in \(y\), up to multiplication by \(-1\).
Their vertical discriminants are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname {disc}_y(F_4)
&=-27x^4(x-1)^2(x+1)^2,\\
\operatorname {disc}_y(F_5)
&=-27x^4(x^3+2x^2+3x+1)^2,\\
\operatorname {disc}_y(F_6)
&=-27x^4(x^3+2x^2+3x+1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Only the factor at \(x=0\) is needed below.

For \(x\ne0\), the three normalization parameters over \(x\) are
\[
t,\qquad \zeta t,\qquad \zeta^2t,
\qquad \zeta^2+\zeta+1=0.
\]
Near the origin,
\[
x=t^3,\qquad y=t^2+O(t^4).
\]
Thus the origin has Puiseux pair \((2,3)\), so it is an \(A_2\)
cusp.  Moreover all three points in the nearby vertical fiber tend
to this one cusp as \(x\to0\).

Degree six is not a separate case:
\[
q_6(t)=q_5(t)+t^6=q_5(t)+x^2.
\]
The polynomial automorphism
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto(x,y-x^2)
\]
takes \(\Delta_6\) to \(\Delta_5\), equivalently
\[
F_6(x,y+x^2)=-F_5(x,y).
\]

## 2. The local cusp group surjects globally

Choose a Milnor ball \(B_\epsilon\) about the cusp, small enough to
contain no other singular point.  For a sufficiently small regular
value \(x_*\ne0\), the three points of
\(\Delta_D\cap\{x=x_*\}\), a basepoint, and standard based meridians
around all three points can be chosen in the vertical disc
\[
\{x=x_*\}\cap B_\epsilon.
\]

Because \(F_D\) has constant degree three in \(y\), the affine
Zariski--van Kampen epimorphism for the projection to the \(x\)-axis
says that the three meridians of this regular vertical fiber generate
\(\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus\Delta_D)\).  With the preceding
basepoint choice, the epimorphism factors as
\[
\pi_1\bigl(
(\{x=x_*\}\cap B_\epsilon)\setminus\Delta_D
\bigr)
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(B_\epsilon\setminus\Delta_D)
\longrightarrow
\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus\Delta_D).
\]
The composite is onto, hence the second arrow is onto:
\[
\pi_1(B_\epsilon\setminus\Delta_D)
\twoheadrightarrow
\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus\Delta_D).
\]

This is the only global topological input.  It uses the special
full-degree property: every point of the degree-three vertical fiber
is captured in the one Milnor ball.

## 3. The \(S_4\) obstruction

The complement group of an \(A_2\) cusp has the meridional
presentation
\[
\langle u,v\mid uvu=vuv\rangle.
\]
Let
\[
\rho:\pi_1(\mathbf A^2\setminus\Delta_D)\longrightarrow S_4
\]
send smooth meridians to transpositions.  Composing \(\rho\) with the
local-to-global epimorphism shows that its image is generated by two
transpositions, again denoted \(u,v\), satisfying the braid relation.

If \(u=v\), they generate \(C_2\).  If \(u\ne v\), disjoint supports
would make \(u\) and \(v\) commute, in which case
\(uvu=vuv\) would force \(u=v\).  Their supports must therefore meet
in exactly one letter.  They generate the copy of \(S_3\) on the
union of their supports and fix the fourth letter.  In both cases the
image is nontransitive.

Consequently none of the three explicit curves can be the branch
curve of a connected quartic cover with generically simple branching.
This eliminates these explicit degree-\(4,5,6\) feasibility
certificates, not every curve with the same delta ledger.  A surviving
Route A construction must avoid having one cusp capture the entire
generic fiber of a useful linear projection.
