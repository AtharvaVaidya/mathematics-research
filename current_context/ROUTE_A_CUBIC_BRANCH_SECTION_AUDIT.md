# Route A: the cubic branch-section constraint

Date: 25 July 2026

## Outcome

Let
\[
B=\mathbf C[u,v,w]/(w^2-u-u^2v),\qquad S=\operatorname {Spec}B,
\]
and suppose that a Darboux pair \(P,Q\in B\) defines an étale map
\[
F=(P,Q):S\longrightarrow \mathbf A^2
\]
of generic degree \(3\).

The degree-two unit argument does not extend to a contradiction, but it
does have the following exact cubic replacement.

> **Cubic branch-section theorem.** Let \(Y\to\mathbf A^2\) be the finite
> normalization determined by
> \(\operatorname {Frac}(B)/\mathbf C(P,Q)\).  The extension cannot be
> cyclic.  At the generic point of every branch component
> \(\Gamma=V(f)\), the inverse image in \(Y\) has the form
> \[
> \operatorname {div}_Y(f)=2E+C,
> \]
> where \(E\) is the ramified prime and \(C\) is an unramified
> residue-degree-one prime.  The open surface \(S\subset Y\) omits
> \(E\), meets \(C\), and
> \[
> \operatorname {div}_S(f(P,Q))=C\cap S.
> \]
> In particular the retained sheet is a principal prime divisor:
> \[
> [C\cap S]=0\quad\text{in }\operatorname {Cl}(B).
> \]

Since
\[
\operatorname {Cl}(B)\simeq\mathbf Z/2,\qquad
[D]=[H]\ne0
\]
for
\[
D=V(u,w),\qquad H=V(1+uv,w),
\]
neither \(D\) nor \(H\) can be the retained sheet over a cubic branch
component.  In particular the collision curve \(F(D)\) is not a branch
component.  The latter conclusion also follows from the mandatory
collision theorem: above \(F(D)\), the open surface has at least two
distinct divisor components, whereas a simple cubic branch has only one
unramified prime available to meet \(S\).

This is a genuine all-coefficient restriction, but not a degree-three
exclusion.  The principal curve
\[
C_0=V(u-1)\simeq\mathbf A^1
\]
shows that \(S\) has allowable class-zero primes.  Moreover the exact
cubic map
\[
(s,t)\longmapsto (x,y)=(s,t^3-3st)
\]
has precisely the required \(2+1\) branch geometry.  Thus units, the
class group, the canonical degree-two chart, and the symplectic form do
not by themselves rule out a cubic Darboux map.

## 1. The finite normalization and the existence of branch

Put
\[
K=\operatorname {Frac}B,\qquad L=\mathbf C(P,Q),\qquad
A=\mathbf C[P,Q]\simeq\mathbf C[x,y],
\]
and let \(A'\) be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(K\).  Then
\[
Y=\operatorname {Spec}A'\longrightarrow\operatorname {Spec}A
\]
is finite of degree \(3\).

As in the degree-two audit, normality of \(B\) gives \(A'\subset B\).
The induced map \(S\to Y\) is birational and quasi-finite, hence is an
open immersion by Zariski's Main Theorem.  We identify \(S\) with this
open subset.

If \(Y\to\mathbf A^2\) had no divisorial branch locus, purity and
\(\pi_1^{\mathrm{et}}(\mathbf A^2_{\mathbf C})=1\) would make it a
trivial finite étale cover.  This is impossible because \(A'\) is a
domain and the degree is \(3\).  A branch divisor therefore exists.

## 2. Total cubic ramification is impossible

Let \(\Gamma=V(f)\) be an irreducible branch component.  If the valuation
of \(f\) were totally ramified in \(K/L\), then there would be a unique
prime \(E\) above it, with ramification index \(3\).  Since \(F\) is
étale on \(S\), the entire prime \(E\) would be omitted from \(S\).
Exactly as in the quadratic proof, this would imply
\[
F^{-1}(\Gamma)=\varnothing,\qquad f(P,Q)\in B^\times.
\]
But
\[
B^\times=\mathbf C^\times
\]
and \(P,Q\) are algebraically independent, so a nonconstant \(f\) cannot
pull back to a scalar.  Total ramification is impossible.

In particular a cyclic cubic extension is impossible: every nontrivial
tame inertia group in a cyclic group of order \(3\) has order \(3\).
The remaining cubic extension is non-Galois, its Galois closure has
group \(S_3\), and every branch inertia group is generated by a
transposition.

## 3. The retained sheet is principal

At the generic point of a branch component, the degree formula for a
tame cubic extension and the exclusion of total ramification leave only
\[
3=2\cdot1+1\cdot1.
\]
Consequently there are exactly two height-one primes above \((f)\):
\[
\operatorname {div}_Y(f)=2E+C,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(E\) is ramified and \(C\) is unramified of residue degree one.

The ramified prime is disjoint from \(S\), because the restriction
\(F:Y\supset S\to\mathbf A^2\) is étale.  The unramified prime cannot
also be disjoint from \(S\).  Otherwise the full inverse image of
\(\Gamma\) would be omitted and \(f(P,Q)\) would again be a nonconstant
unit.  Hence \(C\cap S\) is a nonempty prime divisor, and restricting
(1) to the open set \(S\) gives
\[
\operatorname {div}_S(f(P,Q))=C\cap S.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{[C\cap S]=0\text{ in }\operatorname {Cl}(B).}
\tag{3}
\]

The word “sheet” here is generic.  The map
\(C\to\Gamma\) is birational, and becomes an isomorphism on suitable
dense opens of the normalizations; no global section over a singular
branch curve is being asserted.

## 4. What the class group excludes

The standard divisor calculation on \(S\) is
\[
\operatorname {div}(u)=2D,\qquad
\operatorname {div}(w)=D+H,
\]
with
\[
\operatorname {Cl}(B)=\langle[D]\rangle\simeq\mathbf Z/2,\qquad
[H]=[D]\ne0.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (3)--(4) prove that neither \(D\) nor \(H\) can equal the
unique retained prime over a branch component.

For \(D\) there is an additional geometric formulation.  The mandatory
collision theorem says that \(D\) maps birationally onto
\(\Gamma_D=\overline{F(D)}\) and that at least one other source divisor
dominates \(\Gamma_D\).  If \(\Gamma_D\) were a cubic branch component,
both divisors would have to lie in the single irreducible open curve
\(C\cap S\), which is impossible.  Therefore
\[
\boxed{\Gamma_D\text{ is not a branch component of }Y\to\mathbf A^2.}
\tag{5}
\]
Generically above \(\Gamma_D\), the finite normalization is unramified.
The next global problem is therefore a three-sheet collision problem,
not a branch-section problem.

The class group does not exclude every retained sheet.  Indeed
\[
B/(u-1)
\simeq
\mathbf C[v,w]/(w^2-1-v)
\simeq\mathbf C[w].
\]
Thus
\[
C_0=V(u-1)
\]
is a smooth irreducible principal divisor of class zero.  This is an
exact internal witness that condition (3) is compatible with the
divisor theory of \(S\).

## 5. The canonical degree-two chart does not kill the cubic resolvent

There is a polynomial étale chart
\[
\pi_2:\mathbf A^2_{a,b}\longrightarrow S
\]
given by
\[
u=a^2,\qquad
w=a(1+2a^2b),\qquad
v=4b(1+a^2b).
\tag{6}
\]
It has generic degree \(2\) and adjoins \(\sqrt u\).  For the bracket
\[
\{u,v\}=-2w,\qquad
\{u,w\}=-u^2,\qquad
\{v,w\}=1+2uv,
\]
ordinary plane Jacobians satisfy
\[
J(\pi_2^*f,\pi_2^*g)=-4\,\pi_2^*\{f,g\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Thus \(\pi_2\) is everywhere étale.  Valuation-theoretically this agrees
with \(\operatorname {div}(u)=2D\): the quadratic extension
\[
K(\sqrt u)/K
\]
is unramified at every height-one valuation.

For a non-Galois cubic \(K/L\), its Galois closure over \(K\) is instead
the quadratic resolvent
\[
K(\sqrt\Delta),
\]
where \(\Delta\in L^\times/L^{\times2}\) is the cubic discriminant.
At a simple branch component, \(\Delta\) has odd valuation.  Along the
retained unramified prime \(C\cap S\), its pullback still has valuation
one.  Hence
\[
K(\sqrt\Delta)/K
\]
is ramified along \(C\cap S\), and cannot equal the unramified extension
\(K(\sqrt u)/K\).

This distinction is not a contradiction.  It only says that the
canonical double chart is not the quadratic resolvent of the hypothetical
cubic.  Composing \(F\) with (6) produces a plane Keller map of generic
degree \(6\); no degree reduction follows from the existence of the
intermediate étale double cover.

## 6. The symplectic form allows deletion of the ramified sheet

For a finite map of smooth surfaces, the pullback of a local base area
form vanishes along the ramification divisor.  At a simple cubic branch
the vanishing order along \(E\) is one.  Removing \(E\) removes exactly
the degeneracy of the pulled-back form, while the unramified sheet
\(C\cap S\) remains symplectic.

Thus the symplectic condition is aligned with, rather than opposed to,
the \(2+1\) geometry:
\[
\text{ramified double sheet omitted},\qquad
\text{unramified simple sheet retained}.
\]

## 7. Exact cubic countermodel

Consider the finite polynomial map
\[
\phi:\mathbf A^2_{s,t}\longrightarrow\mathbf A^2_{x,y},\qquad
x=s,\qquad y=t^3-3st.
\tag{8}
\]
Its function-field extension is the generic depressed cubic
\[
T^3-3xT-y=0.
\]
It has degree \(3\), and its discriminant, up to a nonzero scalar, is
\[
\Delta=4x^3-y^2.
\]
This is not a square, so the Galois closure has group \(S_3\).

The Jacobian and pulled-back branch equation factor exactly as
\[
J_\phi=-3(s-t^2),
\tag{9}
\]
\[
\phi^*(4x^3-y^2)
=(s-t^2)^2(4s-t^2).
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore
\[
E=V(s-t^2)
\]
is the ramified double sheet and
\[
C=V(4s-t^2)
\]
is the unramified simple sheet.  On \(C\),
\[
J_\phi=\frac94t^2,
\]
which is nonzero generically.  After deleting \(E\),
\[
U=\mathbf A^2\setminus E\longrightarrow\mathbf A^2
\]
is everywhere étale and retains \(C\cap U\).  Equations (9)--(10) give
an exact realization of the only branch geometry left by the cubic
theorem.

This countermodel is deliberately sharp but not a Darboux map from
\(S\).  Its open source has nonconstant units:
\[
\mathcal O(U)^\times
=\mathbf C^\times(s-t^2)^{\mathbf Z}.
\]
It proves that cubic monodromy, divisor multiplicities, and the
symplectic Jacobian alone cannot yield a contradiction.  The constant-unit
and class-group constraints on \(S\) add (3), but \(C_0=V(u-1)\) shows
that even this added constraint is not formally inconsistent.

## 8. Precise remaining target

The audited invariants force every actual cubic branch component to
have a class-zero retained prime disjoint from the distinguished roles
of \(D\) and \(H\).  Meanwhile the mandatory collision curve
\(\Gamma_D\) is unbranched in the finite normalization.

A further exclusion therefore needs genuinely global information, for
example:

1. a proof that every class-zero candidate retained sheet must intersect
   the omitted ramification boundary incompatibly with étaleness;
2. control of the three unramified sheets above \(\Gamma_D\), strong
   enough to contradict the mandatory \(D\)-collision; or
3. a completion theorem relating the omitted ramification divisors to
   the unique double fiber \(\operatorname {div}(u)=2D\).

Local ramification, the parity class \([D]\), the canonical double chart,
and the symplectic zero along \(E\) are exhausted by the theorem and
countermodel above.

## Theorem-level inputs

The exact verifier checks all displayed polynomial identities.  The
following standard geometric facts remain theorem-level inputs:

1. finiteness of normalization over \(\mathbf C[P,Q]\);
2. the birational quasi-finite form of Zariski's Main Theorem;
3. purity of branch locus and
   \(\pi_1^{\mathrm{et}}(\mathbf A^2_{\mathbf C})=1\);
4. tame cubic decomposition and inertia theory over a DVR;
5. the class-group computation (4) and the mandatory collision theorem
   from the preceding Route A audit.
