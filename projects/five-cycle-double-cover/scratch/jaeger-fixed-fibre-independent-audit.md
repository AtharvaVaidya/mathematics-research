## 1. Symmetric-exchange fibres

Let \(T_1,T_2,T_3\) be spanning trees of a connected graph and put
\[
 m_e=\bigl|\{i:e\in T_i\}\bigr|.
\]
A reciprocal exchange
\[
 T_i'=T_i-e+f,\qquad T_j'=T_j-f+e
\]
which leaves both displayed sets as bases necessarily has
\(e\in T_i-T_j\) and \(f\in T_j-T_i\).  It therefore preserves both the
multiset union and \(T_i\cap T_j\).  In particular, it preserves every
\(m_e\) and preserves an empty triple intersection.

Conversely, two multisets of graphic-matroid bases with the same
multiset union define two monomials of the same incidence multidegree.
Their difference belongs to the graphic matroid's toric ideal.
Blasiak's theorem says that this ideal is generated by the quadratic
binomials from single-element symmetric exchanges.  Equivalently (the
standard Markov-basis interpretation of a toric ideal generating set),
every such multidegree fibre is connected by those exchanges.  Thus,
after forgetting the order of the three trees, the symmetric-exchange
components really are exactly the feasible fixed-\(m\) fibres.

The cited source is Jonah Blasiak, *The toric ideal of a graphic matroid
is generated by quadrics*, Combinatorica 28 (2008), 283--297,
Theorem 1.2, DOI 10.1007/s00493-008-2256-6.

## 2. The three-defect classification

Now let \(G\) be cubic on \(n\) vertices.  Then
\[
 |E(G)|=\frac{3n}{2},\qquad
 \sum_e m_e=3(n-1).
\]
If \(T_1\cap T_2\cap T_3=\varnothing\), then \(0\leq m_e\leq2\).
Define \(d_e=2-m_e\).  It follows that
\[
 d_e\in\{0,1,2\},\qquad
 \sum_e d_e
 =2\frac{3n}{2}-3(n-1)=3.
\]
The only partitions of three into parts of size at most two are
\(1+1+1\) and \(2+1\).  Therefore the two types in the project note are
exhaustive:

1. three distinct edges have \(m_e=1\), and all other edges have
   \(m_e=2\);
2. one edge has \(m_e=0\), another has \(m_e=1\), and all other edges
   have \(m_e=2\).

No graph-theoretic premise beyond cubicity and the empty common
intersection is hidden in this calculation.

## 3. A stronger prescribed-fibre feasibility theorem

The project note proves that a 3-edge-connected graph has at least one
triple with empty common intersection.  In the cubic case one can prove
more:

> **Lemma.**  Let \(G\) be a finite 3-edge-connected cubic graph.  Every
> multiplicity vector \(m_e=2-d_e\), where
> \(d_e\in\{0,1,2\}\) and \(\sum_e d_e=3\), is realized by three spanning
> trees.

**Proof.** Replace every edge \(e\) by \(m_e\) parallel copies, obtaining
the multigraph \(H_m\).  It has
\[
 |E(H_m)|=\sum_e(2-d_e)=3n-3=3(n-1)
\]
edges.  A realization of \(m\) by three spanning trees is exactly a
decomposition of \(H_m\) into three edge-disjoint spanning trees.

By the Nash-Williams--Tutte spanning-tree packing theorem, such a
decomposition exists if and only if every vertex partition
\(\mathcal P\) into \(k\) nonempty parts has at least \(3(k-1)\)
crossing edge copies.  Let \(q\) be the number of original edges of
\(G\) crossing \(\mathcal P\), and let
\[
 D=\sum_{\substack{e\text{ crosses}\\\mathcal P}}d_e.
\]
The number of crossing copies in \(H_m\) is \(2q-D\).  If \(k\geq2\),
every part is a proper nonempty vertex set, so 3-edge-connectivity gives
boundary size at least three for each part.  Summing the \(k\)
boundaries counts every crossing edge twice, and hence
\[
 2q\geq3k.
\]
Since \(D\leq\sum_e d_e=3\),
\[
 2q-D\geq3k-3=3(k-1).
\]
The case \(k=1\) is empty.  The packing theorem now supplies three
edge-disjoint spanning trees of \(H_m\); projecting the labelled copies
back to \(G\) gives three spanning trees with the prescribed
multiplicities. \(\square\)

This proves, in particular, that every star type-A pattern and every
adjacent type-B pattern mentioned below is feasible.  In fact adjacency
is unnecessary for feasibility.

For comparison, the cographic matroid-cover argument in the project
note is also correct.  A cotree is a basis of \(M(G)^*\), and empty
common tree intersection is the same as coverage of \(E(G)\) by three
cographic bases.  For connected \(G\),
\[
 r_{M(G)^*}(A)=|A|+1-c(G-A).
\]
The matroid partition criterion \(|A|\leq3r(A)\) is therefore precisely
\[
 3(c(G-A)-1)\leq2|A|.
\]
The boundary-sum proof in the project note establishes this condition
for every 3-edge-connected graph.  This is a matroid-cover proof; the
prescribed-multiplicity lemma above is the direct place to invoke the
Nash-Williams--Tutte tree-packing theorem.

## 4. Forced local flow values in the special fibres

Write \(F_i=F(T_i)\) for the fundamental completion and encode an edge
by
\[
 \phi(e)=
 1_{e\in F_1}+2\,1_{e\in F_2}+4\,1_{e\in F_3}
 \in\mathbb F_2^3-\{0\}.
\]
Since \(F_i\) contains every edge outside \(T_i\), an edge of tree
multiplicity zero has value \(111\), while an edge of tree multiplicity
one has Hamming weight at least two.  At a cubic vertex the three
nonzero incident flow values are distinct and xor to zero.

For a type-A star, the three defect edges at the centre have
multiplicity one.  Each tree must use exactly one of them.  After
permuting coordinates, their values are forced to be
\[
 110,\quad101,\quad011.
\]

For an adjacent type-B fibre, let the zero edge be \(uv\) and let the
multiplicity-one edge share \(u\).  The zero edge has value \(111\).
The multiplicity-one edge cannot also have value \(111\) at \(u\), so
its value is its forced weight-two cotree-membership vector.  The third
edge at \(u\) has the complementary weight-one value.

These observations make two tempting sufficient targets:

* in a star fibre, choose a representative with \(111\) absent;
* in an adjacent type-B fibre, choose one in which \(111\) occurs only
  on the zero edge.

If a nonzero flow value occurs at most once, at least three edges of the
corresponding four-edge perfect matching of \(K_8\) are absent from the
coordinate co-occurrence graph.  Colouring the endpoints of those three
absent pairs alike and giving the remaining two points singleton
colours gives a literal five-colouring.  Thus either target is
sufficient for a good Oum/Jaeger representative.

## 7. What remains

Because the prescribed-fibre lemma proves every star and adjacent type-B
pattern feasible, either universal assertion

* every type-A star fibre contains a good representative, or
* every adjacent type-B fibre contains a good representative

would, by itself, prove FiveCDC through the already established
Jaeger/Oum reduction.  Neither assertion has been proved here, and no
countermodel to either good-representative assertion was found.  The
Petersen calculation only rules out the simplest proposed proof of the
star assertion, namely globally eliminating the locally absent value
\(111\).  The 34-vertex strong snark rules out the broader strategy of
forcing any Fano direction to occur at most once.
