## 1. Typed rooted-cap states

Let `A` be a loopless cubic graph with a distinguished vertex `z`, whose
three incident edges are called ports `a,b,c`, and a proper root edge `r`
not incident with `z`.  Let

\[
 q:E(A)\longrightarrow D_5=\binom{[5]}2
\]

be a `D5`-flow.  For a coordinate pair `P`, put

\[
 Y_P(q)=\{e:|q(e)\cap P|=1\}.
\]

Every nonempty component of `Y_P` is a circuit.  Suppose one such circuit
contains `r` and exactly the two ports `a,b`.  The third port `c` is
inactive for `P`.  Since both `P` and `q(c)` have size two, inactivity
means exactly one of

\[
                         P=q(c),\qquad P\cap q(c)=\varnothing.       \tag{1}
\]

Call these the **internal** and **external** modes, respectively.  The
typed rooted-cap signature `T(A,z,r)` is the subset of the six states

\[
 \{ab,ac,bc\}\times\{\mathsf{in},\mathsf{out}\}                 \tag{2}
\]

realized by some `D5`-flow and some factor component through `r`.

The mode has a coordinate-free meaning.  At `z`, the three port labels
are the sides of a coordinate triangle.  If they are normalized, in
physical order, to

\[
                         q(a)=01,\quad q(b)=02,\quad q(c)=12,       \tag{3}
\]

then the factors using ports `a,b` are precisely

\[
                            P\in\{12,03,04\}.                     \tag{4}
\]

The first is internal and the last two are external.  The two external
choices are exchanged by the unused-coordinate swap `3 <-> 4`, which
fixes the ordered connector triangle (3).

## 2. Exact gluing lemma

Cut a nontrivial three-edge cut of a cubic graph and cap each shore by a
new cubic vertex.  Let the proper shore roots be `r` and `s`, and identify
the three physical ports across the cut.

> **Typed-port gluing lemma.**  If the two rooted cap signatures have a
> common typed state from (2), then the glued graph has a `D5`-flow in
> which one factor component contains both `r` and `s`.

### Proof

Choose witnesses for the common physical port pair and common mode.
Apply a coordinate permutation to the first witness so its ordered port
triangle is (3), and one to the second witness so its ordered port
triangle is identical.  In internal mode the factor pair is uniquely the
inactive connector label, hence it agrees on both shores.  In external
mode it is one of the last two pairs in (4); if the two witnesses chose
different ones, apply `3 <-> 4` on the whole second shore.  This fixes all
three connector labels and makes the two factor pairs agree.

Delete the two cap vertices and glue corresponding semiedges.  Equal
connector labels make the `D5` vertex equations agree across the cut.
On either shore, deleting the cap vertex turns the displayed factor
circuit into a factor path through its root with endpoints at the same
two physical ports.  Gluing the two paths produces one factor circuit
through both roots.  This proves the lemma.  `square`

Call a subset of (2) a **double star** if, for some one physical port,
both modes occur on each of the two port pairs incident with it.  Two
double stars on a three-element port set have a common physical pair,
and both contain both modes on that pair.  Therefore:

> **Double-star corollary.**  Two rooted caps whose exact typed signatures
> each contain a double star always glue root-good.

There is also a useful one-sided form.  If one signature contains a
double star and the other signature merely covers all three physical
ports, the two underlying pair sets (each of size at least two in the
triangle) intersect.  The double-star side offers both modes on the
common pair, so the signatures again have a common typed state.

## 3. Complete finite theorem through order 14

The accompanying C++ program canonically generates every `D5`-flow
modulo global `S5` on every biconnected simple cubic graph generated by

```text
geng -Cq -d3 -D3 n
```

for even `n=4,6,...,14`.  For every vertex `z` and every edge `r` not
incident with `z`, it computes the exact union (2) over all flows and all
ten factors.  It then checks the double-star predicate directly.

The six mask bits are ordered

```text
ab-in, ab-out, ac-in, ac-out, bc-in, bc-out.
```

Across all 587 graphs and 138,144 rooted interfaces, every exact
signature contains a double star.  Only the seven masks

```text
15, 47, 51, 59, 60, 62, 63
```

occur once order ten is reached.  The three inclusion-minimal masks
`15,51,60` are exactly the three possible four-state double stars; the
other four masks contain one.

The Python replay independently recomputes all flows and all rooted

## 4. Exact limitation

The universal double-star statement is **not proved**.  It is stronger
than mere port coverage.  A hypothetical rooted cap might cover all three
ports with only two typed states of incompatible modes; the elementary
set argument alone does not exclude that possibility.

Consequently this package does not prove universal three-cut
reducibility, the rooted-factor premise, or FiveCDC.  It identifies the
precise six-state obstruction and excludes it completely only through
order 14.
