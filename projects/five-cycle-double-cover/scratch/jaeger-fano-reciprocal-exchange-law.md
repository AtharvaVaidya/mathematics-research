# Exact seven-plane defect law for a reciprocal tree exchange

Date: 2026-07-28

Status: **HUMAN PROOF OF AN EXACT REFORMULATION AND UPDATE LAW.  THIS
DOES NOT PROVE THE EXISTENCE OF A DESCENDING EXCHANGE AND DOES NOT
RESOLVE FIVECDC.**

## 1. Setup

Let \(G\) be a finite loopless cubic graph.  Let
\((T_0,T_1,T_2)\) be spanning trees with empty common intersection, and
let \(K_i\subseteq T_i\) be the unique edge set having odd degree at
every vertex.  With \(e_0,e_1,e_2\) the standard basis of
\(W=\mathbb F_2^3\), put
\[
 \phi(x)=\sum_{i:x\notin K_i}e_i.                            \tag{1}
\]
The complement of each \(K_i\) is Eulerian, so \(\phi\) is a
\(W\)-flow.  It is nowhere zero because \(K_i\subseteq T_i\) and the
three trees have empty common intersection.

For \(0\ne h\in W^*\), write
\[
 E_h=\{x:h(\phi(x))=0\}.                                    \tag{2}
\]
At a cubic vertex the three incident nonzero flow values are distinct
and sum to zero.  They therefore form the three nonzero points of a
two-dimensional subspace.  Consequently every vertex has degree one or
three in \(E_h\).

## 2. A cut-colour formula for the component defect

Fix \(w\in W\) with \(h(w)=1\).  If \(Q\) is a component of \(E_h\),
define
\[
 \beta_{h,w}(Q)=
 |\delta(Q)\cap\phi^{-1}(w)|\pmod2.                          \tag{3}
\]

> **Lemma 2.1.**  The value in (3) is independent of the choice of
> \(w\) in the four-point affine plane \(h^{-1}(1)\).  It is exactly the
> leaf-parity defect used in the five-point component criterion.

**Proof.**
Every edge of \(\delta(Q)\) has its value in
\(A=h^{-1}(1)\).  The flow cut equation gives
\[
                         \sum_{x\in\delta(Q)}\phi(x)=0.       \tag{4}
\]
Reduce the four colour multiplicities modulo two.  The four columns in
\(A\) span \(W\), and their sum is zero.  Hence the kernel of the
resulting \(3\times4\) matrix is the one-dimensional space generated by
the all-ones vector.  The four boundary-colour parities are therefore
equal.

For the second statement, let \(n_v\in W^*\) be the normal of the
incident flow plane at \(v\).  Extend the usual leaf charge to all
vertices by
\[
                         q_{h,w}(v)=1+n_v(w).                \tag{5}
\]
If \(v\) has degree three in \(E_h\), then \(n_v=h\), so (5) is zero.
If \(v\) has degree one, its two non-\(E_h\) incident colours contribute
one occurrence of \(w\) precisely when their incident plane contains
\(w\), equivalently when \(n_v(w)=0\).  Thus (5) is the local parity of
the \(w\)-coloured boundary edges.  Edges with both ends in \(Q\) are
counted twice and cancel.  Summing (5) over \(Q\) gives (3), which is
the component criterion's leaf xor. \(\square\)

There is a useful immediate consequence.  Contract the components of
\(E_h\), retaining the edges outside \(E_h\).  For any one colour
\(w\in h^{-1}(1)\), \(\beta_h\) is the odd-degree vertex set of the
\(w\)-coloured quotient subgraph.  Therefore
\[
             d_h=|\{Q:\beta_h(Q)=1\}|\quad\hbox{is even}.     \tag{6}
\]

Equivalently, choose \(g,\ell\in W^*\) such that
\((g,\ell,h)\) is a basis of \(W^*\).  There is a unique \(w\) with
\[
                  g(w)=\ell(w)=0,\qquad h(w)=1,
\]
and Lemma 2.1 becomes
\[
       \beta_h(Q)=|\delta(Q)\cap E_g\cap E_\ell|\pmod2.       \tag{7}
\]

The flow-level component condition itself is independently Theorem
3.16 of Radek Hušek and Robert Šámal, *Exponentially Many Circuit Double
Covers*, [arXiv:2607.24724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24724); their
Conjecture 3.19 asks for a flow satisfying it.  The contribution pursued
here is the Jaeger star/tree selection and its reciprocal-exchange law,
not priority for that criterion.

## 3. The fundamental-cycle switch

Make a legal reciprocal exchange between coordinates \(i\ne j\):
\[
 T_i'=T_i-b+a,\qquad T_j'=T_j-a+b,                           \tag{8}
\]
where \(a\notin T_i\), \(b\notin T_j\), and both displayed sets are
spanning trees.  Let \(C_i\) be the fundamental cycle in \(T_i+a\) and
\(C_j\) the fundamental cycle in \(T_j+b\).  Legality implies
\(b\in C_i\) and \(a\in C_j\).  Define
\[
 Z_i=\begin{cases}C_i,&b\in K_i,\\ \varnothing,&b\notin K_i,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 Z_j=\begin{cases}C_j,&a\in K_j,\\ \varnothing,&a\notin K_j.\end{cases} \tag{9}
\]

> **Lemma 3.1 (odd-kernel exchange law).**
> \[
> K_i'=K_i\mathbin\triangle Z_i,\qquad
> K_j'=K_j\mathbin\triangle Z_j,                             \tag{10}
> \]
> and the third odd kernel is unchanged.

**Proof.**
If \(b\notin K_i\), the old \(K_i\) is still a subset of \(T_i'\) and
still has odd degree at every vertex, so uniqueness gives
\(K_i'=K_i\).  If \(b\in K_i\), then \(K_i\triangle C_i\) excludes
\(b\), includes \(a\), and is a subset of \(T_i'\).  Toggling a cycle
does not change degree parity, so uniqueness gives the first formula.
The proof for \(j\) is identical. \(\square\)

It follows directly from (1) that
\[
 \phi'(x)=\phi(x)
       +{\bf1}_{Z_i}(x)e_i+{\bf1}_{Z_j}(x)e_j.               \tag{11}
\]
For every \(f\in W^*\), define
\[
 Z_f=f(e_i)Z_i\mathbin\triangle f(e_j)Z_j.                   \tag{12}
\]
Taking \(f\) of (11) gives the exact topology update
\[
                         E_f'=E_f\mathbin\triangle Z_f.       \tag{13}
\]

If both switches in (9) are active, the seven nonzero functionals split
as follows:

```text
1 functional : Z_f = empty
2 functionals: Z_f = Z_i
2 functionals: Z_f = Z_j
2 functionals: Z_f = Z_i triangle Z_j.
```

If exactly one switch is active, three plane subgraphs are unchanged
and four are toggled by its fundamental cycle.  If neither is active,
the complete flow and all seven defect vectors are unchanged even
though the tree packing may change.

## 4. Exact component-vector update

Fix \(h\ne0\), and choose \(g,\ell\) as in (7).  By (13), the new
components are exactly
\[
                  \operatorname{comp}(E_h\triangle Z_h).    \tag{14}
\]
For every such new component \(Q'\), equations (7) and (13) give
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \beta_h'(Q')=
 |\delta(Q')\cap(E_g\triangle Z_g)
                  \cap(E_\ell\triangle Z_\ell)|\pmod2.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{15}
\]
This is the exact update for all seven component-parity vectors,
including exchanges that split or merge old components.

For a more explicit boundary formula, put
\[
 R_{h;g,\ell}=
   (Z_g\cap E_\ell)
   \mathbin\triangle(E_g\cap Z_\ell)
   \mathbin\triangle(Z_g\cap Z_\ell).                        \tag{16}
\]
Expanding the two characteristic functions in (15) over \(\mathbb F_2\)
gives, for every vertex set \(S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &|\delta(S)\cap(E_g\triangle Z_g)
                    \cap(E_\ell\triangle Z_\ell)|\\
 &\qquad =
 |\delta(S)\cap E_g\cap E_\ell|
 +|\delta(S)\cap R_{h;g,\ell}|\pmod2.                        \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]

When \(Z_h=\varnothing\), the old and new component partitions agree.
Let \(\partial_h R\) denote the boundary-incidence vector
\[
              (\partial_hR)(Q)=|\delta(Q)\cap R|\pmod2.
\]
Then (17) specializes to the literal vector equation
\[
                    \beta_h'=\beta_h+\partial_hR_{h;g,\ell}. \tag{18}
\]
Writing \(\gamma=\partial_hR_{h;g,\ell}\), the ordinary integer defect
count changes by
\[
 d_h'-d_h
   =|\operatorname{supp}\gamma|
    -2|\operatorname{supp}\gamma
          \cap\operatorname{supp}\beta_h|.                   \tag{19}
\]
Thus, when the plane subgraph is unchanged, descent is exactly the
statement that the boundary toggle \(\gamma\) hits more currently bad
components than good ones.

For example, if \(h=e_k^*\) and the exchange is between the other two
coordinates \(i,j\), take \(g=e_i^*\), \(\ell=e_j^*\).  Then
\[
 E_g=K_i,\qquad E_\ell=K_j,\qquad Z_h=\varnothing,
\]
and (16) becomes
\[
 R=(Z_i\cap K_j)
    \mathbin\triangle(K_i\cap Z_j)
    \mathbin\triangle(Z_i\cap Z_j).                          \tag{20}
\]

## 5. A local vertex form

There is also an exact formula that handles the split/merge case without
set expansion.  Let \(a,b,a+b\) be the old incident colours at a vertex.
Using the determinant volume form on \(W\), its plane normal is
\[
                         n_v=a\times b,
\]
where \((a\times b)(x)=\det(a,b,x)\).  Suppose the exchange perturbations
on the first two incident edges are \(r,s\); the third is \(r+s\)
because (11) is a flow.  Bilinearity gives
\[
 n_v'-n_v=a\times s+r\times b+r\times s.                    \tag{21}
\]
Consequently the charge (5) changes by
\[
 q_{h,w}'(v)-q_{h,w}(v)
   =\det(a,s,w)+\det(r,b,w)+\det(r,s,w).                     \tag{22}
\]
If \(P_h\) and \(P_h'\) are the component-versus-vertex incidence
matrices of \(E_h\) and \(E_h'\), respectively, then the entire update
can be written
\[
       \beta_h=P_hq_{h,w},\qquad
       \beta_h'=P_h'(q_{h,w}+\Delta q_{h,w}).                \tag{23}
\]
Equations (13), (22), and (23) are a local reconstruction of every new
component defect.

## 6. What the law does not supply

Equation (19) turns an unchanged-plane descent question into a precise
cut-bias question, but it does not guarantee a favourable legal
exchange.  The fixed-coordinate order-14 countermodel in
`jaeger-star-parity-descent-countermodel.md` shows that all available
toggles for one selected coordinate may instead increase its defect.

More strongly, the positive order-16 state in
`jaeger-fano-min-immediate-descent-countermodel.md` has no incident
exchange lowering \(d_{\min}\).  A kernel-inert neutral exchange first
changes the available fundamental cycles, after which a second exchange
reaches \(d_{\min}=0\).  Therefore a universal symmetric-descent proof,
if true, must include neutral plateau motion or an equivalent global
argument; one-exchange averaging is insufficient.

Fixed-kernel motion alone is not enough either.  A second state in the
same order-16 fibre, frozen in
`jaeger-fano-min-immediate-descent-countermodel.md`, has
\(d_{\min}=2\), no descending incident exchange, and no kernel-inert
incident exchange at all.  Its fixed-kernel realization component is
therefore a singleton.  An **active** same-level exchange changes the
kernel triple, after which a second exchange descends to zero.

The precise remaining statement is consequently the full same-level
component theorem tested through order 14: every connected component of
every positive \(d_{\min}\)-level has an edge to a lower level.  The
exchange law proves no such boundary must exist.  Both inactive and
active neutral moves may be essential, so neither one-exchange averaging
nor an exposure theorem confined to one ordered kernel triple can prove
the surviving claim.
