# Zero dual price on the rainbow quotient cut

Date: 2026-07-29

Status: **EXACT STRUCTURAL THEOREM / NOT A FIVE-CYCLE-DOUBLE-COVER
RESOLUTION**.

## 1. Setup

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite loopless cubic graph.  Let
\[
                         f=(h,s)
\]
be a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_2^2\)-flow, and identify
the support of the first coordinate with its edge set \(h\).  Assume that
\(h\) has minimum cardinality among all extendable first-coordinate
supports.  Put \(K=\mathbb F_2^2\), and for \(c\in K\) define
\[
 M_c=\{e\in h:s(e)=c\},\qquad T_c=\partial M_c,\qquad
 J_c=h-M_c.                                                \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\partial A\) is the set of vertices incident with an odd number
of edges of \(A\).

The four sets \(M_c\) partition \(h\), and every \(M_c\) is a matching.
The minimum-projection exchange theorem says that \(J_c\) is a
minimum-cardinality \(T_c\)-join in \(G-M_c\), simultaneously for all
four \(c\).

Let \({\cal W}\) be the component partition of the spanning subgraph
\((V,E-h)\).  A **quotient shore** is a union of members of
\({\cal W}\).

## 2. The four explicit primal and dual programs

For \(S\subseteq V\), write
\[
 \delta_c(S)=\delta_G(S)-M_c,\qquad
 \theta_c(S)=|S\cap T_c|\pmod2.
\]
Since \(T_c=\partial M_c\), the cut-parity identity gives
\[
                    \theta_c(S)=|\delta_G(S)\cap M_c|\pmod2. \tag{2}
\]

The standard minimum \(T_c\)-join program in \(G-M_c\) is
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \text{minimize}&\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E-M_c}x_e\\[2mm]
 \text{subject to}
   &\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\delta_c(S)}x_e\geq1
       \quad(S\subseteq V,\ \theta_c(S)=1),\\
   &x_e\geq0\quad(e\in E-M_c).
\end{array}                                                \tag{P_c}
\]
The Edmonds--Johnson \(T\)-join polyhedron theorem makes
\(\chi_{J_c}\) an optimal integral solution.  Its ordinary LP dual is
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \text{maximize}&\displaystyle
       \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq V\\\theta_c(S)=1}}y_{c,S}\\[2mm]
 \text{subject to}
   &\displaystyle
       \sum_{\substack{S:\ e\in\delta_G(S)\\\theta_c(S)=1}}
          y_{c,S}\leq1\quad(e\in E-M_c),\\
   &y_{c,S}\geq0.
\end{array}                                                \tag{D_c}
\]
One may identify \(S\) with \(V-S\) to remove duplicate cut variables.
No integrality of the dual variables is asserted or needed.

Strong duality and (1) give
\[
                    \max(D_c)=\min(P_c)=|J_c|=|h|-|M_c|.    \tag{3}
\]
For an optimal dual define the load on \(e\in E-M_c\) by
\[
 \ell_c(e)=
       \sum_{\substack{S:\ e\in\delta_G(S)\\\theta_c(S)=1}}y_{c,S}.
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Complementary slackness with the displayed optimum \(\chi_{J_c}\)
gives the two implications
\[
\begin{split}
 e\in J_c&\Longrightarrow \ell_c(e)=1,\\
 y_{c,S}>0&\Longrightarrow |J_c\cap\delta_G(S)|=1.          \tag{5}
\end{split}
\]
In particular, if optimal duals are chosen for all four colours, every
support edge \(e\), whose unique low colour is \(r_e\), is tight in the
three systems \(c\ne r_e\).  Thus its aggregate four-dual load is
exactly three.  Also
\[
                         \sum_{c\in K}\max(D_c)=3|h|.       \tag{6}
\]

## 3. Quotient-cut zero-price theorem

> **Theorem.** Let \(S\) be a quotient shore.  For every \(c\in K\),
> every optimal solution of \((D_c)\) has
> \[
>                              y_{c,S}=0.                   \tag{7}
> \]
> Consequently every positive cut-dual variable splits the interior of
> at least one component of \(G-h\).

**Proof.**  Because \(S\) is a union of components of \(G-h\), every
edge of \(\delta_G(S)\) belongs to \(h\).  Flow conservation across the
cut says
\[
 \sum_{e\in\delta_G(S)}(1,s(e))=0.
\]
Writing
\[
                  n_d=|\delta_G(S)\cap M_d|\pmod2
\]
for \(d\in K\), this is
\[
                       \sum_dn_d=0,\qquad\sum_dn_dd=0.      \tag{8}
\]
The \(3\times4\) binary matrix in (8) has kernel generated by the
all-ones vector.  Hence the four bits \(n_d\) are equal.

If they are all zero, then (2) gives \(\theta_c(S)=0\), so \(S\) is not
even an indexed variable of \((D_c)\).

If they are all one, then \(S\) is \(T_c\)-odd.  Moreover
\[
\begin{split}
 |J_c\cap\delta_G(S)|
 &=|(\delta_G(S)\cap h)-M_c|\\
 &=\sum_{d\ne c}|\delta_G(S)\cap M_d|\geq3,                \tag{9}
\end{split}
\]
because each of the other three summands is a positive odd integer.
But (5) says a positive \(y_{c,S}\) would force the same quantity to
equal one.  Therefore \(y_{c,S}=0\).  This proves (7) in both cases.

Finally a vertex shore which does not split any component of \(G-h\)
is exactly a quotient shore.  Hence every positive dual shore splits
at least one complement component. \(\square\)

This conclusion is stronger than merely saying that the particular
rainbow component has zero price: **all** cuts visible after contracting
the components of \(G-h\) have zero price in all four optimal duals.

## 4. A support-density consequence

The forced interior dual mass gives a numerical restriction on every
globally minimum extendable projection.

> **Corollary.** For every \(c\in K\),
> \[
>                         |E-h|\geq |J_c|=|h|-|M_c|.        \tag{10}
> \]
> Consequently
> \[
>                         |E-h|\geq\frac34|h|.              \tag{11}
> \]
> If \(G\) is cubic, then
> \[
>                              |h|\leq\frac67|V|.           \tag{12}
> \]

**Proof.**  Fix an optimal dual for colour \(c\).  Every shore with
positive coefficient splits a component of \(G-h\), so at least one
edge of \(E-h\) crosses it.  By (5), exactly one edge of \(J_c\) crosses
it.  Hence every positive dual cut has at least two edges in
\(G-M_c\).  Double-counting dual cut-edge incidences gives
\[
\begin{split}
 2|J_c|
 &=2\sum_Sy_{c,S}\\
 &\leq\sum_Sy_{c,S}|\delta_c(S)|\\
 &=\sum_{e\in E-M_c}\ell_c(e)\\
 &\leq |J_c|+|E-h|.                                      \tag{13}
\end{split}
\]
The last line uses tightness on the \(|J_c|\) support edges and unit
capacity on every complement edge.  Rearranging proves (10).

Some colour class has size at most \(|h|/4\), so (10) for that colour
gives (11).  In a cubic graph \(|E|=3|V|/2\).  Substituting
\(|E-h|=3|V|/2-|h|\) into (11) gives \(7|h|/4\leq3|V|/2\),
which is (12). \(\square\)

For example, a counterexample to the minimum-projection selection
principle with the already-proved lower bound \(|h|\geq16\) must have
at least \(19\) vertices, hence at least \(20\) vertices in the cubic
case.  This is only a density restriction, not a universal size bound.

## 5. Exact six-map recomputation law

We now record precisely what a witness-neutralizing switch does to the
four programs.  This also explains why the theorem is a genuine no-go
for the most direct dual potential.

Let \(Y\) be the quotient shore selected by a dual inconsistency witness
for affine cleaning.  On each oriented support circuit let
\(\epsilon_i=1\) when its \(i\)-th vertex lies in \(Y\), let \(g_i\) be
the transformed complement derivative there, and choose cyclic
potentials \(p_i\in K\) satisfying
\[
                         p_{i-1}+p_i=\epsilon_i g_i.        \tag{14}
\]
The witness balance is exactly the condition that (14) closes.  If
\(r_i\) is the old low colour on the \(i\)-th support edge, then
postcomposing the component maps in \(Y\) by
\(U\in\operatorname{GL}(2,2)\) admits the integrated word
\[
                         r_i^U=r_i+(U+I)p_i.                \tag{15}
\]

For every shore \(S\) and colour \(c\), the recomputed terminal parity
and deleted capacity set are therefore
\[
\begin{split}
 \theta_c^U(S)
   &=\bigoplus_{e\in\delta_G(S)\cap h}
           {\bf1}_{\,r_e+(U+I)p_e=c},\\
 M_c^U&=\{e\in h:r_e+(U+I)p_e=c\}.                         \tag{16}
\end{split}
\]
The post-switch dual \(D_c(U)\) is obtained from \((D_c)\) by retaining
exactly the shores with \(\theta_c^U(S)=1\), deleting the edge
capacities in \(M_c^U\), and leaving every other capacity equal to one.
This is the full exact transformation; in general it requires a new
optimal dual rather than a transport of the old coefficients.

The selected shore \(Y\) has all four cut-colour parities equal.  Before
neutralization their common value is the obstruction bit
\(F_Y(I)=1\), so \(Y\) indexes a variable in all four old duals.  The
zero-price theorem gives
\[
                           y_{c,Y}=0\qquad(c\in K).          \tag{17}
\]
The six-map identity supplies a legal \(U\) with \(F_Y(U)=0\).
Then \(Y\) is \(T_c^U\)-even for all four colours and indexes no variable
in any \(D_c(U)\).  Thus neutralization removes four variables whose
coefficients were already forced to zero.  No objective decrease follows
from the selected cut.

There is also a useful pointwise orbit formula.  Let
\[
 A_0=\{I,\rho,\rho^2\},\qquad
 A_1=\{\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3\}
\]
be the two three-element cosets in
\(\operatorname{GL}(2,2)\).  For either \(A=A_0\) or \(A_1\),
\[
 \{r_i^U:U\in A\}=
 \begin{cases}
   \{r_i,r_i,r_i\},&p_i=0,\\
   K-\{r_i+p_i\},&p_i\ne0,
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{18}
\]
as multisets.  Indeed, for \(p_i\ne0\), the values \(Up_i\) run once
through the three nonzero elements of \(K\) in either coset.

Put
\[
\begin{split}
 a_c&=|\{i:p_i=0,\ r_i=c\}|,\\
 b_c&=|\{i:p_i\ne0,\ r_i+p_i=c\}|,\qquad
 m=|\{i:p_i\ne0\}|.
\end{split}
\]
Summing (14) gives the exact class-size and optimum-value laws
\[
\begin{split}
 \sum_{U\in A}|M_c^U|&=3a_c+m-b_c,\\
 \sum_{U\in A}\max D_c(U)
   &=3|h|-3a_c-m+b_c.                                     \tag{19}
\end{split}
\]
For each individual \(U\),
\[
                  \sum_{c\in K}\max D_c(U)=3|h|,           \tag{20}
\]
because the four \(M_c^U\)'s partition \(h\).  Hence neither the
four-colour aggregate dual objective nor the selected quotient-cut
coefficient supplies a decreasing potential.

## 6. What remains

The theorem does not show that shortest-join duality is useless.  It
shows exactly where its usable information must live: in shores that cut
through the complement components.  Those shores encode internal
distance, path-pairing, or blossom information.  A successful dynamic
descent would have to relate that interior information across a
componentwise \(\operatorname{GL}(2,2)\) or Kempe switch.

No such universal transport or descent is proved here.  In particular,
this package does not prove the minimum-projection selection conjecture
and does not prove or disprove FiveCDC.
