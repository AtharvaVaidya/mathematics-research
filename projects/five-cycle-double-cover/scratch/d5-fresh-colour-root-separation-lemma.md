# Fresh-colour separation in a Tait-supported \(D_5\) state

Date: **2026-07-28**

Status: **HUMAN-CHECKABLE LEMMA / NOT A FIVECDC RESOLUTION**.

## Statement

Let \(G\) be cubic and let
\[
q:E(G)\longrightarrow \binom{[5]}2
\]
satisfy xor zero at every vertex.  Suppose that all labels of \(q\)
use only three coordinates \(A=\{a,b,c\}\).  For an edge pair
\(r,s\), suppose that no component of any
\[
Y_{ij}=C_i\mathbin\triangle C_j,\qquad
C_i=\{e:i\in q(e)\},
\]
contains both \(r\) and \(s\).

Then at most two Kempe switches, each preserving
\[
                    \Phi(q)=\sum_{i=0}^4\kappa(C_i),
\]
produce a state in which the labels of \(r\) and \(s\) are disjoint.
Here \(\kappa\) counts nonempty components.

More precisely:

- if \(q(r)\ne q(s)\), one \(\Phi\)-neutral switch suffices;
- if \(q(r)=q(s)\), two \(\Phi\)-neutral switches suffice.

Since the surface Euler characteristic is
\(\chi(S(q))=\Phi(q)-|V(G)|/2\), the switches are also
surface-Euler-neutral.

## Proof

First record the fresh-colour operation.  Let \(x\) be a used
coordinate and let \(d\) be unused, so \(C_d=\varnothing\).  Then
\[
                         Y_{xd}=C_x.
\]
If \(K\) is one component of \(C_x\), it is therefore a legal
\(Y_{xd}\)-component on which to switch \(x,d\).  The switch gives
\[
 C'_x=C_x\setminus K,\qquad C'_d=K,\qquad C'_z=C_z
 \quad(z\notin\{x,d\}).
\]
If \(C_x\) has another component, this replaces the component counts
\[
                    (\kappa(C_x),0)
       \longmapsto (\kappa(C_x)-1,1),
\]
so \(\Phi(q')=\Phi(q)\).

Now \(q(r)\) and \(q(s)\) are two 2-subsets of the same 3-set, hence
they have a common coordinate \(x\).

In a three-coordinate state, if
\(\{y,z\}=A\setminus\{x\}\), the edgewise identity
\[
                            Y_{yz}=C_x                         \tag{1}
\]
holds: the labels containing exactly one of \(y,z\) are precisely
\(xy,xz\), which are precisely the labels containing \(x\).
Both roots lie in \(C_x\).  By the root-bad hypothesis and (1), they
lie in distinct components of \(C_x\).  Let \(K\) be the component
containing \(r\), and let \(d\notin A\).  Apply the fresh-colour
switch \(x,d\) on \(K\).  It is neutral because the component
containing \(s\) remains in \(C_x\).

If \(q(r)\ne q(s)\), write
\[
                           q(r)=xy,\qquad q(s)=xz.
\]
After the switch,
\[
                           q'(r)=dy,\qquad q'(s)=xz,
\]
and these labels are disjoint.  This proves the one-switch case.

If \(q(r)=q(s)=xy\), the first switch gives
\[
                           q'(r)=dy,\qquad q'(s)=xy.
\]
The switch did not change \(C_y\).  Applying (1) with \(y\) in place
of \(x\), root-badness in the original state says that \(r,s\) lie in
distinct components of \(C_y\).  Let \(L\) be the component containing
\(r\), and let \(e\) be the other coordinate outside \(A\), so
\(C'_e=\varnothing\).  Switch \(y,e\) on \(L\).  Again another
component, the one containing \(s\), remains in \(C_y\), so the switch
is neutral.  Finally
\[
                          q''(r)=de,\qquad q''(s)=xy,
\]
which are disjoint.  This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

## What this proves—and what it does not

This gives a proof-grade source of equal-Euler moves in every
root-bad Tait-supported state.  In particular, such a state cannot be
an isolated terminal Euler plateau for that root pair.  It also
explains the decisive middle move in the sharp order-14 path:
\[
                    (\kappa(C_0),\kappa(C_3))=(2,0)
                    \longmapsto (1,1).
\]

The lemma does **not** yet show that the disjointly labelled roots lie
on one factor circuit.  That is a global connectivity assertion and
is exactly where multiple neutral surgeries may still be required.
