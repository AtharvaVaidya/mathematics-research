# Human statement and consequence

For every retained triangle-free simple 3-connected non-Tait cubic graph
\(H\) of order \(20\), \(22\), or \(24\), every vertex \(z\), and every
proper root edge \(r\) of the three-pole \(H-z\), normalize the three
connectors to

\[
                         (01,02,12).
\]

If \(R(H,z,r)\) is the set of root labels occurring in fixed-five
\(D_5\)-labellings with that normalized boundary, then

\[
R(H,z,r)\supseteq
\{12,03,04\},\quad
\{02,13,14\},\quad\text{or}\quad
\{01,23,24\}.
\]

The finite domain contains 13,901 caps, 330,790 deleted-vertex cores, and
10,824,084 roots.  Both exact implementations classify every root as
nonempty and base-pair closed.

The path-rooted triangle-induction theorem proves that an exposed
root-end triangle directly supplies a base pair, while contracting an
exposed connector-end triangle carries any fork triple to another fork
triple.  Therefore the verified triangle-free premise implies fork
closure for every path-rooted capped shore of order at most 24.

If a simple exceptional cap has order at most 26, both shores of a
principal cyclic three-cut have order at most 24.  A fork in one shore
restricts a signature having no disjoint cross-pair on the other shore
to at most two labels; the second shore's fork has three distinct
labels.  Hence the mixed exceptional relation is impossible in this
bounded cyclic-three branch.

This is a finite structural theorem.  It does not establish the
unbounded rooted closure theorem, a universal fixed-five signature
theorem, or Five-CDC.
