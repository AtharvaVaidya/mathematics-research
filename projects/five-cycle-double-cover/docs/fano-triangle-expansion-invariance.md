# Fixed-projection cleanability is invariant under triangle expansion

Date: **2026-07-27**.

Status: **HUMAN-CHECKABLE REDUCTION THEOREM**.

## 1. Statement

Let \(H\) be a loopless cubic graph and let \(G\) be obtained by
replacing one vertex \(v\) by a triangle, attaching the three old
incident edges one to each new triangle vertex.  Contraction of the
triangle induces
\[
             \pi:Z_1(G;{\mathbb F}_2)\longrightarrow
                   Z_1(H;{\mathbb F}_2).
\]
Its kernel is generated by the triangle cycle \(T\).

For a binary cycle \(h\), call \(h\) **cleanable** when its exact
two-cycle normal form is feasible, equivalently when there is a
\(D_5\)-flow with one distinguished coordinate present exactly on
\(\operatorname{supp}h\).  Here
\[
 D_5=\left\{\{i,j\}:1\le i<j\le5\right\}
\]
is written as the ten weight-two vectors of the even subspace of
\({\mathbb F}_2^5\).

> **Triangle invariance theorem.**  For every \(h'\in Z_1(G)\),
> \[
>       h'\text{ is cleanable in }G
>       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
>       \pi(h')\text{ is cleanable in }H.               \tag{1}
> \]

Consequently
\[
                  \operatorname{Bad}(G)
       =\pi^{-1}\bigl(\operatorname{Bad}(H)\bigr).       \tag{2}
\]

## 2. The three local extensions

At a cubic vertex, three \(D_5\)-labels with XOR zero are necessarily
\[
                         ab,\quad ac,\quad bc            \tag{3}
\]
for three distinct coordinates \(a,b,c\).  Indeed, the labels are
distinct, every coordinate occurs evenly, and their six total
coordinate occurrences therefore form the three edges of a triangle.

Attach the external labels \(ab,ac,bc\) to triangle vertices
\(v_1,v_2,v_3\), respectively.  Write the internal labels in edge order
\[
                         v_1v_2,\quad v_2v_3,\quad v_3v_1.
\]
Solving the three vertex equations inside \(D_5\) gives exactly
\[
 (bc,ab,ac),\qquad
 (ad,cd,bd),                                             \tag{4}
\]
where in the second pattern \(d\) is either of the two coordinates
outside \(\{a,b,c\}\).  Direct substitution checks conservation, and
the first internal label determines the other two, so the list is
complete.

Fix the distinguished coordinate \(r\).

* If \(r\notin\{a,b,c\}\), the external \(r\)-pattern is \(000\).
  The first extension in (4), or the extension using the other outside
  coordinate, has internal pattern \(000\); choosing \(d=r\) has
  internal pattern \(111\).  These are exactly the two binary-cycle
  lifts through the triangle.
* If \(r\in\{a,b,c\}\), say \(r=a\), the external pattern has two ones.
  In the displayed edge order, the first extension has internal pattern
  \(011\), while either \(d\)-extension has pattern \(100\).  Again
  these are exactly the two parity-compatible binary-cycle lifts.

Thus every \(D_5\)-flow on \(H\) whose distinguished coordinate is
\(\pi(h')\) extends to a \(D_5\)-flow on \(G\) whose distinguished
coordinate is the prescribed lift \(h'\).  This proves the reverse
implication in (1).

Conversely, contract the triangle in any \(D_5\)-flow on \(G\).
Adding its three vertex equations cancels every internal edge twice and
shows that the three external labels sum to zero.  They remain nonzero
members of \(D_5\), so they give a valid \(D_5\)-flow at the contracted
vertex.  The distinguished coordinate contracts from \(h'\) to
\(\pi(h')\).  This proves the forward implication and hence (1)--(2).

## 3. All-seven obstructions cannot first appear in a triangle expansion

Let \(U\le Z_1(G)\) be a three-dimensional cycle subspace whose supports
cover every edge.  Such a \(U\) is exactly a nowhere-zero
\({\mathbb F}_2^3\)-flow written through its seven nonzero functional
projections.

Suppose every nonzero member of \(U\) were bad.

If \(T\notin U\), then \(\pi|_U\) is injective.  The image is a
three-dimensional subspace covering every edge of \(H\), and (2) makes
all seven of its nonzero members bad.  Hence \(H\) already had an
all-seven obstruction.

If \(T\in U\), then \(\pi(U)\) is two-dimensional.  It covers every
edge of \(H\), because \(T\) has no support outside the contracted
triangle.  Therefore \(\pi(U)\) is a nowhere-zero
\({\mathbb F}_2^2\)-flow, or Tait coloring, of \(H\).  The Tait
corollary says every binary projection of \(H\) is cleanable,
contradicting (2) for the nonzero images of members of \(U\).

We have proved:

> **No-first-obstruction corollary.**  If \(H\) has no covering
> three-dimensional all-bad cycle subspace, neither does any graph
> obtained from \(H\) by iterated vertex-to-triangle expansions.

This explains the exact doubling
\[
 7,\ 14,\ 28,\ 56,\ 112
\]
of total bad-projection counts along the small Petersen/Tietze triangle
expansion chain without treating that pattern as empirical induction.
